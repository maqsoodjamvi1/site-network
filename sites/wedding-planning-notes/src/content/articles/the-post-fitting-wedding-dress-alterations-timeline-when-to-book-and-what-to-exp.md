When I picked up my wedding dress from the boutique, the salesperson handed me a white canvas bag and said, "Now you just need to find a tailor." That was the moment I realized buying the gown was only half the battle. If you are standing in your living room right now wondering when on earth you need to drop your dress off so it fits right on the big day, you are not alone. 

Here is the exact timeline and step-by-step reality of what happens during the wedding dress alteration process, based on what actually works in the real world.

## The Golden Rule: Start Two to Three Months Out

The biggest mistake I see brides make—and the one I almost made myself—is waiting until a month before the wedding to book an appointment. Good tailors who specialize in bridal wear book out months in advance, especially during peak spring and fall wedding seasons. 

You should plan to have your first official fitting about eight to ten weeks before your wedding date. This leaves enough breathing room for two to three rounds of adjustments without inducing a panic attack. If you have lost or gained a noticeable amount of weight, or if your dress requires massive restructuring like rebuilding a corset or altering heavy beadwork, push that first appointment back to the twelve-week mark.

## What to Bring to Your First Fitting

Do not show up to your first appointment empty-handed or wearing the wrong undergarments. Your tailor needs to see the exact foundation garments you plan to wear down the aisle because even a quarter-inch difference in your bra padding changes how the bodice lays. 

Pack these items in a separate bag:
* The exact shoes you will wear on your wedding day, paying close attention to the heel height.
* The shapewear, bustier, or sticky bra you intend to wear (buy these beforehand; do not guess).
* Any special undergarments or petticoats that add volume to the skirt.

During this first appointment, the tailor will pin you into the dress from top to bottom. They will check the bust, take in the side seams, adjust the shoulders, and pin the hem. Be honest during this stage if something feels too tight or pinches when you sit down. A dress can look stunning in a mirror, but if you cannot breathe or raise your arms to dance, it needs adjusting.

## The Waiting Game: Weeks Six Through Four

Once the pins are in, the tailor gets to work. This middle phase usually lasts about three to four weeks. While you are waiting, try to keep your weight and fitness routine relatively stable. Crash dieting or suddenly committing to heavy weightlifting right before your final fittings can throw off weeks of delicate tailoring, particularly around the ribcage and hips.

During this time, the tailor is carefully taking apart the dress—often working from the inside out—stitching, pressing, and reshaping the fabric to match your measurements. 

## The Second Fitting: The Fine-Tuning Phase

About four weeks before the wedding, you will return for your second fitting. Put on your shoes and undergarments again, and slip into the gown. This is where you test out the mobility. 

Walk around the fitting room, sit down on a chair, raise your arms to mimic hugging someone, and dance in a circle. If the hem is still grazing the floor and you are tripping over the tulle, tell the tailor right then. It is much easier to take off another half-inch of fabric now than it is to fix it the week of the wedding. If everything fits like a glove, the tailor will mark the spots for the bustle—the hidden hooks and loops that lift your train off the floor for the reception.

## The Final Pickup: One to Two Weeks Out

Your final appointment usually happens about seven to ten days before the wedding. In most cases, this is just a quick try-on to make sure the final tweaks are perfect, and then you take the dress home. 

By this point, your wedding checklist is a mile long, and picking up a gown might feel like just another errand. However, take fifteen minutes when you get the dress home to hang it securely in a cool, dry place out of reach of pets, and check over the zipper one last time so you can walk into your wedding morning with total peace of mind.

As you map out your final countdown to the altar, sorting out your alteration appointments now will keep your schedule manageable and your stress levels low.
