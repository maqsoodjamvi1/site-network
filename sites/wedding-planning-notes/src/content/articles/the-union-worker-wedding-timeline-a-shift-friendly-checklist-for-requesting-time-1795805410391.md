Planning a wedding while working a union job comes with a very specific set of rules. Unlike corporate or salaried roles where you might just ping your manager on Slack to move a few days around, navigating time off when you work under a Collective Bargaining Agreement (CBA) requires strategy, seniority awareness, and a lot of advance planning. 

When my partner and I started looking at dates for our wedding, I quickly realized that traditional wedding planning timelines didn't account for shift bids, blackout dates, or mandatory overtime lists. If you are clocking in at a manufacturing plant, running transit lines, working construction, or staffing a hospital under a union contract, you have to approach your wedding timeline differently. Here is the shift-friendly checklist I used to lock down my time off without risking my vacation accrual or dealing with last-minute scheduling panic.

## 12 Months Out: Review Your CBA and Shift Bid Schedules

Before you even look at venue availability or put down a deposit, open up your union contract. You need to look specifically at three sections: vacation bidding rules, personal day allocations, and FMLA or bereavement clauses (just in case). 

Every workplace handles time off differently. In some locals, vacation weeks are bid on once a year based strictly on seniority. If your wedding falls during a peak production month or a heavy construction season, someone with more seniority might already have that week locked in. 

Take a look at your employer's shift bid timeline, too. If your department bids on shifts every six months, you need to know exactly when the bidding window opens for the season of your wedding. Do not let a venue lock you into a date before you know if you can actually get off work that weekend. 

## 9 Months Out: Have the Conversation with Your Shop Steward

Long before you fill out formal paperwork, have a quiet conversation with your shop steward or union rep. They know the unwritten rules of the floor or the department that aren't printed in the handbook. 

Ask them practical questions: Is management currently pushing back on vacation approvals due to staffing shortages? Are there mandatory overtime mandates that might override a weekend off? Your steward can give you a realistic read on whether requesting a Friday and Monday off around your wedding date will go smoothly or if it's going to trigger a grievance process. 

This is also the time to look at your overtime bank or compensatory time, if your contract allows it. If you have built up hours that you can trade or cash out for time off, figure out how to convert them before the calendar fills up.

## 6 Months Out: Submit Your Formal Leave Request

Once your venue and date are officially booked, it is time to put your request in writing. Do not rely on a casual verbal agreement with your shift supervisor. Union environments run on paper trails. 

Submit your vacation request form through the official company portal or hand a physical copy to management with a request for a signed and dated receipt. Make sure you cover all your bases:
* The main wedding day (if it falls on a work shift)
* The day before for travel and rehearsal
* The day after for recovery and out-of-town family farewells
* Your scheduled days off immediately adjacent, so you don't get called in on your rest days

If your workplace uses a seniority-based calendar, submit this the exact minute the submission window opens. Do not wait. 

## 3 Months Out: Line Up Shift Swaps and Coverage

Even with approved vacation time, things happen. People get sick, production quotas go up, or management suddenly claims an "emergency operational need." To protect your time off, secure your own backup.

Talk to your trusted coworkers on your shift or in your classification. If your contract allows shift trades, find someone willing to work your shift in exchange for you covering one of theirs later in the year. Getting a peer-to-peer trade approved by management is often much easier than getting a brand-new vacation day approved from scratch, especially if you work in a 24/7 operation where coverage is mandatory.

Write down the agreement clearly and submit the shift-trade paperwork together so there's no confusion when your wedding week arrives.

## 1 Month Out: Double-Check the Schedule and Payroll

Never assume your time off made it from the supervisor's desk into the final payroll and scheduling software. Thirty days out, pull up your upcoming schedule and verify that your name is marked off and your vacation pay is properly coded. 

If you notice you are still scheduled to work, address it immediately with both HR and your shop steward. Fixing a scheduling error a month in advance is a simple administrative fix; fixing it on a Friday afternoon when you're supposed to be tying your bowtie is a nightmare.

Balancing a union job with wedding planning takes extra effort, but getting your ducks in a row early means you can actually enjoy the ceremony without checking your phone for work updates.
