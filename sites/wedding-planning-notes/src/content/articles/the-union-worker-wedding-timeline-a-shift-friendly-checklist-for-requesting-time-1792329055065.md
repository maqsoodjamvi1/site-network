Planning a wedding while working a union job comes with a unique set of challenges. When your schedule is dictated by a collective bargaining agreement, seniority bids, and strict attendance policies, you can't just submit a casual time-off request two weeks before the ceremony. Between the rehearsal dinner, out-of-town travel, the wedding day itself, and the honeymoon, you might need anywhere from a few days to a couple of weeks away from the floor or the site. 

Navigating the union bureaucracy to secure your wedding dates requires a strategic approach. Here is a practical, shift-friendly timeline to help you lock in your time off without risking your standing, your vacation pay, or your sanity.

## 12 to 9 Months Out: Review the CBA and Master the Bidding Schedule

Before you tour a single venue or put down a deposit, open up your Collective Bargaining Agreement (CBA). You need to look specifically at the sections covering vacation accrual, personal days, bereavement leave, and—most importantly—the vacation bidding process. 

Every union shop handles time off differently. In some trades, vacation weeks are bid on annually based on company seniority, usually in the late fall or early spring. If your wedding falls during peak season (like summer or around major holidays), you will be competing with coworkers who have more seniority and want those exact same weeks off. 

Talk to your shop steward immediately. Explain your situation off the record to get the lay of the land. They can tell you if your target wedding week historically gets locked up by senior members, or if there are shift-swapping precedents you can leverage later. Knowing your contractual rights and local shop culture this early prevents you from booking a non-refundable venue on a weekend you legally cannot get off.

## 8 to 6 Months Out: Secure the Dates and Submit the Formal Request

Once your venue and date are locked in, it is time to officially get it on management's radar. Do not wait for the standard two-week notice window. Union environments run on paper trails, and early submission is your best defense against arbitrary denials.

Fill out the official leave request form provided by your employer. Make sure you request not just the wedding day, but the setup day, the day after, and your planned honeymoon travel window. If you work rotating shifts or a continental schedule, map out your exact working days surrounding the event so you aren't caught off guard by a scheduled graveyard shift the night before your rehearsal.

Keep a physical or digital copy of the signed request form with a timestamp. If your supervisor or plant manager sits on the request, having proof of when you submitted it protects you if management later claims you missed a deadline. 

## 4 to 3 Months Out: Line Up Shift Trades and Cover

Even with approved vacation time, unexpected operational needs or peak production schedules can sometimes threaten time-off approvals in union shops. This is where your workplace relationships become your safety net. 

Now is the time to quietly coordinate with reliable coworkers on your crew who might be willing to cover your shifts if a last-minute emergency pops up, or if you need to trade days to balance the schedule. 

*   Identify colleagues on alternative shifts or lower seniority tiers who might want the extra overtime or straight-time hours.
*   Review your local's rules regarding shift trades—some contracts require trades to be paid back within the same pay period or require explicit foreman sign-off.
*   Confirm availability with your coverage partners so you have a backup plan ready to present to management if staffing issues threaten your approved leave.

## 2 Months Out: Handle FMLA, Leave of Absence, or Extended Travel Logistics

If you are planning an extended honeymoon that stretches past your accrued paid time off, you need to address the gap now. Many union contracts allow for an unpaid Leave of Absence (LOA) for personal reasons, but these usually require formal approval from both your direct supervisor and a union rep.

Submit your LOA paperwork well ahead of the deadline. Be clear about your return date so payroll and scheduling can plan accordingly. If you are crossing state lines or traveling internationally for your honeymoon, double-check your health insurance continuation policies (such as Cobra or union-sponsored health fund rules) to ensure your coverage remains active while you are off the clock.

## The Final Month: Finalize the Paperwork and Confirm Pay

With thirty days to go, take a proactive pass at your administrative details. Union payroll errors can take weeks to untangle, and the last thing you want is a botched paycheck right before you walk down the aisle.

Check your upcoming pay stubs to ensure your vacation hours are properly accrued and scheduled to be paid out on the right check. Touch base with your payroll department or HR rep to confirm that your leave status is officially logged in the system. Finally, give your direct supervisor a polite, verbal reminder that your approved time off is approaching, ensuring everyone is on the same page.

Balancing a union job with wedding planning takes extra legwork, but treating your time-off request with the same discipline you bring to your trade will pay off. Taking these steps early ensures you can clock out with peace of mind and actually enjoy your wedding day.
