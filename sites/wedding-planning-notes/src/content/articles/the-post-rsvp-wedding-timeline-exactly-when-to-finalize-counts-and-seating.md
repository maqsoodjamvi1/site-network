When the RSVP deadline finally arrives, you might feel a massive wave of relief. The hard part of wedding planning—pestering people to drop their cards in the mail—is over. But if you think your guest list work is done, I have some news. The post-RSVP phase is actually a high-stakes sprint of math, communication, and table arrangements that sets the tone for your entire wedding weekend.

Having navigated this exact scramble myself, I learned that winging the final weeks leads to a lot of unnecessary stress. To save your sanity, you need a reverse-engineered timeline that tells you exactly who to talk to and when, starting the moment your RSVP window slams shut.

## The 4-Week Out Window: The Final Headcount and B-List Math

Your RSVP deadline should always land four weeks before your wedding date. Caterers, venues, and rental companies almost universally require your final, locked-in headcount 28 days out so they can order food, schedule staff, and build floor plans. 

The day after your RSVP deadline passes, make a list of everyone who hasn't responded. Do not wait. Call or text them immediately with a friendly script: "Hey! We are finalizing numbers with our caterer today and need to give them a hard count. Can we lock you in as a yes or no?" Give them a strict 24-hour turnaround. 

Once those stragglers are accounted for, you will inevitably have a few declines. This is your moment to activate the B-list, if you have one. Send those invitations out immediately with a quick turnaround request. By the end of this week, your total guest count is officially frozen. 

## The 3-Week Out Window: Submitting Numbers and Rental Orders

With your final number in hand, it is time to officially lock in contracts. This is where many couples lose money by failing to account for vendor meals. 

*   Count your actual guests, then add your photographer, videographer, day-of coordinator, and DJ to the total. Your venue and caterer need to feed them, and they need a chair at a table (or a dedicated vendor green room).
*   Finalize your chair and table rental counts. If you end up with 115 guests instead of 130, drop your chair rental order now. Most rental companies allow minor downward adjustments up to three weeks out, but rarely at the two-week mark.
*   Submit your dietary restriction list to your caterer. Group them clearly by table number so the serving staff doesn't have to wander around the room shouting, "Who ordered the vegan risotto?"

## The 2-Week Out Window: Tackling the Seating Chart

Do not attempt to build a seating chart before your RSVP date is completely finalized. Moving people around when numbers are fluctuating will drive you crazy. Two weeks out is the sweet spot because your list is static, but you still have time to overthink it without panicking.

Start by sorting your guests into logical groups: college friends, work colleagues, extended family, and neighbors. Put your VIPs—parents, grandparents, and the wedding party—at tables closest to the head table or sweetheart table. 

When placing difficult family dynamics, lean on practicality over perfection. If two relatives do not get along, put them on opposite sides of the room, or at large tables where they can easily avoid making eye contact. Remember, your guests will spend most of the dinner talking to the three people directly next to them, not the entire table.

## The 10-Day Out Window: Printing and Place Cards

About ten days before the wedding, your seating chart is locked, and it is time to make it physical. Whether you are using a large welcome mirror, a framed poster, or individual escort cards, this is the deadline for printing.

Alphabetize your escort cards by last name, not by table number. When a line forms at your entryway during cocktail hour, guests need to find their last initial instantly. If they have to scan a list of 120 names sorted by table, the bottleneck at the door will back up your entire cocktail hour. 

Hand your final seating chart and place cards to your day-of coordinator or venue manager, along with a diagram showing how the tables are numbered in the room.

## The Final Week: Managing Late Cancellations

Even with a locked-in headcount, life happens. In the final seven days before the wedding, you will likely get one or two last-minute cancellations due to illness or travel emergencies. 

Do not call your caterer to remove a single plate at this stage—most contracts have a threshold where numbers cannot be decreased in the final week anyway. Instead, use that extra seat for a vendor, or let a friendly single guest expand their elbow room. If a plus-one drops out, simply slide a solo guest into that seat so nobody is sitting at a lonely "odd man out" table.
