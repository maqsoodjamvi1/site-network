When I decided to plan a 20-person wedding in less than two months, everyone told me I was out of my mind. Standard wedding advice assumes you have a year, a spreadsheet with twelve different budget tabs, and a guest list that requires renting a hotel ballroom. But if you are cutting your guest list down to just your closest family and friends, you actually do not need twelve months. In fact, an eight-week timeline keeps decision fatigue low and saves you from overthinking every single detail. 

Here is the exact week-by-week checklist I used, broken down so you can plan a gorgeous, stress-free micro-wedding without losing your sanity.

## Weeks 8 to 7: The Non-Negotiables and the Venue

Before you buy a single spool of ribbon, you need to lock down the two things that dictate everything else: your budget limit and your exact headcount. For a micro-wedding, "intimate" usually means 10 to 30 people. Sit down with your partner and write out a firm maximum number. 

Once you have that number, finding a venue becomes infinitely easier than booking a traditional space. Skip the standard hotel ballrooms and look for alternative spaces that are ready-made for small groups:
* Private dining rooms in your favorite local restaurants
* A scenic public park pavilion or botanical garden that requires a simple permit
* A gorgeous Airbnb or vacation rental that allows small events (always get written permission from the host first)
* An art gallery, boutique rooftop, or greenhouse

Secure your date and venue first. Once you have a locked location and a confirmed guest list, the rest of the puzzle pieces fall into place naturally.

## Weeks 6 to 5: Wardrobe and Key Vendors

With your venue secured, turn your attention to what you are wearing and the professionals who will capture the day. Because you are on a compressed timeline, forget about ordering a custom wedding dress with a six-month production window. Look at ready-to-wear designers, department store evening wear, or high-end vintage boutiques. If your outfit needs tailoring, drop it off at a local tailor immediately and tell them your exact wedding date so they can fast-track it.

For vendors, scale down your expectations to match your guest count. You do not need a massive DJ setup with giant speakers for 20 people. Instead, hire a local acoustic musician for the ceremony and dinner, or curate a great playlist and rent a portable Bluetooth speaker. 

However, do not skimp on photography. Even with a tiny guest list, you will want high-quality photos to remember the day. Look for local photographers who offer hourly rates rather than full-day packages, which can save you a significant amount of money.

## Weeks 4 to 3: Invitations and Logistics

It is time to notify your guests. Because micro-weddings happen fast, traditional paper invitations with a six-week RSVP window will not work. Send digital invitations via text, email, or a wedding website platform. Give guests a strict two-week RSVP deadline so you can finalize your headcount.

Use this window to handle the legal requirements. Research your local county clerk’s office rules for obtaining a marriage license. Note how long the waiting period is, what identification you need to bring, and whether the license expires after a certain number of days. Book your appointment now so you are not scrambling the week of the wedding.

## Weeks 2 to 1: Details and Final Counts

By week two, your RSVPs should be locked in. If you are hosting your micro-wedding at a restaurant, give the manager your final headcount and dietary restrictions. If you are handling food yourself or hiring a private chef, confirm the menu and delivery times.

This is also the time to pull together the micro-details that make a small wedding feel deeply personal:
* Write your vows out on physical paper so you are not reading from a phone screen.
* Order a small, beautiful cake or a tower of pastries from a local bakery instead of a multi-tiered wedding cake.
* Put together handwritten place cards or welcome notes for each guest at the dinner table. Because there are only a few of you, personal touches go a long way.

## Wedding Week: Breathe and Delegate

By the time your wedding week arrives, your checklist should be virtually empty. Avoid the temptation to add DIY projects at the last minute. Assign a trusted friend or family member to be the point of contact on the day of the event—someone who can hold your emergency kit, cue the music, and make sure the venue looks right while you actually enjoy getting ready. 

Planning a micro-wedding in eight weeks forces you to focus on what actually matters: good food, great company, and marrying your favorite person without months of mounting anxiety. As you prepare to take these next steps and finalize your timeline, exploring curated resources and planning tools can help keep your momentum going.
