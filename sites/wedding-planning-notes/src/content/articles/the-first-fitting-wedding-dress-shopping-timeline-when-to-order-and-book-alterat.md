Planning a wedding involves managing a dozen moving parts at once, but few deadlines cause as much quiet panic as the bridal gown timeline. When I went through the process myself, I quickly realized that traditional advice like "just shop nine months out" leaves out the messy middle: the shipping delays, the factory sizing discrepancies, and the reality of fitting appointments. 

If you are standing at the starting line of your dress search, you need a realistic, step-by-step roadmap. Here is the exact timeline I wish I had on day one, broken down from the moment you book your first appointment to the final zip on your wedding day.

## 10 to 12 Months Out: The Order Window

Most brides are surprised to learn that wedding dresses are rarely pulled off a rack and handed to you in a garment bag. With very few exceptions, the gown you try on in the boutique is a sample. Once you say yes, the consultant takes your measurements, matches them to the designer’s specific size chart, and orders a brand-new dress to be made. 

This is why the 10-to-to-12-month mark is the golden window for ordering. Standard production timelines for most bridal designers run between four and six months. Add in international shipping, customs, and the inevitable holiday or seasonal factory slowdowns, and your dress will easily take half a year to arrive at the salon. 

If you are working with a shorter engagement, do not panic, but do pivot your strategy. Many designers offer rush cuts for an additional fee, and off-the-rack sample sales can save you months of waiting. However, standard ordering strictly requires that year-long runway.

## 6 to 8 Months Out: Securing Your Alterations Tailor

One of the biggest mistakes couples make is assuming the dress shopping ends when the dress is ordered. In reality, ordering the dress is only half the battle; making it fit your body is the other. 

You should begin researching and booking your alterations tailor around the six-to-eight-month mark—or roughly two months before your dress is scheduled to arrive. Independent bridal seamstress schedules fill up fast, especially during peak wedding season (May through October). 

If you bought your gown at a boutique with an in-house alterations department, they will usually schedule your fitting timeline automatically when the dress arrives. But if you are taking your dress to an independent tailor, call them as soon as you have an estimated delivery date. Do not wait until the box arrives on your doorstep to start looking for someone with availability.

## 2 Months Out: The First Fitting

Your dress has finally arrived at the salon, you’ve tried it on, and you’ve carried it to your first official tailoring appointment. This is where the real transformation happens. 

Plan to bring three specific items to this first fitting, because a seamstress cannot properly pin your hem without them:
* The exact shoes you plan to wear down the aisle (even a half-inch difference in heel height changes everything).
* The foundational shapewear or undergarments you will wear on the wedding day.
* The strapless bra or cups you plan to sew into the bodice.

During this first appointment, the tailor will pin the shoulders, take in or let out the side seams, and establish the length of the hem. Expect to stand on a pedestal for 45 to 60 minutes while they work. Do not worry if the dress feels completely shapeless or pinned to death—this is the rough draft phase.

## 4 Weeks Out: The Second Fitting

About a month before the wedding, you will return for your second fitting. By this point, the major structural changes have been made. The torso should fit snugly, the hem should skim the floor without dragging under your shoes, and the neckline should lay flat against your chest.

This appointment is also when the tailor will focus on the bustle—the system of buttons, hooks, or hidden ribbons used to lift your train off the floor for the reception. Watch closely as the tailor demonstrates how to bustle the dress. Take a video on your phone of the process so your maid of honor can reference it on the wedding day, as figuring out a complex French bustle in a dark bridal suite is a recipe for pre-ceremony stress.

## 1 to 2 Weeks Out: The Final Pickup

Your final fitting is usually a quick check-in rather than a major work session. You try the dress on one last time to ensure all the alterations hold up, zip it up independently, and walk around the room to make sure you can sit, breathe, and dance. 

Once you give the green light, the dress is steamed, pressed, and carefully packed into a breathable garment bag. From here, it hangs safely in your closet until the morning of the wedding. 

Navigating the bridal boutique and tailoring calendar comes down to reverse-engineering your wedding date and building in a comfortable buffer for unexpected delays.
