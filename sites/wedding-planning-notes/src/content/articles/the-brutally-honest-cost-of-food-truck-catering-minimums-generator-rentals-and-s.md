When I first started pricing out food trucks for my outdoor wedding, I thought I had cracked the code. I envisioned skipping the stuffy ballroom catering fees, paying a flat per-plate rate, and feeding my guests casual street tacos under string lights. 

Then I got the actual quotes. 

If you are looking at food trucks to save money on your wedding, I have some news that might sting. While food trucks *can* be a budget-friendly option, they come with a hidden ecosystem of logistics costs that traditional caterers usually bake quietly into their per-person pricing. Before you sign a contract, you need to understand how food truck pricing actually works so you do not get blindsided six months before your big day.

## The Minimum Spend Trap

The first surprise I ran into was the food truck minimum. You might assume that if you are only hosting 60 guests, you will only pay for 60 plates. Food truck operators, however, have high overhead and a hard cap on how fast they can push food out of a small window. 

To make a wedding gig worth their time—especially on a Friday or Saturday night when they could be parked downtown serving hundreds of people—they enforce strict minimum spending requirements. 

For a decent-sized truck, a weekend minimum often sits around $2,500 to $4,000, regardless of whether you have 100 guests or 50. If you have 75 guests and the truck's minimum is $3,000, you are suddenly paying $40 a head for food, even if you ordered their cheapest $15 slider option. You either have to pay the difference out of pocket, find a way to artificially inflate your food order, or keep looking for a smaller truck.

## The Generator Problem

Traditional catering kitchens come with their own power. Food trucks, despite having engines, often cannot run their heavy-duty flattops, fryers, and AC units off standard venue outlets. If your wedding venue is a rustic barn, a public park, a family backyard, or a raw industrial space, the truck is going to need a generator.

Truck owners will rarely let you plug into a random residential outlet because a tripped breaker means their entire stock of meat spoils mid-service. This means one of three things:
* You rent a heavy-duty, quiet inverter generator specifically for the truck.
* The truck brings their own industrial generator and tacks a $250 to $500 equipment fee onto your bill.
* You pay an electrician to install a temporary heavy-duty hookup at your venue.

Do not skip asking this question during your initial vendor call. I nearly booked a truck that assumed my venue had power, only to find out their draw required a 50-amp hookup that the venue simply did not possess.

## Service Fees, Travel, and Gratuity

When you look at a menu online, you see the price of a burrito or a slice of wood-fired pizza. You do not see the math required to get that food to a remote venue. 

Most trucks calculate a mileage fee if you are driving outside their home city limits. If your dream venue is 45 minutes out in the countryside, expect a travel surcharge. On top of that, look closely at the fine print for service fees. These are distinct from gratuity and often cover licensing, health department permits for remote sites, and basic setup. 

Speaking of service: decide early how you want the food served. If guests have to wait in a 25-person queue for their food, you are creating a bottleneck that ruins the pacing of your reception. Many couples hire extra staff or pay the truck an additional fee to do "pass-around" service or set up a stationary buffet line away from the truck window, which defeats the charm of a food truck. If you stick to traditional window service, factor in that your guests will be standing in line for 15 to 20 minutes, meaning your timeline for speeches and dancing needs to stretch accordingly.

## Hidden Costs Checklist

Before you fall in love with a truck's Instagram feed, run through this checklist with the owner to get an accurate total:

* What is your guaranteed minimum spend, and does tax and tip count toward it?
* Do you require a generator, and what is the rental fee if we need to supply one?
* Are there travel or mileage fees for our venue location?
* What is the hourly feeding rate? (Can they get all 100 guests through the line in 45 minutes, or will half the room finish eating before the other half gets their food?)
* Do you provide trash cans and cleanup, or are we responsible for the paper boat and napkin mess left on the lawn?
* What happens if it rains? Is there an indoor backup plan, or will the truck cancel if the ground is too muddy to drive onto?

Food trucks can create an incredibly fun, relaxed atmosphere that guests remember for years, but treating them like a casual fast-food stop will lead to budget overruns. Now that you know what questions to ask and where the hidden numbers live, you can vet your local vendors accurately and lock down a realistic budget for your reception.
