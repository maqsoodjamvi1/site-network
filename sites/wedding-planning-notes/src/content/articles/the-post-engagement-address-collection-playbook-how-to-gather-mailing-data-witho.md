Getting engaged is a whirlwind. Within forty-eight hours of the ring going on my finger, I realized I had a massive logistical problem on my hands: I had zero idea where most of our friends and family actually lived. Sure, I had phone numbers and Instagram handles, but I didn't have current mailing addresses for save-the-dates, let alone formal wedding invitations. 

If you are staring down a blank spreadsheet right now, take a deep breath. Gathering addresses is one of those early wedding planning tasks that sounds simple on paper, but can quickly devolve into a messy game of social media DMs and typo-ridden text threads. Worse yet, a poorly managed address collection process is the number one culprit behind accidental guest list inflation. 

Here is the exact playbook I used to collect every single mailing address we needed without letting our guest count spiral out of control.

## Lock Down Your Venue Capacity Before You Ask for Anything

Before you send a single text asking "Hey, what’s your address?", you need to know your hard ceiling. Sit down with your partner and establish your maximum venue capacity and your budget limit. 

The biggest mistake couples make is treating address collection as an informal survey. If you ask your second-cousin or a former college roommate for their address, they will immediately assume they are invited. When you inevitably have to cut them later to stay under venue limits, it creates an awkward social friction. 

Build your draft guest list in a private spreadsheet first. Make strict columns for "Must Invite," "If Budget/Space Permits," and "Do Not Invite." Only collect addresses for the first two categories—and be very careful with that second one.

## Use a Digital Form (And Skip the Group Texts)

Do not try to collect addresses via a continuous group text or by writing them down on scraps of paper. You will lose them, misspell street names, or forget whose apartment number goes with whose name. 

Instead, set up a free online form using Google Forms or Jotform. It takes about ten minutes to build. Create fields for:
* Full name(s) of guests
* Street address
* Apartment, suite, or unit number
* City, State, and Zip Code
* Email address (useful later for digital updates or thank-you notes)

Once your form is live, you can text or email the direct link to your friends and family. When they fill it out, their information populates directly into a neat, organized spreadsheet. This eliminates transcription errors and saves you hours of manual data entry.

## Create a Script to Prevent Guest List Inflation

Even with a digital form, people are naturally curious. They might text back asking, "Wait, are kids invited?" or "Is a plus-one included?" 

To prevent people from adding uninvited guests to your spreadsheet, be clear in your initial outreach. I used a casual, honest script that set expectations immediately. Send something along these lines:

"Hi [Name]! We are finally getting our wedding ducks in a row and putting together our mailing list for save-the-dates. Could you take thirty seconds to drop your current mailing address into this quick form? [Link] Can't wait to celebrate with you soon!"

Notice how this message focuses strictly on save-the-dates and logistics. It keeps the tone celebratory rather than bureaucratic, while gently signaling that this is part of an official planning phase.

## Handle the Outliers and the "No-Responders"

No matter how user-friendly your digital form is, about twenty percent of your guest list simply will not fill it out. Your older relatives might find digital forms confusing, and your chronically busy friends will likely read your text, mean to reply, and forget completely.

For older family members, just call them on the phone. Grandparents and aunts usually love the excuse for a quick chat anyway, and you can jot their address down directly. 

For the forgetful friends who ignore your form after two reminders, stop chasing them. If they are truly close to you, bite the bullet and message them directly: "Hey! Just need your address for the final envelope batch—are you still at [Old Address], or did you move recently?" This makes it easy for them to just reply "Still here!" with a quick yes or no.

Getting your mailing data sorted early takes a massive weight off your shoulders and keeps your stationery timeline on track. As you wrap up your address collection and move into designing your suites, keeping your master list organized in one place will make addressing envelopes feel infinitely more manageable.
