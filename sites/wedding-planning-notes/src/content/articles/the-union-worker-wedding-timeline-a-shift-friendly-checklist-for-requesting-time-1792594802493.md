Planning a wedding is stressful enough, but if you or your partner work under a union contract, the coordination adds a whole different layer of logistics. When your schedule is dictated by seniority lists, rotating shifts, or strict bid processes, you can’t just drop a casual "I need a Friday off" to a manager three weeks before the big day. 

Working a trade or in an industry with a Collective Bargaining Agreement (CBA) actually has some major perks for wedding planning—like guaranteed pay rates and structured leave policies—but it requires playing the long game. Here is a practical, shift-friendly timeline and checklist to help you lock in your wedding dates without risking your PTO, your sanity, or your standing at the hall.

## 12 to 18 Months Out: Review the CBA and Map the Rotation

Before you put down a single deposit on a venue, open your union contract. You need to look specifically at clauses covering personal leave, vacation bidding, bereavement leave (if you are combining a wedding and a honeymoon immediately), and FMLA rules if applicable. 

If your workplace uses an annual shift bid or a seniority-based vacation calendar, your wedding date needs to be chosen around these cycles. For example, if vacation picks happen every November for the upcoming year, you must be engaged and ready to lock in your wedding dates before that bidding window opens. Do not guess how your shop handles shift trades; talk directly to your shop steward or union rep to understand how management typically handles consecutive time-off requests during peak seasons.

## 9 to 12 Months Out: Leverage Seniority and Submit Preliminary Notices

Once you have your venue and official date secured, it is time to get ahead of the scheduling system. Even if official vacation requests aren't open yet, early communication prevents scheduling conflicts.

*   **Check the blackout dates:** Review your employer's calendar for mandatory overtime periods, seasonal shutdowns, or plant turnarounds that overlap with your wedding weekend.
*   **Talk to your supervisor informally:** Give your direct boss a heads-up that you will be requesting a specific block of time off. Building this buffer helps prevent them from acting blindsided when the official paperwork hits their desk.
*   **Line up shift coverage:** If you work in an environment where trades are common (like manufacturing, healthcare, or transit), start quietly sounding out coworkers who might be willing to cover your shifts or swap days with you later. 

## 6 Months Out: Submit Formal Leave Paperwork and Track It

This is the danger zone for union workers. If your workplace requires vacation requests to be submitted through a specific portal or via a paper slip, do it the exact day the window opens. 

Print a copy of your request confirmation or get a signed receipt from your supervisor. In many shops, union contracts dictate that vacation requests are approved strictly on a first-come, first-served basis outside of the annual bid. If management drags their feet on approving your leave, loop in your shop steward immediately to ensure they aren't violating turnaround times specified in your CBA. Make sure you request enough days on either side of the wedding for travel, rehearsal dinners, and at least a few days of decompression before heading back to the floor or the site.

## 3 Months Out: Finalize Shift Trades and Coverage Deals

By the three-month mark, you should have your official time-off approval in writing. If your leave request was partially denied—perhaps management claims they are short-staffed that weekend—now is the time to utilize your union network. 

Draft formal shift-trade agreements with coworkers who are qualified to do your specific job duties. Most union contracts have strict rules about overtime liability and shift-swap approvals, so ensure your paperwork complies with local guidelines. Getting a coworker to agree verbally isn't enough; submit the official trade forms to HR or operations so nobody gets stuck with an unexpected attendance point or a missed-punch penalty while you are cutting your cake.

## 1 Month Out: Confirm Coverage and Prep Your Crew

The final weeks before the wedding should be focused on tying up loose ends at work so you can fully disconnect. Check in one last time with the colleagues covering your shifts to ensure they have all the details they need. Leave clear handover notes if you are working a technical role. 

If you are taking a two-week honeymoon, confirm your return-to-work date with HR and verify that your health insurance or dues deductions won't be disrupted during an unpaid leave of absence. Taking these proactive steps ensures that when you walk down the aisle, you won't have a single thought about what's happening back on the job.

Navigating a union schedule while planning a wedding takes extra coordination, but starting early puts you firmly in control of your time.
