When I booked my first bridal salon appointment, I thought it would be just like the movies: sipping champagne, trying on a few glamorous silhouettes, and crying tears of joy into a mirror while my friends cheered. Instead, I walked into a brightly lit room, stared at a rack of three hundred identical white gowns, and immediately broke out in a sweat. Nobody warns you that bridal sizing makes zero sense, or that clips pinch, or that you have to stand half-naked in front of a total stranger about ten minutes after walking through the door. 

If you are feeling a knot of anxiety in your stomach thinking about your upcoming shopping trip, take a deep breath. You can completely bypass the panic by doing a little bit of tactical prep ahead of time. Here is how I wish I had prepared for my first round of appointments, broken down into practical steps you can take this week.

## Untangle Your Undergarments Before You Go

Let’s start with the most awkward part of the day: the fitting room. Your stylist is going to be in the room with you, helping you shimmy into corsets, button heavy silk, and adjust layers of tulle. To make this as painless as possible, your choice of underwear matters immensely. 

Wear nude, seamless underwear. Avoid brightly colored lace thongs or high-waisted shapewear that has thick seams, because they will show through the sample gowns—which are almost always thin, unlined, and ivory or blush. 

As for bras, leave yours at home. Most wedding dresses have heavy internal structure, boning, and cups already sewn into the bodice. Wearing a regular bra will only get in the way when you try on strapless, backless, or plunging styles. If you are worried about support or coverage, buy a pair of adhesive silicone lift-covers or a simple strapless bustier, but honestly, most stylists prefer working with a completely bare canvas up top.

## Bring the Right Shoes and Hair Ties

You do not need to buy your wedding shoes before you buy your dress, but you do need to understand how height changes a hemline. If you plan to wear three-inch block heels on your wedding day, trying on dresses barefoot will throw off the proportions. 

Grab a pair of shoes that matches the heel height you actually intend to wear. If you are wearing flat sandals or Converse sneakers, bring those instead. 

Hair is the other secret weapon. When you pull a dress over your head, loose hair gets static-clingy, tangled in zippers, and makeup-stained. Bring a hair tie, a claw clip, or bobby pins so you can throw your hair up into a messy bun halfway through the appointment. Being able to instantly see how a dress looks with your hair up versus down will help you visualize the final bridal look without guessing.

## Curate Your Entourage Wisely

Everyone in your life will have an opinion on your wedding dress, but you only need to hear the ones that actually matter on appointment day. The biggest mistake you can make is bringing a crowd of six people because you feel obligated to include everyone. 

Limit your crew to two or three people maximum. Choose the folks who know your personal style better than you do, and who will genuinely support your vision rather than projecting their own. If your mom loves ballgowns but you secretly want a sleek, modern slip dress, bringing a mom who will sigh every time you put on a minimalist gown is a recipe for tears of frustration. 

Set expectations with your crew before you walk through the salon doors. Tell them, "I want to try on things outside my comfort zone just to be sure, so please keep an open mind." 

## Ditch the Exact Pinterest Board

You probably have a Pinterest board titled "Wedding Stuff" with four hundred saved pins. That is great for finding general aesthetics, but stop treating those specific dresses as a shopping checklist. 

Bridal boutiques carry specific designers, and local salons rarely have the exact dress you saw on a European runway influencer last Tuesday. More importantly, what looks stunning on a five-foot-ten model might completely overwhelm your frame, or vice versa. 

Instead of searching for a specific dress, focus on communication. When you meet your stylist, tell them how you want to *feel* rather than what you want to wear. Saying "I want to feel like a Greek goddess who is comfortable enough to dance all night" gives your stylist much better direction than pointing at a photo and saying "I want this exact lace pattern." Let the stylist pull a few wildcard options, too—they have an eye for silhouettes that flatter specific body types in ways you might not expect.

Getting ready for your dress appointments is mostly about lowering the stakes and giving yourself permission to figure it out as you go. You do not need to find "The One" on your very first try, and you certainly don't need to know every single design detail right now.
