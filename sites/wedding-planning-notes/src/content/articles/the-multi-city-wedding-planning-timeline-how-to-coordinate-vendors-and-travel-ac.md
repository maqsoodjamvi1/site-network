Planning a wedding is stressful enough when everything happens in your hometown. When you are trying to coordinate florists, caterers, and guests across state lines—or even just a few hours apart in different major metro areas—the complexity multiplies quickly. I learned this the hard way when planning my own ceremony in Savannah, Georgia, while living and working in New York City. Between coordinating venue walk-throughs around flight schedules and paying heavy out-of-town travel fees for my photographer, I quickly realized standard wedding checklists do not cut it for multi-city logistics. 

If you are currently juggling multiple locations for your big day, here is the practical, step-by-step timeline I used to keep my sanity, manage my budget, and get all my important people to the right place at the right time.

## 10 to 12 Months Out: Define Your Hubs and Lock the Big Vendors

Before you put down a deposit on a scenic barn three states away, you need to map out your primary locations. Usually, multi-city weddings involve a "home base" where you live, the "destination base" where the wedding happens, and potentially a third location if you are hosting a rehearsal dinner or post-wedding brunch elsewhere. 

During this phase, decide which vendors must travel and which you should hire locally. For instance, hair and makeup artists are notoriously difficult to fly in cost-effectively. It is almost always cheaper to hire local beauty professionals in your wedding city and schedule a trial run for the next time you visit. On the other hand, if you have a specific photographer whose artistic style you love, budget for their flight and hotel stay rather than gambling on someone unfamiliar in your wedding market.

## 6 to 9 Months Out: Batch Your Travel and Schedule Mega-Trips

Do not make the mistake of flying or driving out to your wedding city every time you need to make a decision. Instead, batch your tasks into two or three high-productivity weekend trips. 

*   **Trip One (Tasting and Trials):** Schedule menu tastings with your caterer, cake consultations, and floral mock-ups all within a strict 48-hour window. 
*   **Trip Two (Rehearsal and Final Walk-Through):** Meet with your day-of coordinator at the venue to walk through the exact timeline, rain or shine. 

When booking these trips, use the same loyalty programs or airlines consistently. If your guests will be traveling the same route, set up a hotel room block now and monitor flight trends so you can give your attendees a heads-up when ticket prices drop.

## 3 to 5 Months Out: Build a Shared Digital Logistics Hub

When you are working across state lines, communication breakdowns happen easily. A vendor in Chicago might assume a New York-based coordinator is handling the rental drop-off, while the coordinator thinks otherwise. 

Create a shared cloud folder or wedding management dashboard that includes master timelines, flight itineraries for out-of-town wedding party members, and contact sheets with exact arrival times. Make sure your day-of coordinator has direct access to this. If you are hiring a local coordinator in your wedding city, ensure they are comfortable managing remote couples and have experience dealing with vendors who are traveling in from out of state.

## 6 to 8 Weeks Out: Finalize the Travel Manifest and Transport Plan

By this point, RSVPs are rolling in, and you need to lock down how people are moving around. Multi-city weddings often involve a gap between the ceremony site and the reception venue, or hotels that are miles away from the main events.

Book shuttle buses or private vans to move your guests safely between the hotel block and the venue, especially if out-of-towners won't have rental cars or are unfamiliar with local traffic patterns. Create a physical or digital insert for your welcome bags that clearly outlines pick-up times for the shuttles, local taxi apps that work well in the area, and emergency contact numbers for your wedding planner.

## The Final Week: Delegate the Remote Details

In the final week, you cannot be physically present to sign for arriving decor shipments or meet rental truck drivers at the venue. This is where your local point-of-contact comes in. Hand off the responsibility of receiving deliveries, managing early set-ups, and dealing with last-minute local hiccups to your coordinator or a trusted local friend. Pack your own bags with all critical items—such as marriage licenses, rings, and custom wedding party gifts—in your carry-on luggage if you are flying.

Getting married across multiple cities takes extra organization, but seeing your vision come together miles away from home is deeply rewarding.
