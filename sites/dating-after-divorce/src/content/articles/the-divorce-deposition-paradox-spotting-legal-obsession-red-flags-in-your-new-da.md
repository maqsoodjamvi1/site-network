When you start dating again after a long marriage or a messy split, your internal radar is already working overtime. You are looking for the usual red flags: mismatched life goals, bad tippers, or someone who still talks about their mother way too much. But there is a very specific, highly exhausting behavior that often flies under the radar until it is too far along: the person who treats dating like a discovery phase in a high-stakes civil lawsuit.

I learned this the hard way a few years ago. I went out for coffee with a man who, within forty-five minutes, began cross-examining me about my financial history, my exact custody arrangements, and the timeline of my previous relationship down to the calendar month. It did not feel like a conversation. It felt like a deposition. 

If you are dating in your thirties, forties, or beyond, you will inevitably run into people who are carrying unhealed legal baggage. Spotting this early can save you months of emotional exhaustion. Here is how to tell the difference between someone who is healthily cautious and someone who is treating you like an adverse witness.

## The Interrogation Versus the Conversation

A normal first or second date is a two-way street of discovery. You share a story, they share a story, and you both gauge whether you like each other's vibe. Legal obsession looks completely different. It is one-sided, relentless, and clinical.

Watch out for the person who asks rapid-fire questions designed to pin you down to specifics rather than get to know your personality. If they are asking you to clarify statements you made twenty minutes ago—"Wait, earlier you said you moved here in May, but now you are saying spring? Which is it?"—you are sitting across from a Perry Mason wannabe, not a romantic partner. 

Healthy people want to know how you feel. Legally obsessed people want to know what you can prove. If every date feels like you are on the witness stand trying to avoid perjury over brunch, take a step back.

## The Weaponized Past

Everyone has baggage. Divorced adults, in particular, have paperwork. But there is a massive chasm between having a difficult past and making your past the third wheel on every single date.

When someone is obsessed with the legal mechanics of their breakup, they talk about it with an eerie amount of jargon. They use terms like "injunction," "discovery," "bad faith," and "contempt of court" casually over tacos. They view their ex not as a human being they once loved, but as a litigious adversary in an ongoing war. 

Here is the real danger: people who are obsessed with their legal battles are almost always looking for an ally to validate their grievances, not a partner to build a future with. If you notice that every date involves a thirty-minute recap of their lawyer's latest email, they are not emotionally available. They are emotionally occupied by their docket.

## Financial and Digital Forensics

In a deposition, lawyers look for inconsistencies in your story, your spending, and your digital footprint. A legally obsessed date will often project these tactics onto you.

Pay attention to how they talk about your social media presence or your schedule. Do they question why you haven't posted pictures of your home, or do they cross-reference your whereabouts based on things you mentioned weeks ago? 

I once dated someone who kept a mental spreadsheet of my travel schedule, asking pointed questions about who I was with and where I stayed, under the guise of "just being curious about my life." It wasn't curiosity; it was surveillance. When people have been burned in the legal system—especially in bitter asset divisions—they often develop trust issues so severe that they audit every person they meet. You are not responsible for auditing their anxiety, and you certainly don't need to submit receipts for your weekend plans.

## How to Handle the Deposition Date

If you find yourself across the table from someone treating the evening like a pre-trial hearing, you do not need to play along. You do not need to answer rapid-fire financial questions or provide a detailed alibi for your single years.

Set a hard boundary immediately. You can say something simple like, "I'm looking to keep our conversations focused on the present and getting to know each other, rather than dissecting legal histories right now." 

Watch their reaction closely. A reasonable person will apologize, realize they crossed a line, and pivot the conversation to something lighter. A legally obsessed person will often double down, arguing that they are just being "practical" or "protecting themselves," and that you should appreciate their transparency. That defense is your final cue to exit.

Navigating the dating world after a certain age requires patience, but it also requires strong boundaries. Protecting your peace means recognizing when someone is bringing a courtroom mentality to a coffee shop. 

As you think about how to spot these warning signs and protect your energy moving forward, take a moment to evaluate the pacing of the next dates you accept. Assessing your own boundaries early on makes it much easier to walk away when someone's baggage starts weighing down your evening.
