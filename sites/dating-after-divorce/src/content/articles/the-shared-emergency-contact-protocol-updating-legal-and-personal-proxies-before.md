Stepping back into the dating scene after a long hiatus usually involves updating your wardrobe, maybe downloading a couple of apps, and figuring out how to take a decent selfie. But a few years ago, after a bad first date where I lost my phone and my ride home in an unfamiliar neighborhood, I realized I had missed the most critical preparation step. My emergency contacts were still set to my ex-boyfriend from five years prior. 

When you are single and navigating modern dating—meeting strangers from the internet in dimly lit bars or driving out to unfamiliar suburbs—your physical safety net matters just as much as your emotional boundaries. Before you go on your first active date, you need a shared emergency contact protocol. Here is how I set mine up, and how you can do it this afternoon.

## Why Your Old Emergency Contacts Need an Audit

Most of us set our emergency contacts on our phones once and never look at them again. If you are like most adults, your emergency contact is probably a parent who lives three time zones away, or an ex you are no longer on speaking terms with. 

If you get into a situation where you need an advocate—whether that is a medical emergency, a scary encounter with a stranger, or simply being stranded with a dead phone—an out-of-state parent cannot call an Uber for you or meet you at local urgent care. 

Your dating emergency contact needs to be someone local, level-headed, and reachable during the exact hours you are likely to be out on dates (typically Thursday through Saturday evenings). They also need to know the specific parameters of where you are going. 

## Step 1: Designate Your "First Alert" Buddy

Pick one trusted friend or family member who lives within thirty minutes of your usual hangout spots. This person needs to agree to act as your dating proxy. 

My friend Sarah is my designated buddy. Before I leave the house for a first date, I send her a standard text template. It includes the first name of the person I am meeting, the name and address of the venue, and the estimated time I plan to check in. 

Crucially, establish a "silence means trouble" rule. If I tell Sarah I will text her by 9:00 PM to let her know I am heading home, and she does not hear from me by 9:15 PM, she has permission to call me. If I do not pick up after two calls, she knows to check my location sharing and take the next step we agreed upon. 

## Step 2: Leverage Real-Time Location Sharing

Relying on someone to guess where you are is a recipe for panic. Instead, use built-in smartphone features to share your live location temporarily. 

Both iOS and Android make it simple to share your location for a set duration, such as "until the end of the day." When I meet someone new, I turn on live location sharing with Sarah for four hours. 

- Open your messaging app or map application.
- Select your trusted contact and tap "Share My Location."
- Set a hard expiration time so you do not accidentally broadcast your whereabouts for the next week.
- Confirm that your contact can actually see your dot moving on their screen before you walk out the front door.

This eliminates the awkwardness of manual check-ins while ensuring someone has eyes on your transit routes, especially if you are taking public transportation or rideshares home.

## Step 3: Update Your Legal Proxies and Phone ICE Settings

While a dating buddy helps with immediate safety on a Friday night, you also need to look at your permanent legal and medical paperwork if you are jumping back into dating seriously. 

If you are incapacitated on a date and taken to a hospital, medical staff will look at your phone's In Case of Emergency (ICE) settings. 

- **Update your phone's lock screen:** Make sure your lock screen medical ID lists a current, local contact who can make decisions or show up at the hospital. 
- **Healthcare proxy paperwork:** If you own a home or have specific medical wishes, ensure your designated healthcare proxy form lists someone who is actually local and current in your life. 

It feels slightly morbid to update your medical power of attorney just because you downloaded a dating app, but think of it as baseline adult maintenance. You update your car insurance before taking a road trip; updating your emergency contacts is the insurance policy for your social life.

## Moving Forward With Confidence

Taking these steps takes about fifteen minutes, but it completely changes how you feel when you walk into a date. You stop worrying about the "what ifs" because you have built a reliable, practical safety net behind the scenes. Once your digital breadcrumbs are set and your local proxy is briefed, you can focus entirely on whether you actually like the person sitting across from you.
