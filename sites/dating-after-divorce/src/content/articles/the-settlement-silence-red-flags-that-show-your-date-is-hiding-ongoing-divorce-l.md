Dating someone going through a messy breakup is complicated enough. Dating someone who claims they are "divorcing" while actively hiding ongoing litigation is an entirely different hazard. When a date hides the courtroom reality of their split—subpoenas, contested asset division, temporary custody orders, or looming deposition dates—they aren't just protecting their privacy. They are drafting you into a high-conflict legal drama without your consent. 

Over the years of coaching clients through mid-life dating, I have seen too many people invest their hearts and homes into someone whose divorce papers were still churning through county clerks' offices long after they claimed to be "basically single." Here are the specific, unvarnished red flags that show your date is hiding ongoing divorce litigation, and how to spot them before you get tangled in the fallout.

## The Logistics Do Not Match the Timeline

When someone tells you they have been separated for six months, their calendar should reflect a single life. If they are hiding ongoing litigation, their schedule will feature bizarre, rigid anomalies that they blame on "work" or "family." 

Look closely at how they handle weekends and evenings. If they are locked in a contested custody battle, they will have strict court-ordered parenting time. A date who suddenly vanishes every other Thursday evening and every alternating weekend—without offering clear details about their kids—is often bound by a temporary custody schedule. Furthermore, if they claim their divorce was "finalized online" or "just needs a judge's signature," but they still refuse to invite you to their house or insist you can only hang out at your place, check your local county's online court portal. If their name pops up as a respondent in an active domestic relations case with a trial date set for next month, you are dealing with active litigation.

## Financial Friction and the "Frozen Asset" Excuse

Divorce litigation freezes assets, restricts spending, and requires full financial disclosure. If your date is dodging basic financial milestones or acting strangely paranoid about money, the courthouse is usually the culprit.

Watch out for the date who can never pay with a credit card, insists exclusively on cash, or makes bizarre excuses about why they cannot log into their bank account on their phone in front of you. In many jurisdictions, running up joint debt or hiding marital funds during active litigation is a punishable offense. If your partner is hiding an ongoing case, they may be terrified that a forensic accountant or their spouse's aggressive divorce attorney will subpoena their personal records and trace dinners out, gifts, or weekend getaways back to marital funds. If they suddenly pump the brakes on moving in together, buying property, or even taking a pricey vacation because "money is tied up," ask direct questions about the paperwork backing that up.

## The "Crazy Ex" Narrative Is Overly Litigious

Everyone has baggage, but someone hiding active litigation weaponizes their spouse's legal moves as pure emotional instability rather than legal strategy. 

If your date constantly complains that their ex is "dragging things out just to be mean," pay attention to the actual mechanics of what they are describing. Often, when a date says their ex is "refusing to sign," it actually means their lawyer advised them not to accept a deeply unfair settlement. If they casually mention that process servers keep showing up at strange places, or that they have to check their email every morning for updates from their legal team, the conflict isn't just an emotional ghost of the past—it is an active legal battle happening right now. A person who has truly moved on speaks about their legal dissolution with boring, administrative neutrality. A person hiding active litigation speaks about it with high-strung paranoia, constantly checking their phone for the next filing.

## How to Protect Your Peace and Next Steps

Discovering that your partner lied about the status of their legal proceedings feels a lot like betrayal, and practically speaking, it introduces immense volatility into your own life. Active lawsuits can drain bank accounts, consume emotional bandwidth, and occasionally drag third parties into depositions or discovery requests if joint financial commingling occurs early in a new relationship. 

If you suspect your date is keeping secrets about a pending courtroom battle, the best approach is direct and unambiguous. Ask to see the judgment of dissolution, or simply state that you are not comfortable moving forward with someone whose legal marital status is ambiguous. You deserve a partner whose life is fully open for inspection, not someone treating their divorce docket like a state secret. Consider evaluating your boundaries around transparency and legal history as you decide how to handle your next conversation.
