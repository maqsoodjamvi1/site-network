When you are a few dates into seeing someone new, money usually isn't the first thing on your mind. You are probably focused on whether you like their laugh, what they do for a living, and how they treat the server. But once you hit the three-to-six-month mark and start talking about a future, financial history matters. 

If your date is divorced, their financial past doesn't just live in a filing cabinet from a previous life. It can actively bleed into your present. One of the most telling, yet frequently overlooked, pieces of financial residue is the joint tax return. 

## Why Joint Tax Returns Linger After a Divorce

People often assume that once a judge signs a divorce decree, financial ties are cleanly severed. In reality, the IRS doesn't care about your family court timeline; it cares about your filing status. 

Depending on when a couple’s final legal separation occurs during a calendar year, they might file a joint return for that final year, or even subsequent years if legal proceedings drag on. More importantly, filing jointly means both people sign a legal document swearing under penalty of perjury that the numbers are correct. It also binds them to something called "joint and several liability."

This means the IRS can legally come after either person for the entire tax debt, plus penalties and interest, even if one spouse earned zero dollars and the other hid income offshore. When your new partner tells you, "Oh, my ex and I just signed one last return together to keep things simple," that is a flashing yellow light. It means their financial fortunes are still legally intertwined with someone else's.

## Spotting the Red Flags During Casual Conversation

You do not need to ask for a credit report on the third date, but you should listen closely to how your date talks about their past marriage and current obligations. Financial entanglement rarely stays hidden if you know what to listen for.

Watch out for these specific verbal cues:
*   "We are still waiting on a massive IRS refund from a joint return we filed while separated." (This means money is tied up, and an audit could drag you both in later.)
*   "My ex handles all our old tax stuff because they know the accountant." (This signals a continued, unnecessary dependence.)
*   "I had to pay a huge tax bill because of something my ex did on our last joint return." (This indicates unresolved liability and poor vetting of legal documents.)
*   "We haven't officially updated our withholding with our employers yet."

If you hear these phrases, it tells you that the emotional separation happened, but the administrative uncoupling is incomplete. 

## The Real-World Risk to Your Own Future

Why should this matter to you as a dating partner? Because financial messiness is contagious. 

Imagine you date someone for a year, things get serious, and you eventually move in together or consider marriage. If your partner is still untangling joint tax returns, they might be dealing with unexpected tax liens, delayed refunds, or IRS audits. If the IRS slaps a levy on their bank account for a disputed joint return from three years ago, that affects their cash flow. If you are sharing rent, grocery bills, or a joint savings account by that point, their financial emergency suddenly impacts your daily life.

Furthermore, a person who is cavalier about signing legal documents with an ex—just to keep the peace or avoid a fight—might struggle with healthy boundaries. It shows a reluctance to handle uncomfortable legal and financial realities directly.

## How to Handle Financial Baggage Gracefully

You are not an auditor, and it is not your job to interrogate your partner across the dinner table. However, you do have a right to protect your own peace of mind. 

If your partner mentions an ongoing entanglements with an ex's finances, treat it as an opportunity to observe their problem-solving skills. Ask calm, curious questions. How are they resolving it? Are they working with a certified public accountant or a family law attorney to ensure a clean break? Do they have a clear timeline for when these old returns will be fully settled and behind them?

Look for accountability. A healthy partner will acknowledge that mixing finances post-divorce was a mistake or a temporary necessity, and they will be actively working to cut the cord. If they brush it off as no big deal while stressing out about the mail every April, that mismatch between attitude and anxiety is your cue to tread carefully.

As you navigate the complexities of dating someone with a financial past, keeping your eyes open early on saves you from costly surprises down the road. Evaluating how a partner wraps up old chapters is one of the best ways to predict how they will handle new ones with you.
