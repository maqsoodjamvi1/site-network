Dating someone new is exciting, but when you are dating as an adult, baggage comes with the territory. Most people have an ex or two in their past. However, there is a massive difference between someone who has been legally single for three years and someone who is currently sitting across a restaurant table trying to hide the fact that they are knee-deep in a contested family court battle. 

When a person conceals active divorce litigation, it is rarely about keeping things "private" for a first or second date. Usually, it is about control, distraction, or using a new partner as an emotional and financial cushion while their life burns down in the courtroom. 

If you suspect the person you are seeing is omitting a major detail about their marital status, here are the specific red flags that indicate ongoing divorce litigation—and what you can actually do to protect yourself.

## The "Busy" Schedule That Revolves Around Court Dates

Everyone has hectic work weeks, but someone going through an active divorce operates on a very specific, erratic calendar. If your date is constantly rescheduling at the last minute with vague excuses like "something urgent came up with my lawyer" or "family court stuff," pay attention. 

Active litigation involves mandatory mediation sessions, unexpected depositions, hearings that get pushed back, and frantic evening calls with counsel. If their schedule feels chaotic in a way that seems tied to weekdays, paperwork, or sudden emotional crashes, look closer. Someone whose divorce was finalized years ago does not have impromptu crises involving a legal team every Thursday afternoon.

## Financial Peculiarities and Strange Cash Habits

Divorce freezes assets, and people trying to hide money or avoid spousal support often behave in ways that bleed into their dating lives. If your date insists on only paying in cash, hesitates to book a vacation more than two weeks in advance, or gets visibly cagey when you ask basic questions about their living situation, alarm bells should ring.

Watch out for the housing anomaly, too. If they claim to own a home but live temporarily in a sparse corporate apartment, or if they claim they are "just crashing with a buddy" for an unusually long stretch, they might have been ordered out of the marital residence by a judge. A newly single person setting up a permanent home looks very different from someone squatting in temporary housing while a property division is fought over.

## The Narrative Doesn't Add Up Chronologically

Liars slip up on timelines. When you ask about their past, a person hiding an active divorce will often blur the lines of when they actually separated from their spouse. They might say, "We’ve been separated for two years," but when you cross-reference stories, you realize they lived in the same house until six months ago. 

Another classic tell is the "ghost spouse" who is mentioned with intense, white-hot animosity rather than casual indifference. If they talk about their ex with an obsessive frequency—even if it is pure hatred—it means the legal battle is fresh enough that the emotional cord hasn't been cut. True emotional detachment takes time; active litigation keeps the wound wide open.

## Social Media Blackouts and Privacy Extremes

We live in a digital age where most adults have a footprint. If your date has zero social media presence, uses a completely fake name online, or explicitly asks you not to tag them in photos or check in at restaurants, ask yourself why. 

While some people value privacy, extreme digital concealment is a favorite tactic of people trying to hide assets, children, or an active public court docket from a jealous spouse or a sharp-eyed investigator. If they are aggressively protective of their phone—turning it face down, taking calls in the parking lot, or locking the screen the second you walk back into the room—they might be texting their divorce lawyer or, worse, their actual spouse.

## What You Should Do Next

If these signs are hitting too close to home, do not panic, but do not ignore your gut. Trust your instincts if the math simply does not add up. Before you invest more of your heart, time, and energy into someone who may still be legally and emotionally entangled elsewhere, you need to verify the facts so you can make an informed choice about your own future.
