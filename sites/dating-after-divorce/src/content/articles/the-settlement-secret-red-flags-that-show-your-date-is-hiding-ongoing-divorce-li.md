When you start dating again as an adult, you expect a certain amount of baggage. Maybe your new match has an ex they still talk to, or they are figuring out how to co-parent a rescue dog. But there is a massive difference between a routine breakup and an active, high-conflict legal battle winding its way through family court. 

People in the middle of ongoing divorce litigation sometimes try to hide it. They might worry that mentioning a pending legal proceeding on a first or second date makes them look unstable, or they might be trying to protect assets from a spouse while looking for a fresh start. 

If you are dating someone new and suspect their "ex" is actually still a legal spouse, look past the polite conversation. Here are the specific, real-world warning signs that your date is hiding ongoing divorce litigation, and what you can actually do about it before you get too invested.

## Their Schedule Has Bizarre, Rigid Blind Spots

Everyone gets busy with work, family, and hobbies. However, someone going through an active divorce faces specific scheduling constraints dictated by temporary court orders, attorneys, and mandated mediation sessions. 

If your date can never hang out on specific weekdays, always has to take phone calls in their car with the window rolled up, or suddenly cancels because of "family court" that they quickly rebrand as a "work emergency," pay attention. Notice if they have custody schedules that seem weirdly specific—like dropping everything every other Thursday at precisely 5:00 PM—yet they refuse to explain the custody arrangement clearly. When someone is hiding a pending court date, their calendar will have erratic gaps that they cover up with vague excuses.

## Financial Transparency Hits a Brick Wall

Dating in your thirties, forties, and beyond usually involves talking about money to some degree. You split the bill, you talk about career goals, or you discuss upcoming vacations. But someone whose divorce is currently winding through the courts is legally barred from hiding or transferring assets. Their financial life is under a microscope by a spouse's divorce attorney.

If your date insists on strictly using cash, refuses to let you pick up mail at their house, or gets visibly anxious when you suggest going to a restaurant in their own neighborhood, they might be worried about running into a process server or a private investigator. Furthermore, if they claim to own a home or a business but refuse to ever host you there, citing "remodeling" or "roommate situations" for months on end, the real reason is often a marital home tied up in equitable distribution litigation.

## They Speak in the Present Tense About a 'Past' Separation

Listen closely to the pronouns and verb tenses your date uses when they talk about their former life. Someone who finalized their divorce a year ago talks about their legal spouse in the past tense: "My ex and I used to live there," or "We finalized everything last summer."

Someone in the thick of litigation often slips up. They might say, "My wife’s lawyer wants us to handle holidays this way," or "We are still figuring out the house." If you point this out and they immediately scramble to correct themselves—claiming they meant "ex-wife" out of habit—trust your gut. People rarely slip up and use the present tense for a relationship that has been legally dead for years. 

## Their Digital Footprint Tells a Different Story

You do not need to become a private investigator, but a casual glance at public records can save you months of emotional fallout. In most jurisdictions, divorce filings are matters of public record. 

* Check county court websites where your date currently lives or previously lived with a spouse. Search civil family court dockets using their full legal name.
* Look for recent filings regarding property deeds, motion hearings, or temporary financial orders.
* Notice if their social media profiles are completely scrubbed of family photos, yet their tagged photos from friends show them wearing a wedding band as recently as a few months ago.

If you find an active case number, it does not necessarily mean you should run for the hills. Some divorces drag on for years due to complex assets or court backlogs. The real red flag is not the litigation itself; it is the active deception about it.
