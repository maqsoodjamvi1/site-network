Dating someone who is technically still married can feel like stepping into a minefield disguised as a meadow. When a new person tells you, "We’ve been separated for two years, and the final decree is just sitting on a judge's desk," it sounds like a formality. You might think the emotional heavy lifting is already done. 

From my own years of watching friends navigate this—and going through it myself—I've learned that a pending divorce timeline is rarely just about paperwork. It is often a smoke detector for messy boundaries, lingering attachment, and practical legal hazards that can blindside you. If you are currently dating someone waiting on a final court stamp, here is how to spot the difference between an innocent administrative delay and a parade of red flags.

## The "Endless Filing" Loop

In an ideal world, an uncontested divorce moves from a petition to a signed decree in a matter of months. When a date tells you their final paperwork has been "two weeks away" for the past eight months, pay attention. Courts are slow, but they are not *that* slow without a reason.

Ask yourself what is actually causing the holdup. Is the ex-spouse dragging their feet on asset division? Is your date avoiding a difficult conversation about child support calculations because they are afraid of conflict? When someone perpetually blames the court system for a delayed timeline, they are often avoiding accountability for finishing their own life chapter. If they cannot finalize a legal proceeding, they may also struggle to finalize emotional closure.

## The Stealth Relationship Status

When someone is in the final stages of a divorce, transparency is your best shield. Look closely at how your date talks about their living situation and their ex. 

If they insist on meeting exclusively at your place because "it’s just easier," or if they take phone calls from their lawyer in the car rather than stepping inside, you need to pump the brakes. A person who has genuinely moved on will have clear boundaries with their ex-spouse. 

*   **Check the communication channels:** Are they texting their ex late at night about non-emergency matters? 
*   **Observe their public presence:** Are they hiding your existence on social media because they are worried the soon-to-be-ex will see it and blow up the divorce settlement? 
*   **Evaluate the living arrangements:** If they still share a roof or a garage with their spouse "for the sake of the kids" or financial convenience, you are not entering a dating relationship; you are stepping into a household dynamic you cannot control.

## Using You as an Emotional Rebound Shield

Divorce is one of the most stressful life events a person can endure. When someone starts dating before the ink is dry on the decree, they often use a new partner as a human sedative. 

You might feel like you have an incredible, fast-moving connection, but ask yourself if you are hearing more about their legal battles than about your own life. If every date night feels like a therapy session where you act as the unpaid counselor comforting them through loneliness and legal dread, you are experiencing the rebound effect. A person going through a major life dissolution needs stability, but they need to provide it for themselves rather than borrowing it from you.

## Financial and Legal Entanglements

Romance makes us want to ignore the boring details, but pending divorces carry real financial baggage. Until that judge signs the decree, your new partner’s financial decisions are legally tied to someone else.

If they are hiding debt, hiding assets to shield them from the divorce court, or borrowing money from you while waiting for their settlement to clear, walk away immediately. Furthermore, in some states, incurring new debt or making major purchases while a divorce is pending can complicate their legal standing. If they are asking you to co-sign anything, move in together, or invest in their business ventures before they are legally single, you are taking on legal risks that belong in a courtroom, not a budding romance.

Navigating this situation requires a clear-eyed assessment of whether your date is genuinely available or just looking for a soft landing place to cushion their transition. Protecting your own peace means setting boundaries around how much emotional labor you invest while their past life is still legally unresolved. 

As you weigh whether to continue investing your time in this relationship, it helps to look at the concrete patterns rather than the excuses. Taking a step back to evaluate your next steps will ensure you do not end up collateral damage in someone else's unfinished business.
