If you have spent any time looking into manifestation techniques, you have likely run into the 369 method. Popularized on social media and rooted in Nikola Tesla's supposed obsession with the numbers 3, 6, and 9, the process is simple: you write down your desire 3 times in the morning, 6 times in the afternoon, and 9 times in the night for 33 or 45 days. 

It works great when your goal is entirely within your control. Writing "I wake up with a clear schedule" or "I feel confident walking into meetings" is straightforward. But what happens when your manifestation involves other people? 

Maybe you are trying to get a promotion that requires sign-off from a reluctant department head, or you are trying to sell a house to a specific type of buyer, or you want to resolve a bitter co-parenting dispute with an ex. When your goal relies on other human beings making choices, standard scripting often falls flat. You cannot script someone else’s free will, and trying to force a specific outcome onto another person usually just creates internal resistance.

Here is how to restructure your 369 scripting when your goal involves multiple stakeholders, so you can manifest the outcome without trying to control human puppets.

## Shift the Script From "Them" to "The Dynamic"

The biggest mistake people make when manifesting involving other people is writing scripts that read like stage directions. Writing "My boss hands me the VP title and a 20% raise" puts the focus on an external action that you cannot force. If your boss is having a terrible quarter, that specific script is going to feel like a lie every time you write it down.

Instead, shift your focus to the shared dynamic and your own state of being within that room. When multiple stakeholders are involved, your job is to manifest the friction-free environment where a mutually beneficial decision can naturally occur.

For example, instead of writing:
*   *Bad:* "The committee approves my budget immediately."
*   *Good:* "My presentation to the committee lands with absolute clarity, creating an atmosphere of trust and forward momentum."

This subtle pivot removes the dependency on a specific person's arbitrary mood and focuses instead on the energetic space you are occupying together. You are scripting your influence and the quality of the interaction, not demanding a specific compliance from them.

## Use the 3-6-9 Progression to Deconstruct the Stakeholders

The beauty of the 369 method is that it gives you three distinct writing phases throughout the day. When you are dealing with a complex web of people, use this structure to address different layers of the problem: morning for your mindset, afternoon for the collective energy, and evening for the ultimate resolution.

Here is how to map a real-world scenario—say, buying a competitive piece of real estate where you need the seller, the seller's agent, and your own bank to cooperate—into the daily 369 structure:

*   **Morning (3 times):** Focus on your own internal alignment and clarity. Write down how you show up. *Example: I approach this negotiation with a calm, grounded, and unshakeable sense of timing.*
*   **Afternoon (6 times):** Focus on the friction points and the stakeholders. Write down the dissolving of obstacles between the parties. *Example: Communication flows smoothly and transparently between all parties involved in the transaction.*
*   **Evening (9 times):** Focus on the final, harmonious result that benefits everyone. *Example: Everyone involved walks away feeling respected, satisfied, and secure in this agreement.*

This tiered approach stops you from blindly repeating a rigid demand nine times a day, which often starts to feel desperate rather than powerful. 

## Focus on the Feeling of Consensus, Not Coercion

Manifestation works best when it lowers your cortisol and builds your confidence. If you are scripting about other people and you feel a knot in your stomach—wondering if they are going to ruin it—your script is too specific and too reliant on external control.

When multiple stakeholders are involved, the ultimate feeling you want to generate is consensus. You want to feel what it is like when resistance drops. Think about a time in the past when a group of people easily agreed on a plan with you. Remember the physical sensation of ease, the lack of defensiveness, and the feeling of shared momentum. 

Build your 369 scripts around recreating that exact physical and emotional memory. When you write your phrases, check in with your body. If your shoulders drop and you breathe easier, your script is hitting the right mark. If you feel tense or manipulative, rewrite it to focus on mutual benefit.

Navigating situations with bosses, clients, family members, or business partners requires strategy in the physical world and alignment in your internal world. By adjusting your scripts to account for the shared dynamic rather than demanding specific actions from others, you clear the path for real solutions to land.
