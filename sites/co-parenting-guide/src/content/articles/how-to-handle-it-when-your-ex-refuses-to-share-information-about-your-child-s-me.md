When my ex-husband stopped texting me the specifics about our son’s daily ADHD medication, I panicked. Leo was eight at the time, adjusting to a new extended-release stimulant, and timing was everything. Get it wrong, and he’d either be bouncing off the walls during my dinner shift or struggling to focus through homework the next morning. When I texted my ex for the exact milligram breakdown and administration time, I got back a vague text saying, "Don't worry about it, I've got it handled at my house." 

If you are dealing with a co-parent who treats vital medical information like state secrets, you already know the sinking feeling in your stomach. It is terrifying to feel locked out of your own child's healthcare. But freezing up or getting into a text-message shouting match won't get you the dosage numbers. Here is the practical playbook I used to take the emotion out of the conflict, protect my son's health, and build a reliable system that my ex couldn't easily bypass.

## Go Straight to the Source

The biggest mistake I made in the first week was treating my ex as the only gatekeeper of this information. The truth is, your ex is just the middleman. Cut them out. 

Call your pediatrician’s office or the prescribing physician directly. Tell the medical receptionist the truth: "My co-parent and I share custody, and I need to ensure continuity of care between our two households. Can you print out a complete medication schedule, including the exact brand name, milligram dosage, and timing instructions?" 

Under federal HIPAA rules in the United States, unless a specific court order strips you of medical decision-making rights, you have every legal right to your child's medical records. Most doctors' offices will happily email you a PDF summary or portal invite within an hour. Once you have it, you no longer *need* your ex to tell you the dosage. You already have it in writing from a medical professional.

## Build a Paper Trail (Without Taking the Bait)

When your ex refuses to share information, they are often trying to provoke an emotional reaction so they can point to you as the "difficult" parent. Do not give them that satisfaction. Keep all communication strictly focused on the child's well-being and strictly in writing. 

If your ex refuses to answer a direct question about medication, send a follow-up email that looks like this: 

*"Just to confirm, Leo is currently taking 10mg of Adderall XR at 7:30 AM daily, correct? I want to make sure I am keeping his routine consistent here on Tuesday through Thursday. If I don't hear back by 5:00 PM, I will assume this is correct and administer his morning dose accordingly."*

This approach does three things. First, it documents their refusal to cooperate. Second, it shows a judge or mediator that you are acting reasonably and trying to solve a logistical problem. Third, it puts the burden back on them to correct you if your assumption is wrong. 

## Involve the Pharmacy

Picking up prescriptions is another place where co-parents often weaponize information. If your ex refuses to tell you which pharmacy holds the script or when refills are due, bypass them by consolidating your child's prescriptions into a single, national pharmacy chain. 

Call that chain's customer service line or visit your local branch with your photo ID and custody paperwork. Ask them to link your profile to your child's account. Once this is done, you can download the pharmacy's mobile app. Every time a prescription is filled, refilled, or changed, you will get an immediate push notification on your phone showing the exact drug name, dosage, and prescribing doctor. You won't need to ask your ex a single question; your phone will tell you.

## Update Your Parenting Plan for Next Time

Once the immediate crisis has passed and your child's medication is managed safely, look at the big picture. Informal agreements fail because they rely on good behavior. You need a legally binding structure. 

The next time you are in mediation or modifying your custody agreement, make sure your attorney inserts a specific "Healthcare Communication Clause." A weak clause just says "parents will share medical info." A strong, enforceable clause specifies:
* Both parents must use a co-parenting app (like OurFamilyWizard or AppClose) for all medical logs.
* Any medication changes must be communicated in writing within 24 hours of administration.
* Both parents must have direct access to the child's insurance cards, pharmacy accounts, and primary care portal.

Handling a co-parent who withholds medical information is exhausting, but shifting from a reactive mindset to a systemic one takes their power away. Taking these concrete steps now will give you peace of mind and keep your child safe between households.
