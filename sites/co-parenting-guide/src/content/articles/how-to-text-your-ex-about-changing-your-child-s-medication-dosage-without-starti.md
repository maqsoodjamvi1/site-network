When my son's pediatrician recommended bumping up his ADHD medication dosage, my stomach dropped. Not because I doubted the doctor, but because I knew what was coming next: a text message to my ex-husband that had a fifty-fifty chance of triggering a three-day argument. 

In our early days of co-parenting, a text like this would have gone sideways fast. I would have sent something defensive, he would have replied with suspicion, and suddenly we’d be arguing about insurance coverage and whether the doctor was even competent, completely losing sight of our kid. 

Over the years, through plenty of trial and error, I figured out a formula. Texting an ex about sensitive medical adjustments doesn't have to ruin your week. Here is the exact approach I use now to share medication changes without starting a fight.

## Strip Out the Emotions and Lead With the Authority

When you are nervous, it is tempting to over-explain. You might write a paragraph about how tired your child has been at school, how much you agonized over this decision, and how the doctor *really* emphasized that this was necessary. 

Don't do that. When you over-explain, you sound defensive. And when you sound defensive, your co-parent immediately looks for holes in your argument. 

Instead, lean entirely on the doctor’s authority. You are not asking your ex for permission to change the dosage; you are delivering an update from a medical professional. Keep it clinical and brief. 

**Instead of:** "Hey, I've noticed Leo is really struggling to focus lately and his teacher emailed me twice this week. I talked to Dr. Smith and she thinks we need to try a higher dose of his meds starting Monday."

**Try:** "Hi Mark. Dr. Smith reviewed Leo’s progress and recommended increasing his morning stimulant dose from 10mg to 15mg starting tomorrow. I’ve updated the pharmacy profile."

## Send the Pharmacy and Logistics Info Upfront

Half the time, a co-parent's resistance to a medical change isn't actually about the medicine—it’s about the logistical headache. They are immediately wondering: *Who is paying for this? Who is picking it up? Do I need to give them the new dose on my custody days?*

If you answer these questions before they can ask them, you remove 80% of the friction. 

Before you hit send on that text, have the logistics sorted out in your head. Know which parent has custody when the change starts, and make sure the pharmacy details are locked down. 

Here is a checklist of what to include in the initial message or the immediate follow-up text:
* The exact start date for the new dosage
* Which parent is responsible for picking up the new prescription if refills are needed
* A photo of the updated prescription label or the doctor’s written instructions (crucial if your ex handles medication drop-offs at school)
* Confirmation that the insurance copay has already been handled

## Use a 'Read-Only' Closing

The biggest mistake people make is ending a logistical text with an open-ended question like, "Let me know what you think," or "Are you okay with this?" 

If you ask for their opinion on a clinical decision, you are inviting a debate you don't need to have. If your ex has legitimate legal custody concerns, they can bring them up, but you don't need to prompt a negotiation over a doctor's orders. 

Instead, use a closing that acknowledges receipt of the information without requiring a debate. A simple "Just wanted to keep you in the loop so we're on the same page for drop-offs" works wonders. 

If your ex replies with anger, anxiety, or questions about the doctor's competence, do not take the bait. Resist the urge to argue back via text. If they push back hard, pivot immediately to a phone call or suggest they reach out directly to the pediatrician's office with their concerns. You are responsible for sharing medical updates, but you are not responsible for managing their emotional reaction to them.

Navigating medical updates with an ex is tough, but getting the communication right makes a massive difference in keeping your child's routine stable across two households.
