When my son came back from a weekend visit with his dad without his nebulizer, my stomach completely dropped. He had severe asthma, and that machine wasn't an extra toy or a spare jacket; it was a prescribed medical device we needed every single day. When I texted my ex to ask for it back, I got hit with a wall of silence, followed later by an excuse about how he needed it at his house, too. 

If you are dealing with an ex who is withholding your child's medical equipment—whether it is an inhaler, insulin supplies, an ankle brace, or sensory tools—the panic is real. It feels deeply violating, and worse, it puts your child's health directly in the middle of a power struggle. 

Here is the exact practical playbook I wish I had that afternoon, broken down step-by-step to help you get the equipment back safely and legally.

## Document Everything and Put It in Writing Immediately

Do not call your ex on the phone to hash this out. When you are dealing with someone who is weaponizing a child's medical gear, you need a paper trail from minute one. 

Send a calm, text message or email immediately. Do not take the bait if they try to pull you into an argument about past grievances or child support. Keep it entirely focused on the medical need. 

Say something specific: *"Tom, Leo’s prescribed nebulizer and albuterol chamber were not in his backpack when he returned from your house today. He needs this equipment for his twice-daily breathing treatments. Please let me know if I can pick it up by 6:00 PM tonight, or if you prefer to drop it off on my front porch."*

If they do not respond within a few hours, follow up once. This creates clear evidence for a judge or mediator later that you attempted to resolve the issue reasonably and directly.

## Check Your Custody Agreement for Health Clauses

Pull out your parenting plan or custody order and look past the holiday schedules. You are hunting for the sections detailing medical decision-making and routine care. 

Most standard US custody agreements include a clause stating that both parents must ensure the child has access to necessary medication and medical devices during their respective parenting time. Some states even have explicit laws regarding interference with medical care. 

If your agreement specifies that one parent is the primary medical decision-maker, or if the order lists specific medical needs, highlight those sentences. If you have to involve your attorney or file a motion for clarification, pointing directly to a violated clause in your current order speeds up the process significantly.

## Call Your Pediatrician's Office for an Emergency Workaround

While you are fighting the logistics of getting the original equipment back, your child still needs care. Call your pediatrician or prescribing specialist right away. 

Explain the situation plainly to the front desk or nurse line: *"My co-parent has withheld my child’s medical device, and I need a temporary workaround for the next few days while we resolve it."*

Doctors deal with high-conflict family dynamics more often than you might think. They can frequently do the following:
*   Call in a duplicate prescription to your local pharmacy so you have an immediate replacement.
*   Provide a sample device if it is something like a specialized inhaler or monitor.
*   Write an official note documenting that the child requires continuous access to the device, which you can use as evidence of medical neglect or interference.

Insurance companies can sometimes be tricky about replacing equipment early, but explaining that the device was withheld by the other parent—and mentioning that you are filing a police report or contacting your lawyer—can sometimes help bypass the waiting period.

## Involve Local Authorities Only If There is Imminent Danger

Can you call the police if your ex keeps your child's medical equipment? The answer is frustratingly nuanced. 

If the item is a life-saving device—such as an EpiPen for a severe allergy, a blood glucose monitor for Type 1 diabetes, or an oxygen tank—this crosses the line from a custody dispute into potential child endangerment or medical neglect. In this scenario, call your local non-emergency police line (or 911 if your child is actively in respiratory or medical distress). Ask for a civil standby to retrieve the medical equipment from the other parent's home, or ask officers to accompany you.

However, if the equipment is important for daily management but not immediately life-threatening (like physical therapy equipment or standard nebulizers), local police will often view it as a civil matter and refuse to get involved. If that happens, do not waste energy arguing with the responding officer. Get their badge number, ask them to note the call, and use that documentation for your next legal step.

## File an Emergency Motion If Necessary

If your ex is using your child's health as a control tactic, sending polite texts won't work forever. You may need to have your lawyer file an expedited or emergency motion for temporary orders regarding medical access. 

Judges have zero patience for parents who mess with a child's health. When a judge sees that one parent is deliberately depriving a child of a prescribed medical device, it tends to backfire heavily on that parent. It damages their credibility across the entire custody case. 

Navigating high-conflict co-parenting situations where your child's well-being is treated as leverage requires a balance of strict documentation, reliance on medical professionals, and knowing when to escalate through official legal channels. Taking these structured steps helps take the emotion out of a terrifying situation and puts the focus back on keeping your child safe and healthy.
