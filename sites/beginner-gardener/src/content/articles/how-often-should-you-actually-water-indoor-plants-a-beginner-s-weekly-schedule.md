If you ask ten different houseplant owners how often they water their indoor plants, you will likely get ten different answers. Some swear by watering every Sunday morning, while others wait until their plants are practically begging for mercy. When I first started collecting houseplants years ago, I fell into the trap of following a strict calendar schedule. I poured a cup of water into every single pot every seven days, and within a few months, I had drowned a prized pothos and completely dehydrated a snake plant. 

The hard truth is that calendar schedules kill indoor plants. Homes have wildly different humidity levels, temperatures, and light exposures depending on the season and the room. Instead of a rigid routine, you need a reliable method to check your plants and a flexible weekly rhythm for assessing them. 

## Why Calendar Watering Fails

Plants do not drink on a human schedule. A Monstera sitting in a sun-drenched south-facing window in July is going to dry out at lightning speed. That exact same Monstera sitting in a dim corner in January might go three weeks without needing a drop. 

When you water on a fixed day every week regardless of soil moisture, you suffocate the roots. Roots need oxygen just as much as they need water. If the soil is still wet from the previous week and you add more moisture, the air pockets in the dirt fill up permanently. This leads to root rot, a fungal disaster that turns healthy roots into mush and usually spells the end for the plant.

## The Simple Finger Test

Before you even think about picking up a watering can, you need to use the oldest, most reliable tool in horticulture: your index finger. 

Push your finger into the soil up to the second knuckle—about one to two inches deep. Pay attention to what you feel:

*   **Wet and cool:** Put the watering can down. The soil retains plenty of moisture, and your plant is fine for now.
*   **Damp and room temperature:** Wait a few more days. The surface might look dry, but deep down, the roots are happy.
*   **Dry and warm:** It is time to water. 

For thick-stemmed succulents and snake plants, let the soil dry out completely all the way to the bottom of the pot before you water again. For tropical foliage like ferns, calatheas, and peace lilies, you usually want to water when the top inch or two feels dry, but before the soil completely dries out.

## A Practical Weekly Checking Rhythm

While you should not water on a schedule, you *can* schedule a weekly check-in. This keeps you connected to your indoor garden without putting plants at risk of overwatering. I recommend setting aside ten minutes every Saturday or Sunday morning for a quick inspection. 

Walk through your home with your watering can, but only fill it up after you have checked the soil of every plant. Here is how to break down your weekly routine:

*   **The Look-Over:** Scan your plants for drooping leaves, yellowing lower foliage, or dusty leaves that block sunlight.
*   **The Weight Test:** Lift up smaller nursery pots. Dry soil is remarkably light, while wet soil feels heavy. Once you get used to the weight of your pots, you can tell if a plant needs water without even touching the dirt.
*   **The Finger Check:** Spot-check plants that look slightly dull or whose pots feel light. 
*   **The Soak:** Only water the specific pots that pass the dryness test, making sure to soak them thoroughly until water runs out of the drainage holes.

## The Importance of Drainage

None of this advice works if your pots do not have drainage holes. If water has nowhere to go, it pools at the bottom of the container, creating a swampy environment that rots roots from the bottom up. 

If you have a decorative ceramic pot without a hole, keep your plant in a plastic nursery pot with slots at the bottom, and simply lift that inner pot out when it is time to water. Let it drain completely in the sink for fifteen minutes before placing it back in its decorative container. 

Mastering indoor plant care is less about memorizing strict rules and more about learning to read the subtle signals your plants give you. By swapping a blind weekly watering routine for a weekly check-in habit, you will start to understand the natural rhythm of your indoor garden and keep your greenery thriving year-round. Take a look at your collection today, pick a designated day for your weekly soil check, and see which of your plants are actually thirsty before you water again.
