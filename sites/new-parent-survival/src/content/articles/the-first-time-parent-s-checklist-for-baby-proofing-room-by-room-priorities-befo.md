When my oldest child started army-crawling across our living room rug at six months old, I felt a sudden jolt of panic. Up until that afternoon, baby-proofing felt like a distant chore on a long to-do list. The reality hits fast once your baby stops staying put and starts investigating every baseboard, cord, and low drawer within reach. 

If you are staring down this milestone and wondering where to start without spending a fortune or turning your house into a padded cell, take a deep breath. You do not need to tackle every room at once. Here is the room-by-room checklist I wish I had, focusing strictly on the hazards that matter most before your baby becomes mobile.

## Living Room and Family Room: Cords, Corners, and Tip-Overs

This is usually where babies spend most of their waking hours, making it the top priority. Your main goal here is securing heavy furniture and managing the modern maze of electronics and window treatments.

*   **Anchor televisions and heavy bookcases:** Dressers, bookshelves, and flat-screen TVs are top-heavy and tempting for a toddler to pull up on. Bolt these to wall studs using heavy-duty furniture straps. Do not rely on drywall anchors alone.
*   **Tackle blind cords:** Dangling cords from window blinds are a severe strangulation hazard. Install cord cleats high up on the wall or use wrap-around wind-ups to keep loops completely out of reach.
*   **Manage power strips and charging cables:** Instead of buying dozens of individual outlet caps (which can be a choking hazard themselves), swap your wall plates for sliding-cover safety plates. For power strips, put them inside a cord-management box behind the TV console so tiny fingers cannot tug on plugged-in cords.
*   **Soften sharp edges:** You do not need foam on every single surface, but a coffee table with a sharp glass or wood corner right at a crawling baby's eye level deserves temporary corner guards.

## The Kitchen: Low Cabinets and the Danger Zone

The kitchen is a sensory wonderland for a curious baby, filled with shiny pots, heavy cans, and dangerous cleaning supplies. 

Start by looking at the kitchen from your baby's literal viewpoint. Get down on your hands and knees. What do you see? If you see lower cabinets, you need latches. However, you do not need to lock every single cabinet. Pick one lower cabinet to fill with safe, unbreakable items—like plastic storage containers and wooden spoons—and let your baby raid that specific spot. Keep the rest locked.

Move all cleaning supplies, dishwasher detergent pods, and dish soaps to upper cabinets. If you must keep them under the sink, invest in a heavy-duty sliding lock. Also, remember to turn pot handles inward every time you cook, and consider a stove knob guard if your range controls are on the front.

## The Bathroom: Water Safety and Medicine Cabinets

Bathrooms are compact, but they pack a high concentration of hazards. Beyond the obvious medicine cabinet, water is the primary risk here.

*   **Install a toilet lock:** Babies have top-heavy proportions, meaning they can easily lean over into a toilet bowl, lose their balance, and drown in just a few inches of water. A simple strap lock on the toilet lid takes ten seconds to install and provides immense peace of mind.
*   **Lock up personal care items:** Mouthwash, razors, bar soap, and makeup removers look harmless to a toddler, but many contain alcohol or toxic ingredients. Move everything off the edge of the tub and out of low vanities.
*   **Set your water heater:** Ensure your home's water heater is set to a maximum of 120 degrees Fahrenheit to prevent accidental scalding while your child learns how to turn on the faucet handles.

## Hallways and Bedrooms: Doors, Gates, and Sleep Spaces

As your baby starts exploring outside the main living areas, you need to manage boundary lines and sleep environments.

Install safety gates at the top and bottom of any stairs immediately. For the top of the stairs, always use a hardware-mounted gate screwed directly into the wall studs. Pressure-mounted gates can slip if a child leans hard against them at the top of a drop-off. 

In the nursery or your bedroom, keep cribs completely clear of hanging mobiles once the baby can sit up, and ensure any baby monitor cords are at least three feet away from the crib rails. Check that bedroom doors do not have heavy slamming potential or pinch hazards on the hinges.

Baby proofing does not mean you have to dismantle your home or live in total sterile gray plastic. By focusing on heavy furniture stability, cord management, and locking away true poisons, you create a safe perimeter where your baby can explore freely and build confidence. As your child grows into a toddler, your priorities will shift from low-level cabinet locks to door knob covers and window guards, but tackling these foundational steps now will get you through the crawling stage safely.

When you are ready to put this checklist into action, the next step is taking a quick walk through your own home with a notepad to catalog your specific furniture layout and hardware needs.
