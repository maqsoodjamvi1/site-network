If you brought your baby home from the hospital expecting a predictable rhythm, hitting the five-week mark probably felt like running into a brick wall. Around this age, babies who were previously sleeping in decent stretches suddenly wake up constantly, fight naps, and cry for hours in the evening. 

As a parent who has lived through the absolute fog of the five-week mark, I want to share what actually works when you are trying to build a basic routine while navigating a sudden sleep regression. 

## What the Five-Week Sleep Shift Actually Looks Like

Let's clear up a common point of confusion. Technically, pediatric sleep consultants often talk about the four-month regression. However, babies go through a massive developmental leap and growth spurt right around five weeks old. Their world is expanding, their vision is sharpening, and their little nervous systems are waking up to the environment around them.

In my house, this looked like a baby who suddenly refused to nap anywhere except directly on my chest. Night sleep, which had featured a blissful four-hour stretch, fractured into chaotic two-hour blocks. If you are experiencing this, you aren't doing anything wrong. Your baby's brain is working overtime, which paradoxically makes it much harder for them to actually fall asleep.

## Forget the Clock and Focus on Wake Windows

When you are desperate for structure, it is tempting to try putting your five-week-old on a rigid clock-based schedule (like napping at 9:00 AM and 12:00 PM). Throw that idea out the window right now. At five weeks old, internal body clocks are not fully developed. 

Instead of chasing the clock, build your daily rhythm around wake windows. At this age, most babies can only handle being awake for 45 to 60 minutes total. That timeframe includes feeding, burping, a quick diaper change, and getting them back into a sleep sack. 

If you miss that tiny window and let your baby stay awake for 80 minutes, their body floods with cortisol and adrenaline. That is the exact moment an easy nap turns into a screaming marathon. Watch for early tired cues—red eyebrows, staring off into space, or slow yawns—and start your wind-down routine immediately when you spot them.

## Master the Art of the "Reset" Nap

During a regression, daytime sleep often falls apart completely. Catnaps that last only 15 or 20 minutes become the norm, leading to an overtired baby by late afternoon. 

When my second child hit this phase, I stopped stressing about getting her to sleep in her bassinet for every single nap. To survive the day, I relied on what I call the contact-and-extend method. I would let her fall asleep on me for the first 20 minutes of a nap—using the warmth of my body to help her cycle past that initial light-sleep disturbance—and then attempt to transfer her, or simply accept the contact nap so she at least got some restorative rest. 

*   Keep the room dim and turn on a loud white noise machine, even for daytime naps.
*   Swaddle your baby securely before they get fussy, keeping their arms snug if they haven't shown signs of rolling yet.
*   Do not worry about "bad habits." At five weeks old, you cannot spoil a baby by holding them to help them sleep.

## Managing the Witching Hour and Bedtime Routine

The evening hours at five weeks old can test the patience of a saint. This is peak fussiness time, often accompanied by cluster feeding where your baby wants to eat every 45 minutes from 6:00 PM to 10:00 PM. 

Instead of fighting this, lean into it. Dim the lights in your living room around 6:00 PM, turn down the volume on the television, and park yourself on the couch with a giant glass of water and your favorite snacks. Expect to spend a couple of hours nursing or bottle-feeding on demand. 

You don't need a complex bedtime routine at this age. Keep it simple and sensory-based: a fresh diaper, a clean pair of sleepers, a final feeding in a dark room, and a gentle song. The goal isn't to put them down fast; the goal is to signal to their nervous system that the long stretch of night is approaching.

Navigating a sleep regression while trying to piece together a functional newborn schedule is an exercise in radical flexibility. Some days will go smoothly, and other days your carefully planned wake windows will implode by noon. Focus on keeping your baby fed, watching those short wake windows closely, and giving yourself grace during the difficult hours. As you look toward establishing more predictable patterns in the coming weeks, having the right tools and products on hand can make a noticeable difference in your daily routine.
