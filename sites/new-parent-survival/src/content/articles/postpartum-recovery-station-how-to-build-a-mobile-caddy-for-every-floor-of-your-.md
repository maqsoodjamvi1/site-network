When I came home from the hospital with my first baby, I quickly realized a brutal truth about postpartum recovery: the furthest room in the house is always where you left the burp cloths, the nipple cream, or your phone charger. Trying to navigate stairs while bleeding, leaking, cramping, and operating on two hours of sleep is an Olympic sport no one signs up for. 

That is why I abandoned the traditional nursery-only supply model on day three. Instead, I built mobile postpartum recovery stations using simple plastic rolling caddies. By keeping a fully stocked cart on every floor of my house, I stopped burning precious physical energy running back and forth for basics. Here is the exact blueprint I used to build these lifesavers, so you can set yours up before baby arrives.

## Why a Multi-Floor Mobile Caddy Beats a Stationary Basket

Standard nursery baskets are great, but they are heavy to lug around and trap you in one spot. A rolling utility cart with wheels changes everything. 

If you live in a split-level home, a townhouse, or a two-story house with your bedroom upstairs and the kitchen downstairs, carrying a basket up and down the steps while holding a newborn is an unnecessary fall hazard. By purchasing two inexpensive three-tier rolling carts, you can station one upstairs and one downstairs. You simply wheel your supplies from the living room to the kitchen island, or from the bedside to the master bathroom, without ever having to lift a heavy bin.

## The Bottom Tier: Your Personal Body Recovery Kit

The bottom shelf of your caddy should be entirely dedicated to your healing body. During the first two weeks postpartum, you will not care about cute baby outfits; you will care about pain management and bodily fluids. 

Stock this tier with your bathroom overflow items so you do not have to hobble to the linen closet. Include a generous supply of heavy-duty overnight pads, disposable postpartum underwear (buy a pack a size larger than you think you need), and a peri bottle if you prefer to keep a spare downstairs. Add a travel-sized pack of flushable or ultra-soft bamboo wipes, a tube of lanolin or organic nipple butter, and a large tumbler with a straw for ice water. I also kept a small heating pad down here for severe after-pains and uterine cramping, which tend to flare up during nursing sessions.

## The Middle Tier: Feeding and Pumping Gear

Whether you are nursing, pumping, or formula feeding, the middle tier is your feeding command center. You want everything within arm's reach so you are not stranded on the couch with a screaming, hungry infant while your supplies are out of reach.

On this shelf, place a burp cloth rolled up in every available corner, a box of nursing pads, and a small snack stash. Keep shelf-stable, high-protein snacks here—think beef jerky, nut butter packets, and granola bars—because breastfeeding drains your energy instantly and triggers intense hunger. If you are pumping, place your pump parts, a pack of sanitizing pump wipes, and a dedicated hand-sanitizer pump right on this tier. 

## The Top Tier: Baby Maintenance and Sanity Savers

The top tier is reserved for quick-access items you need to touch with one hand while holding a baby with the other. 

Fill a small plastic bin or divider tray with newborn diapers, a travel pack of sensitive-skin wipes, and a tube of diaper rash ointment. Add a digital forehead thermometer, a bulb syringe, and a pacifier clipped to the side of the cart. Finally, put your sanity savers here: a pair of wireless headphones, a long phone charging cable plugged into a wall brick, and lip balm. When you are trapped under a sleeping baby for a two-hour contact nap, having your phone charger and headphones right next to you prevents absolute boredom.

## How to Customize Your Station for Your Specific Delivery

No two postpartum experiences are identical, and your caddy should reflect how you actually gave birth. 

* If you had a Cesarean section, place your abdominal binder, extra large waist-high underwear that won't rub your incision, and prescribed pain medication caddy securely on the top shelf so you never have to bend down low to retrieve them.
* If you are formula feeding, swap the nursing pads for a pre-measured formula dispenser and a small thermos of warm water, saving you midnight kitchen trips.
* If you have older children or pets in the house, keep any small choking hazards or medicated creams in lidded plastic storage boxes inside the caddy tiers so curious hands cannot reach them.

Setting up these rolling stations takes less than an hour and costs very little, but the physical and mental relief they provide during the fourth trimester is priceless. 

As you finish gathering your supplies and prepping your home for your baby's arrival, think about the layout of your daily routine and where you spend the most stationary hours. Taking thirty minutes today to wheel these stations into place will save you dozens of painful trips up and down the stairs next week.
