If your newborn was finally starting to settle into a predictable rhythm, only to suddenly wake up every hour screaming for milk as if they haven't eaten in days, you are likely in the thick of the four-week growth spurt. 

When my first baby hit this milestone, I felt completely blindsized. One night she was sleeping in neat three-hour blocks, and the next, she was cluster feeding from dusk until dawn and treating her bassinet like it was lined with tacks. 

What makes this phase particularly exhausting is that it often coincides with your first real wave of postpartum exhaustion. You are running on empty, and the temptation to completely scrap your routines—or lack thereof—is massive. However, surviving this blip without losing your sanity comes down to managing the chaos while protecting the basic structures of your day. Here is how I learned to navigate the four-week growth spurt without completely derailing our household routine.

## Lean Into Cluster Feeding Without Abandoning Day-Night Separation

The hallmark of the four-week growth spurt is intense cluster feeding. Your baby’s stomach is expanding rapidly, and they need to signal your milk supply to increase by nursing or bottle-feeding almost constantly. For three or four days, it can feel like you are chained to the couch. 

During my second child's four-week spurt, I learned not to fight the cluster feeding. Trying to put a hungry, growing baby on a strict schedule at this age is a losing battle. Instead, I set up a "feeding station" on the living room couch with a massive water bottle, healthy snacks, a phone charger, and a good television series. 

Crucially, even though your baby will want to nurse or bottle-feed around the clock, do your best to maintain the distinction between day and night. Keep daytime feedings in a bright room with normal household noise. At night, keep lights dim, keep interactions quiet and boring, and put your baby right back down after burping. This preserves their developing circadian rhythm so that sleep returns to normal faster once the growth spurt passes.

## Keep Your Daytime Routine Intact for Your Own Mental Health

When you have been awake since 2:00 AM nursing a frantic infant, the absolute last thing you want to do is get dressed and leave the house. But keeping a loose daytime routine saved my mental health during those foggy weeks.

I committed to one non-negotiable daily anchor: getting outside for a morning walk, even if it was just 15 minutes around the block in my pajamas under a coat. 

*   Wake up around the same general time each morning (even if the night was terrible).
*   Open all the blinds in the house immediately to signal daytime.
*   Get dressed in actual clothes, even if those clothes are just soft joggers.
*   Eat a real breakfast before noon.

These tiny anchors stopped the days from bleeding together into an endless, dark loop of exhaustion. If your baby is fussy and won't nap independently during the day, pop them into a baby wrap or carrier. You can still wash dishes, fold laundry, or walk the dog while they nap upright against your chest, keeping your household functioning while your baby goes through their developmental leap.

## Adjust Your Sleep Expectations, Not Your Bedtime Habits

One of the biggest mistakes I made with my first baby was trying to sleep-train or fix a problem that was actually just biological development. At four weeks old, babies cannot self-soothe, and sleep regressions or disruptions are fueled by pure physical hunger and neurological growth. 

Instead of trying to force independent sleep during this week, lower your bar for household chores and shift your own bedtime earlier. If your baby is waking every 90 minutes, you need to be asleep by 9:00 PM to capture enough cumulative rest to function. 

Maintain your normal wind-down routine for the evening—such as a warm bath (or wipe-down), dimming the lights, and putting on a sleep sack—even if the night ends up being rocky. Consistency matters for your own habits, even if your baby seems completely unfazed by your best bedtime efforts right now.

## Protect the Primary Caregiver’s Sleep in Shifts

You cannot white-knuckle your way through a week of broken sleep without help. If you have a partner, family member, or friend staying with you, divide the night into concrete shifts rather than both of you waking up for every cry.

My husband and I used a split-shift approach during the peak of the growth spurt. I slept from 9:00 PM to 2:00 AM uninterrupted in the guest room while he handled the late-night cluster feeding using expressed milk or formula. Then, we swapped. Knowing I had a guaranteed five-hour block of continuous sleep made the fragmented second half of the night manageable. 

Remember that this intense phase is remarkably short-lived. Usually, within four to seven days, the intense hunger subsides, the frequent waking settles down, and your baby emerges slightly longer, a bit heavier, and ready to resume a more predictable daily pattern. 

As you navigate this messy transition and look ahead to establishing more predictable rhythms over the coming weeks, having the right strategies and support systems in place can make all the difference in keeping your household running smoothly.
