If your six-week-old baby was sleeping in decent stretches last week and is suddenly waking every hour, crying during feeds, and refusing to nap, welcome to the notorious six-week growth spurt. When I went through this with my second child, I thought I had broken all the good sleep habits we were barely starting to build. 

The good news is that you did not do anything wrong. This phase is intense, loud, and exhausting, but it is also biologically temporary. Here is how you can survive the disruption without completely destroying your sanity or your daily routine.

## What is Actually Happening at Six Weeks

Around week six, babies undergo a massive developmental leap. Physically, their metabolisms speed up because they are growing rapidly in both length and brain capacity. You might notice they look a little longer in their footie pajamas or are suddenly more alert when awake. 

This internal shift triggers cluster feeding. Your baby might want to nurse or take a bottle every 45 minutes all evening long. This constant eating fuels the growth, but it also disrupts their tiny circadian rhythms, making deep sleep much harder to achieve. 

## Protecting Your Core Routine

When sleep falls apart, the temptation is to throw all structure out the window and resort to desperate measures—like driving around the neighborhood at 3:00 AM or letting the baby sleep exclusively on your chest all day. While you might need to bend the rules for a night or two, try to keep the backbone of your daily routine intact.

*   **Keep wake windows tight:** At six weeks, your baby can only handle about 45 to 60 minutes of awake time before becoming overtired. Do not push them to stay awake longer just because they slept poorly.
*   **Maintain daytime light and night darkness:** Open the blinds first thing in the morning to signal day, and keep nighttime feedings dim and quiet to signal night.
*   **Stick to your bedtime sequence:** Even if bedtime falls apart into a marathon nursing session, keep doing the bath (or wipe-down), lotion, and swaddle in the same order every night. 

## Managing the Exhaustion Without Unraveling

Survival during this week requires lowering your household standards significantly. When my house looked like a tornado had hit it and I was running on three hours of fractured sleep, I learned a few practical tactics to keep my head above water.

First, lean hard into the "five-minute shift" with a partner if you have one. Instead of waiting for a solid two-hour block of sleep that isn't coming, trade off in strict shifts so both of you can get at least one unbroken stretch of three or four hours. 

Second, let go of nap-time pressure. If your baby refuses to sleep in their bassinet during the day, do not spend 40 minutes fighting them for a 20-minute nap. Pop them into a baby carrier or hold them while you sit on the couch and watch a show. Getting them the rest they need matters far more right now than where that sleep happens.

## When to Expect Relief

The hardest part about the six-week growth spurt is that it often coincides with the peak of newborn fussiness and gas development. It feels like it lasts forever, but physiologically, cluster feeding and extreme sleep disruption from this specific growth spurt usually peak over four to seven days. 

Once your milk supply fully adjusts to your baby’s increased demand, or once the formula-fed baby settles into their new intake volume, sleep typically begins to lengthen again. You will know you are on the other side when your baby can go two or three hours between daytime feeds without melting down.

Navigating this phase without burning your routine to the ground comes down to patience and realistic expectations. As you look for ways to smooth out your baby's sleep habits and regain your own rest over the coming weeks, it helps to have a reliable framework to guide your next steps.
