Discovering that your in-laws have created a fake Instagram account or a burner Facebook profile just to monitor your teenager is a special kind of modern family nightmare. It crosses a major privacy boundary, bypasses your parenting rules, and drops a heavy dose of awkwardness right into your extended family dynamic. 

When I first found out my mother-in-law was operating a secondary account to friend our fifteen-year-old—sidestepping the block list we had placed on her main account—my initial reaction was blind fury. But blowing up the family group chat rarely fixes the root issue. You need a calm, tactical approach that protects your teenager’s digital boundaries while managing the inevitable family fallout.

Here is the step-by-step game plan I used to handle this exact situation, keeping my teenager’s trust intact without starting an all-out war.

## Step 1: Verify the Facts and Talk to Your Teen First

Before you call anyone out, gather your evidence quietly. Take screenshots of the fake profile, note the mutual connections, and confirm it is actually your relative behind the screen. Sometimes kids are dramatic, but sometimes burner accounts are surprisingly obvious—using a family pet's name paired with a distinct hometown or a blurry photo that looks suspiciously like a wedding picture from ten years ago.

Sit down with your teenager and look at the account together. Acknowledge that this feels invasive and frustrating. 

Ask your teen how they want to handle it from their end. Giving them a voice in this process is crucial because it teaches them digital self-defense. Most teens will say they just want the account blocked and gone. Validate their annoyance, and assure them that you have their back. 

## Step 2: Lock Down Your Teen’s Privacy Settings Together

Do not just block the fake profile and move on. If a family member is motivated enough to create a burner account, they will likely try again under a different name. 

Take thirty minutes to review your teen’s social media privacy settings across every platform they use. Switch public profiles to private. Explain to your teen why accepting friend or follower requests from strangers—or accounts with zero mutual friends besides themselves—is a bad idea. 

Turn on two-factor authentication and talk about the concept of digital hygiene. Frame this not as a punishment or a restriction on their freedom, but as a standard security measure. If your in-laws are willing to catfishing-adjacent behaviors to see your kid's TikTok videos, your teenager needs to learn how to spot and block suspicious accounts independently.

## Step 3: Choose Who Makes the Call

Decide whether you or your spouse should confront the in-laws. As a general rule of thumb, the biological child of the relative should always make the phone call or send the message. If my mother-in-law crossed the line, my spouse was the one to address it. This prevents the narrative from shifting into "your spouse is turning you against my side of the family."

Keep the conversation direct, neutral, and devoid of emotional accusations. You do not need to debate whether your parenting style is too strict or why your teen deserves privacy. 

A script that works well sounds like this: "We noticed an anonymous account interacting with [Teen's Name]'s profile, and it traces back to you. Going around our rules and our blocked accounts to secretly monitor our kids destroys our trust. If you want a relationship with our family, you need to respect our boundaries as parents."

## Step 4: Establish Clear, Non-Negotiable Consequences

Boundaries without consequences are just suggestions. Decide in advance what happens if the behavior continues. 

If the in-laws apologize, delete the account, and respect the digital boundary moving forward, you can slowly rebuild that bridge. However, if they deny it despite clear evidence, play the victim, or create yet another profile a month later, the consequences need to escalate. 

In our case, we implemented a temporary break in in-person visits and a complete information blackout. When grandparents prove they cannot respect basic digital boundaries, they lose access to casual updates, sports schedules, and photo shares. Make sure your spouse is entirely on board with these boundaries so the extended family cannot divide and conquer.

Navigating invasive family dynamics requires a steady hand and a united front between you and your partner. Deciding how to manage these sticky situations often brings up broader questions about setting limits with extended family members who struggle to respect your parenting choices.
