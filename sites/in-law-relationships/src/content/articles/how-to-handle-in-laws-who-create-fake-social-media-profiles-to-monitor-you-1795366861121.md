Discovering that your in-laws have created a fake social media account to monitor your online life is a jarring experience. One day you notice a stranger with zero posts and a generic name viewing your Instagram stories, and the next day you realize the dog photo belongs to your mother-in-law’s neighbor's pet. 

When extended family crosses the line from garden-variety nosiness into calculated digital surveillance, it damages trust and forces you to take immediate action. Here is a practical, step-by-step guide on how to confirm your suspicions, protect your digital footprint, and handle the awkward fallout with your partner.

## Confirming Your Suspicions Without Starting a War

Before you confront anyone or change your entire online presence, you need to be reasonably sure who is behind the fake profile. Jumping to conclusions based on a hunch can backfire, leaving you looking paranoid if you are wrong. 

Look for specific tells that tie the burner account back to your spouse’s family. Fake profiles created by older relatives often have glaring weak spots. Check the follower list: does this zero-follower account exclusively follow you, your spouse, and maybe your sibling? Do they only like photos of your children, or view your stories within three minutes of posting, even at 2 AM? 

Cross-reference the account with people your in-laws might know. Sometimes, an in-law will slip up and mention a specific detail you only posted on your private story, giving the game away entirely. Gather two or three solid indicators before you move to the next phase.

## Locking Down Your Digital Boundaries

Once you have a strong sense of who is behind the profile, stop feeding the beast. You do not necessarily need to delete your social media accounts, but you do need to audit your privacy settings immediately. 

Go through your follower lists and remove anyone who is mutual friends with the suspect profile if you suspect they are acting as a fly on the wall. Move your accounts to private mode. More importantly, utilize platform-specific features to block the fake account entirely. 

When you block an aggressive or suspicious account on Instagram or Facebook, the platform gives you the option to block "new accounts they may create." Select that option. This proactive step ensures that if your mother-in-law makes a second or third burner profile using the same device or IP address, the platform can automatically intercept it before it ever reaches your page.

## Aligning With Your Spouse First

You cannot handle this situation effectively if you and your partner are not on the exact same page. This is the hardest part of the process because your spouse may initially default to denial. They might say, "My mom doesn't even know how to use Instagram like that," or "You're reading too much into this."

Sit down with your partner and show them the evidence objectively. Focus on the behavior rather than attacking your in-laws' character. Say something like, "Look at the timing of who views my stories compared to who actually follows me. This account was made the day after we announced we were moving." 

Ask your partner how they want to handle it as a team. Ideally, your partner should be the one to address their own parents. If your partner insists on sweeping it under the rug, you still have the absolute right to block the account and protect your peace of mind without their explicit permission.

## Deciding Whether to Confront or Ignore

You generally have two paths forward: the silent treatment (blocking and moving on) or a direct confrontation. 

* **The Silent Block:** Simply block the account and never mention it. For many families, this is the least stressful route. It sends a clear message without inviting a dramatic, tearful denial or a family feud. They will know you figured it out, but they won't have the satisfaction of a public argument.
* **The Direct Call-Out:** If the surveillance is part of a broader pattern of boundary-stushing, a calm, direct conversation led by your spouse can work. Your spouse can simply tell their parents, "We noticed a fake account monitoring our posts, and for our family's privacy, we are keeping our social media completely closed off to extended family going forward."

Choosing to handle in-law surveillance requires balancing your need for digital privacy with the reality of long-term family dynamics. By securing your accounts, getting your spouse on board, and deciding how much energy you want to give the situation, you can reclaim your online space.
