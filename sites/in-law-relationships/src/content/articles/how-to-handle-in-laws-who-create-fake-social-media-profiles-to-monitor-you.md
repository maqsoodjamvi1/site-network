Discovering that your mother-in-law or father-in-law has created a fake social media profile to spy on your locked-down accounts is a deeply unsettling experience. When you have already blocked their real accounts for your own peace of mind, finding a mysterious new follower with zero mutual friends and a suspiciously blank timeline feels like a direct violation of your home and your boundaries. 

Dealing with digital surveillance from extended family requires a steady hand. You want to address the behavior without sparking an explosive family feud, but you also need to make it abundantly clear that sneaking around your privacy settings will not be tolerated. Here is how I handled this exact situation in my own family, and the practical steps you can take today to lock down your digital life and address the breach.

## Step 1: Confirm Your Suspicions Without Reacting Immediately

Before you accuse anyone, you need to be reasonably sure who is behind the burner account. In-laws trying to be covert often leave a trail of digital breadcrumbs. Look at the profile picture—is it a generic stock photo of a landscape, or worse, a stolen photo of a distant family member or pet? Check who the account interacts with. Does this fake profile only "like" posts from your spouse, your siblings, or other extended family members? 

In my case, the fake profile was following fewer than twenty people, yet half of them were people I grew up with three states away—people my in-laws had never met. The smoking gun was when the account viewed my Instagram story within ninety seconds of it going live, despite me posting it late at night. 

Resist the urge to immediately send a hostile direct message or call them out in a group chat. If you react impulsively, they will likely deny it, play the victim, and accuse you of being paranoid. Gather your evidence quietly so you can approach the situation with absolute confidence.

## Step 2: Lock Down Your Privacy Settings

Once you spot the phantom profile, your immediate priority is defense, not offense. You need to make your digital footprint completely unpenetrable. Simply blocking the fake account is not enough, because a determined in-law will just create another one using a different email address the next day.

*   **Switch to strict approval modes:** Set your Instagram, Facebook, and other platforms to private. Go through your existing followers list and remove anyone you do not personally know or trust, even if they seem harmless.
*   **Audit your friends-of-friends settings:** Many platforms default to letting friends of your friends view your content. Change your settings so that only your direct, approved friends can see your posts, photos, and stories.
*   **Block by IP or phone number if necessary:** If your platform allows it, block new accounts created from the same device or associated with known phone numbers, though privacy settings are usually your best defense.
*   **Curb the flying monkeys:** Recognize that your in-laws might be viewing your life through a well-meaning relative's active account. Politely ask your spouse or trusted family members not to share screenshots, updates, or photos of your household with anyone who asks.

## Step 3: Decide How (or If) to Confront the Behavior

You have to weigh whether a confrontation is actually going to change their behavior or just make family holidays miserable. Sometimes, silent blocking is the best policy. Every time they make a new profile, you block it in silence. This starves them of the drama and confrontation they might secretly be looking for.

However, if the snooping is causing you significant stress or spilling over into your real-life interactions—such as your mother-in-law casually mentioning details from a locked post you shared last week—it is time to address it. 

I recommend having your spouse handle the conversation with their own parents. It keeps you out of the direct line of fire and presents a united front. Your partner can say something straightforward, such as, "We noticed someone using a fake account to view our private pages. We value our digital privacy, and it damages trust when boundaries are bypassed like that." You do not need to prove beyond a shadow of a doubt who made the account; the mere implication that you are wise to the game is often enough to make them back off.

## Moving Forward With Confidence

Dealing with boundary-stomping in-laws is exhausting, but setting firm digital boundaries is an essential part of protecting your mental health and your nuclear family. You have every right to curate who enters your digital space, regardless of their relation to you. 

As you take steps to secure your online presence and decide how to address the root of the issue with your partner, consider what your next move should be regarding your overall family communication boundaries.
