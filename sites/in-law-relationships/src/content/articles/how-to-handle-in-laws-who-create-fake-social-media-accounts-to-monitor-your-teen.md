Finding out that my mother-in-law had created a burner Instagram account just to watch our fourteen-year-old daughter’s stories was a turning point in our household. My daughter came to me, confused and slightly creeped out, asking why a stranger with a blank profile picture and zero posts was viewing her updates three minutes after she posted them. A quick check of the follower list and a little detective work later, the truth came out. 

If you are dealing with a similar situation, you are likely feeling a mix of anger, violation, and awkwardness. When family members cross digital boundaries this way, it introduces a unique kind of tension into family relationships. You want to protect your teenager's privacy, but you also want to avoid a family war that drags in your partner. 

Here is how I handled it, and the practical steps I recommend if you are trying to figure out how to address in-laws who create fake social media accounts to monitor your teens.

## Secure Your Teen's Accounts First

Before you have any difficult conversations with your in-laws, lock down your teenager's digital footprint. Panicking and yelling at Grandma won't stop her from creating *another* fake profile tomorrow under a different name. 

Sit down with your teen and go through their privacy settings together. Switch their accounts from public to private. Explain to your teen that going private means every single follower request has to be manually approved by them. 

Next, teach your teenager the "three-second rule" for accepting followers: if you do not know the person in real life, do not accept the request. If a profile has no mutual friends, no profile picture, and zero posts—like the one my mother-in-law used—it gets blocked immediately. Empowering your teen to take control of their own follower list builds digital literacy and takes the pressure off you to constantly police their feeds.

## Decide Who Handles the Conversation

One of the biggest mistakes people make in this scenario is letting resentment build until they lash out directly at their spouse's parents. If your mother-in-law or father-in-law is the culprit, the golden rule of family dynamics applies: deal with your own family. 

My husband was the one who called his mother. It was uncomfortable for him, but it prevented the situation from turning into a narrative where I was painted as the controlling daughter-in-law keeping the grandchildren away. 

If your partner is reluctant to speak up, use concrete examples rather than emotional accusations. Instead of saying, "Your mom is a cyberstalker," frame it around trust and parenting boundaries: "Mom made a fake account to watch Maya's Instagram stories after we asked for space. It’s undermining our trust, and you need to talk to her about it."

## Keep the Confrontation Focused on Behavior, Not Intent

When your partner talks to the in-law, the conversation needs to stay strictly focused on the action itself, not the underlying motivation. Grandparents often try to justify this behavior by saying, "I just wanted to see what they were up to because I love them," or "I didn't want to bother you guys."

*   Acknowledge the love while rejecting the method: "We know you care about Leo, but creating a secret account to bypass our boundaries is deceptive."
*   Explain the impact on the teen: "When Leo realized a fake profile was watching him, it made him feel paranoid and uncomfortable using social media."
*   Set a clear boundary: "If you want to see updates, ask us to share photos directly, or ask Leo to accept your real account. Fake profiles will be blocked on sight."

You do not need to argue about whether they meant harm. The deception itself is the issue that needs to be addressed.

## Create a Family Media Policy Moving Forward

Once the air is cleared, use this incident to establish clear boundaries for the entire extended family. In our house, we instituted a simple rule: we do not accept extended family members on personal social media accounts until our children turn eighteen. 

Instead, we set up a shared digital photo album using a cloud service. Every week, we drop pictures of the kids into the album. This gives the grandparents the visual updates they crave without forcing our teenagers to perform for an audience of relatives who are analyzing their every post, outfit, and caption. 

Handling an in-law who resorts to digital espionage requires a steady hand, clear boundaries, and a united front with your partner. By locking down your teen's accounts and addressing the behavior directly, you can restore digital privacy and keep family relationships from fracturing completely.
