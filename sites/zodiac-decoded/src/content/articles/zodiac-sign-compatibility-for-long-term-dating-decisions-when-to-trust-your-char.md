When you are six months into a new relationship, the chemical cocktail of the honeymoon phase makes everyone look compatible. Your new partner’s quirks feel charming, their messy apartment feels bohemian, and their habit of texting constantly feels romantic rather than exhausting. But if you are trying to figure out whether this person is actual long-term partner material, relying on those temporary feel-good hormones can lead you off a cliff. 

Over the years of looking at synastry charts—the astrological term for comparing two people's birth charts—I have learned to use astrology not as a magic eight ball to predict a wedding date, but as a practical stress-test. Here is how I actually use astrology to evaluate long-term compatibility when the honeymoon dust is still settling.

## Look Past Sun Signs and Check the Elemental Balance

Most people know their Sun sign and maybe their partner's. While knowing that a Leo and a Scorpio are both fixed signs with a penchant for stubbornness is a good start, Sun signs only scratch the surface. When I am evaluating long-term potential, I look at the elemental balance of both charts: Fire, Earth, Air, and Water.

If you are an Air sign (Gemini, Libra, Aquarius) dating a Water sign (Cancer, Scorpio, Pisces), the initial attraction is often magnetic because you operate in completely different worlds. You bring the witty conversation; they bring the deep emotional currents. 

However, long-term survival requires friction management. When a real crisis hits—like losing a job, moving apartments, or dealing with family drama—the Air sign wants to talk it out logically and detach, while the Water sign needs to retreat and feel the emotional impact. If you do not recognize this elemental divide early, you will mistake your partner's coping mechanism for a personal attack against you.

## The Moon and Venus Test for Daily Domestic Life

The honeymoon phase can mask a lot of daily friction, but once you move in together or merge finances, your Moon and Venus placements take the wheel. 

Your Moon sign rules your emotional baseline, how you process stress, and what you need to feel safe. Your Venus sign governs how you express affection, spend money, and handle domestic aesthetics. If your Venus is in Taurus, you might express love by cooking elaborate meals and creating a cozy, secure home environment. If your partner's Venus is in Sagittarius, they might show love by planning spontaneous weekend road trips and leaving their hiking gear in the hallway. 

Neither approach is wrong, but if you do not spot these differences early, resentment builds. Look at your synastry to see where your Moons and Venuses land. Do they support each other, or are they constantly rubbing each other raw? 

## Track the Hard Aspects: Where the Real Work Lives

Every couple has friction, but astrological aspects tell you *where* that friction will live. When comparing charts, I pay close attention to hard aspects—squares and oppositions—between personal planets. 

* **Mars square Mars:** You both have high energy and independent streaks, meaning arguments can flare up fast over who does the dishes or drives the car.
* **Saturn square Venus:** You might struggle with feeling restricted by each other, or one person might feel like they are constantly playing the role of the parent or teacher in the relationship.
* **Mercury square Jupiter:** One person focuses intensely on details and logistics, while the other talks in grand visions and broad strokes, leading to constant miscommunication on practical matters.

Finding hard aspects in a chart does not mean you should break up. In fact, friction is necessary for growth. The difference is that the honeymoon phase hides these challenges, whereas a chart reveals them before they blindside you. If you know your Mars placements clash, you can consciously build in cooling-off periods during arguments rather than letting ego take over.

## How to Run Your Own Compatibility Audit This Weekend

If you want to stop guessing and start using your charts practically, set aside an hour this weekend to do a realistic compatibility audit using any reputable free chart calculator online. 

1. **Pull both full birth charts.** Make sure you have accurate birth times, not just dates, because houses and rising signs change rapidly.
2. **Identify your Moon and Venus signs.** Compare how you handle stress (Moon) and how you prefer to give and receive affection (Venus). Write down one specific mismatch you notice.
3. **Look for Saturn contacts.** Find where Saturn (the planet of structure and long-term commitment) touches personal planets in your partner's chart. This often highlights where the relationship requires hard work and maturity.
4. **Have a concrete conversation.** Take one astrological observation—such as noticing that your partner needs a lot of alone time (Aquarius Moon) while you need constant reassurance (Cancer Moon)—and talk about it over dinner without judgment. Use it as a framework to talk about real life needs instead of abstract traits.

Astrology will not do the work for you, but it acts as an effective blueprint for understanding the structural integrity of your relationship once the initial high wears off. Taking the time to look beyond the surface lets you decide with clear eyes whether this is someone you want to build a life with.
