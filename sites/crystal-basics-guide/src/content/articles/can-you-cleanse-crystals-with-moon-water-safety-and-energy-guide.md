If you have spent any time looking into crystal care, you have likely run into the idea of moon water. It sounds a bit mystical at first—just water that has sat under the moonlight for a night—but it is actually one of the most popular methods for resetting the energy of tumbled stones, raw clusters, and polished points. 

When I first started working with crystals, I made the mistake of soaking every stone I owned in whatever liquid was handy. I learned the hard way that water and certain minerals do not mix. Before you set out a mason jar under the next full moon, you need to know how to use moon water safely so you do not damage your collection while trying to clear its energy.

## What Exactly Is Moon Water?

Moon water is simply drinking water or spring water that you have left outside or on a windowsill overnight to absorb the light and energetic qualities of the moon, particularly during a full moon or new moon. In crystal practices, water is viewed as a natural cleanser that washes away stagnant energetic residue absorbed by your stones over weeks of use. 

Combining water with the gentle, cooling energy of the moon creates a potent cleansing bath. However, water is a physical element, and crystals are physical minerals. That combination requires a bit of practical chemistry knowledge before you dive in.

## The Golden Rule: Check Your Crystal’s Hardness First

Not all crystals are water-safe. If you drop a water-soluble stone into a bowl of moon water, you will wake up the next morning to a dissolved, pitted, or permanently ruined crystal. 

As a general rule of thumb, look at the Mohs scale of mineral hardness. Any stone ranking lower than a 5 or 6 should generally stay far away from liquids. 

*   **Do not submerge:** Selenite, pyrite, black tourmaline (raw varieties can flake and rust), hematite (contains iron and can rust), lapis lazuli (contains porous calcite and pyrite), and malachite (contains copper and is toxic when wet).
*   **Safe to submerge:** Quartz family stones, such as clear quartz, amethyst, citrine, and rose quartz, generally handle water just fine because they have a hardness of 7.

If you are unsure about a specific piece you bought at a local shop, skip direct submersion entirely. There is a much safer way to use moon water without ruining your favorite minerals.

## How to Do an Indirect Moon Water Cleanse

If you want the energetic benefits of moon water without the risk of dissolving your softer stones, use the indirect method. It takes thirty seconds to set up and poses zero risk to your collection.

1. Pour your collected moon water into a small glass jar or bowl with a secure lid.
2. Place your crystals—even fragile ones like selenite or lepidolite—on a small plate or cloth right next to the glass container. 
3. Intentionally visualize the energy radiating from the moon-charged water out through the glass and surrounding the stones, sweeping away any lingering heaviness.
4. Leave them together overnight. In the morning, pat your crystals dry if any condensation formed, and dump the water outside or use it to water your houseplants.

This indirect approach mimics the energetic cleanse of a water bath while keeping your vulnerable minerals completely dry.

## Direct Submersion Step-by-Step

If you have confirmed your stones are hard, water-safe quartz varieties, you can use direct contact. Here is how I prep mine on a full moon evening:

First, rinse your hard crystals briefly under lukewarm tap water to wash away any surface dust or fingerprints. Next, place them at the bottom of a clean glass bowl. Pour your prepared moon water over the top until the stones are completely submerged. 

Set the bowl outside on a balcony, porch, or on an indoor windowsill where it will get direct moonlight. Let it sit for the duration of the night. Bring the bowl inside before the morning sun hits it directly, as certain heat-sensitive stones like amethyst can fade in harsh sunlight. Remove the crystals, rinse them gently one last time, and place them on a clean towel to air dry.

## Assessing the Results

When you pick up your crystals after a moon water session, pay attention to how they feel in your hand. Physically, they should look bright and clean. Energetically, they often feel lighter, cooler, and more neutral to the touch. 

If a stone still feels heavy or dull after a water rinse, the energetic weight might simply need a different approach, such as sound cleansing with a singing bowl or resting on a bed of dry sea salt for twenty-four hours.
