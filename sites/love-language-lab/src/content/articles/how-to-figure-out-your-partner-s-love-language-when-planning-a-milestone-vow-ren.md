Planning a vow renewal for a milestone anniversary like a 25th or 50th is a wonderful project, but it can also bring old relationship dynamics to the surface. When my spouse and I started organizing our twenty-fifth anniversary celebration, I initially assumed we were on the same page. I wanted a big gathering with all our friends, assuming that celebrating publicly was the ultimate tribute to our marriage. My partner, however, went quiet every time I brought up the guest list. 

It turns out I was planning the party *I* wanted, rather than the celebration that would make my partner feel truly cherished. That friction forced me to look closely at how my spouse actually receives affection, rather than relying on what worked decades ago or what I assumed they wanted. If you are trying to honor a long marriage with a vow renewal, decoding your partner's love language right now will save you months of planning stress and ensure the day actually means the world to them.

## Look Beyond Daily Habits to Past Peak Moments

When we’ve been with someone for decades, it is easy to confuse domestic routines with emotional fulfillment. My partner unloads the dishwasher every morning, which I used to view as an act of service love language. But when I thought back across our twenty-five years, I realized the moments my partner talked about with the most emotion weren't the quiet Tuesday nights at home. They were specific trips we took alone, handwritten letters from tough seasons, and public defenses of our family.

To figure out what your partner truly values for your upcoming vow renewal, make a list of your top five relationship milestones or vacations from the past. Ask your partner, "What is your favorite memory of us from the last ten years?" Listen closely to the nouns and verbs they use. If they talk about how calm and private a specific cabin trip was, throwing a massive 150-person ballroom bash might completely miss the mark for their love language of Quality Time. If they mention how proud they were when you toasted them at a family reunion, Words of Affirmation or a large gathering might be exactly what they need.

## Observe Their Reactions to Other People’s Weddings and Anniversaries

We often broadcast our deepest desires through our criticisms or praise of other people's choices. Pay attention to how your partner reacts when you attend weddings, watch anniversary videos, or talk about friends' vow renewals. 

A few months before we started planning, we attended a flashy silver anniversary party for some neighbors. On the drive home, my partner remarked, "It was nice, but I don't think I could handle standing in front of a microphone like that." That single sentence was a massive clue. My partner values modesty and privacy. If I had booked a venue with a mandatory microphone-heavy ceremony where we read personal vows to a crowd, my spouse would have been mortified, not romantic. 

Take note of what makes your partner roll their eyes versus what makes them lean in. If they tear up watching a quiet, intimate beach elopement on television, take that as a literal blueprint for your own renewal ceremony.

## Audit How They Show Up for You When You Are Stressed

People often give love the exact way they desperately wish to receive it. If you want to know what makes your partner feel deeply loved, look at what they do for you when life gets heavy. 

When I was going through a brutal career transition a few years ago, my partner didn't try to give me pep talks or buy me gifts. Instead, they quietly took over all the household chores, packed my lunches, and made sure my car had gas. That is the textbook definition of Acts of Service. 

Translating this to a vow renewal means skipping the flashy aesthetic details—like custom neon signs or expensive floral arches—and investing that budget into making the day completely frictionless for them. For an Acts of Service partner, the greatest gift you can give on your vow renewal day is removing logistical burdens. Handle the vendor coordination entirely, hire a day-of coordinator so they don't have to answer questions, and protect their schedule so they can just show up and breathe.

## Test Small Gestures Before Booking the Big Venue

Before you put down a non-refundable deposit on a venue or hire a caterer, run a low-stakes experiment based on your working theory of their love language. 

If you suspect your partner's love language is Quality Time, surprise them with an uninterrupted afternoon walk or a dinner where phones are left in the car, and casually drop a question about the vow renewal into the conversation. Watch their physical posture. Do they tense up, or do they visibly relax and engage? If you think they need Gifts or Acts of Service, test a smaller gesture—like writing a specific, heartfelt card about a hard season you survived together—and see how they respond. 

Decades of marriage give us a massive advantage because we already know our partners deeply, but familiarity can also make us blind to how they have changed over time. By observing their reactions, auditing their natural habits, and testing ideas in miniature, you can design a vow renewal that feels tailor-made for the person you married. As you start weighing your options for venues, guest lists, and ceremony formats, taking a moment to align your plans with their core emotional needs will set the tone for your next chapter together.
