Living in an 800-square-foot apartment with a 12-pound tabby cat taught me a harsh truth about modern pet ownership: the pet industry assumes you live in a sprawling suburban home with a dedicated mudroom. When I first adopted Barnaby, my living room quickly transformed into a storage facility for plastic scoops, multi-colored cardboard scratchers, and giant plastic tubs of clumping clay. 

As someone trying to live with less stuff, the visual and physical clutter nearly drove me crazy. A standard minimalist approach tells you to get rid of things you do not use. But you cannot simply get rid of your cat's litter box or food bowls. Instead, living minimally with a pet in a small space requires a shift toward multi-functional design, aggressive consolidation, and changing how you buy supplies. Here is how I streamlined my apartment, eliminated the pet supply chaos, and kept both myself and my cat happy.

## Rethinking the Litter Box Zone

The single biggest visual and spatial disruption in a small apartment is the litter box. Traditionally, we place these plastic trays out in the open where they collect dust, track litter across the hardwood, and dominate a room's aesthetic. 

My first step was moving away from open pans and bulky plastic enclosures. I replaced our standard box with a sleek, wooden litter box cabinet that doubles as a side table next to my living room sofa. From the outside, guests just see a piece of mid-century modern furniture with a small side entrance. Inside, it houses the box, a wall-mounted scooper on a hook, and a small silicone mat to catch stray granules. 

To take it a step further, I applied strict minimalism to the litter maintenance routine. I stopped buying massive 40-pound plastic buckets of litter that took up half of my hallway closet. Instead, I switched to a subscription service that delivers lightweight, paper-based pellets in a compact, flat cardboard box once a month. It slides right under my bed, completely out of sight, freeing up valuable floor space.

## Consolidating Toys and Enrichment Gear

Cats get bored, which leads us to accumulate a graveyard of feathery wands, crinkle balls, and motorized mice that end scattered under the couch. I used to keep these in a large plastic bin, which just became a messy catch-all.

I adopted a "one in, one out" rule for Barnaby’s toys, similar to how I manage my own wardrobe. He has a strict limit of five active toys at any given time. The rest go into a small fabric pouch stored in a closet. Every Sunday evening, I rotate two of the toys out and bring two "new" ones back from the pouch. This keeps him stimulated without requiring a mountain of plastic clutter in the corner of the room.

When it comes to vertical space—which is sacred in a small apartment—I ditched the towering, carpet-covered cat tree. They are notoriously difficult to clean and dominate a room visually. Instead, I installed a single, sleek wooden floating shelf near my bookshelf that acts as a viewing perch, and a vertical sisal scratching post that mounts directly to the back of my bedroom door. Barnaby gets his scratching and climbing needs met, and my floor remains completely clear.

## Streamlining Feeding Stations and Food Storage

Pet food bags are an eyesore, and pet bowls often get kicked around in narrow kitchens. To solve the feeding station clutter, I moved Barnaby’s meals out of the kitchen entirely to avoid tripping over him while cooking. We set up his station inside a recessed nook in the hallway, using heavy ceramic bowls that do not slide around or require a bulky rubber mat underneath.

For food storage, I stopped keeping open bags or bulky plastic bins in the pantry. I invested in one airtight, stackable glass container that holds exactly one month's worth of dry food and sits neatly on a lower kitchen shelf. Treats are kept in a single magnetic tin that sticks to the side of the refrigerator, eliminating the half-open bags of dental chews and freeze-dried minnows that used to clutter my countertops.

Grooming supplies required a similar audit. I emptied a bathroom drawer, threw away three half-used bottles of specialized shampoos I used once, and consolidated everything down to a single metal tin containing a slicker brush, nail clippers, and a small bag of grooming wipes. 

Transitioning to a minimalist pet routine in a tight space is not about depriving your cat of comfort; it is about being intentional with every square inch of your home. By swapping bulky plastic items for multi-use furniture and eliminating backup supplies you do not actually need, you can reclaim your apartment. If you are ready to take the next step toward simplifying your small space while keeping your pet completely content, look at your current storage pain points and decide which single pet zone you want to streamline first.
