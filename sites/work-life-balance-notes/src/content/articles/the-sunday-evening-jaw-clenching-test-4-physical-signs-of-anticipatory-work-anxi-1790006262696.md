If you live for the weekend, you probably know the exact moment the magic starts to fade. For most of us, it is not Monday morning when the alarm blaring at 6:30 AM ruins the mood. It is Sunday afternoon, right around the time the sun starts dipping below the horizon and football or a lazy couch session wraps up. 

Suddenly, a heavy, sinking feeling settles in your chest. You are not actually working yet, but your body is already acting like you are trapped in a high-stakes emergency. 

Psychologists call this anticipatory work anxiety. But you do not need a clinical diagnosis to recognize it. You just need to pay attention to what your physical body is doing while you try to enjoy your final hours of freedom. Over years of burning the candle at both ends and ignoring my own burnout warnings, I learned to spot the physical tells that my brain was trying to hide from me. Here are four physical signs that your Sunday evening dread has crossed the line from normal reluctance into actual anticipatory anxiety—and what you can do about them tonight.

## 1. The TMJ Trap: Clenching Your Jaw and Grinding Your Teeth

Have you ever caught yourself biting down so hard on your back molars that your jaw aches? Or maybe you notice a dull headache radiating right up into your temples while you are folding laundry or watching a Sunday night movie. 

When your mind starts catastrophizing about Monday morning meetings, unread emails, or a demanding boss, your nervous system triggers a primitive fight-or-flight response. Even though the "threat" is just a spreadsheet, your body prepares to physically brace for impact. For many of us, that bracing happens in the masseter muscles of the jaw. 

**What to do right now:** Drop your shoulders away from your ears, and let your tongue rest loosely on the floor of your mouth behind your bottom front teeth. This physical posture physically prevents your jaw from locking shut. If your jaw is still sore, press a warm, damp washcloth against the sides of your face for two minutes to relax the muscle tension.

## 2. The Sunday Scaries Stomach: Digestion Shuts Down

Anxiety is notoriously bad for your gut. If your Sunday evening routine routinely involves sudden nausea, acid reflux, or an urgent trip to the bathroom that has nothing to do with what you ate for dinner, your enteric nervous system is sounding the alarm. 

When anticipatory anxiety takes over, blood flow is redirected away from your digestive tract and toward your major muscle groups, getting you ready to run from a tiger that only exists in your Outlook calendar. This sudden shift often manifests as stomach cramps, a hollow, burning sensation, or a total loss of appetite. 

**What to do right now:** Step away from heavy, greasy comfort foods on Sunday nights. Instead of trying to medicate the anxiety with a pint of ice cream or heavy takeout—which only adds physical distress to your gut—sip on a warm cup of ginger or peppermint tea. Focus on taking slow, belly-expanding breaths. Inhaling deeply for a count of four and exhaling for a count of six physically stimulates the vagus nerve, signaling to your gut that you are currently safe on your couch, not trapped in the conference room.

## 3. The Shallow Chest: Hyper-Fixated Breathing

Take a moment right now to notice how you are breathing. Is the movement happening in your upper chest, or is your stomach rising and falling? 

When we are caught up in mental loops about the coming work week—replaying a conversation gone wrong last Friday or mentally drafting an email you need to send at 9:00 AM—our breathing pattern shifts without us noticing. We slip into rapid, shallow chest breathing. This keeps your body flooded with stress hormones like cortisol and adrenaline, reinforcing the illusion that an emergency is happening right now. 

To break this cycle, you have to force your physiology to change your psychology, rather than the other way around. 

*   Sit upright with your feet flat on the floor.
*   Place one hand on your chest and the other on your stomach.
*   Breathe in through your nose so that only the hand on your stomach moves outward.
*   Exhale slowly through your mouth, feeling your stomach deflate.
*   Repeat this cycle five times before you open any work apps or check your phone notifications.

## 4. The Phantom Weight: Heavy Shoulders and Tension Headaches

Have you ever felt like someone was pressing a heavy bag of flour directly onto your shoulders around 7:00 PM on a Sunday? 

Chronic anticipatory stress causes us to chronically shrug our shoulders upward toward our ears, compressing the cervical spine and cutting off comfortable circulation. By Sunday night, this manifests as a tight band of pressure around your head or a persistent stiffness in your neck that makes turning your head to back out of the driveway feel difficult. 

Your physical posture is telling the story of your mental load. If you are carrying the entire weight of your department's upcoming deadlines on your physical frame, your body is never getting the weekend rest it was promised.

If you are noticing these physical symptoms consistently week after week, it is a clear signal that your current work boundaries are broken. Recognizing these physical red flags is the first step toward figuring out what changes you need to make to reclaim your peace of mind.
