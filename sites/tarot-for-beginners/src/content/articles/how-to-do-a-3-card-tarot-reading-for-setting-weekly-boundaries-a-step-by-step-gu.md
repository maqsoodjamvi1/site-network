Have you ever stared at your calendar on a Sunday night, felt a knot in your stomach, and realized you agreed to three too many things? I have. For the longest time, my default answer to every favor, extra shift, and social invite was a tired "yes." 

Setting boundaries is hard, especially when you are used to keeping everyone else happy. That is why I started using a simple three-card tarot spread every Sunday morning. It moves tarot away from fortune-telling and turns it into a practical boundary-setting tool. Instead of asking what the future holds, I ask the cards to show me where my energy is leaking and how to protect my time for the week ahead.

Here is how you can use a basic three-card draw to build a solid boundary game plan.

## Step 1: Prep Your Deck and Set Your Intention

Before you touch your cards, put your phone on silent. Pulling cards for boundaries requires a clear mind, because your own anxiety can easily color how you read the symbols. Shuffle the deck for about a minute, letting your mind drift to the upcoming week.

Instead of a vague question like "What will my week be like?", get specific. Say out loud: "Show me where I need to set a boundary this week, what is stopping me, and how to hold the line." 

When you feel ready, spread the cards face down in front of you and pull three cards, placing them in a horizontal line from left to right. 

## Step 2: Read Card 1 – The Energy Leak

The first card on the left represents your current vulnerability. It points directly to the place where you are most likely to overextend yourself or give away your power over the next seven days. 

For example, if you pull the Three of Swords, your boundary issue this week might center around emotional exhaustion. You are carrying old hurts or trying to fix a messy dynamic that isn't your responsibility. If you pull the Eight of Pentacles, your work life is the culprit. You are grinding away on tasks that nobody asked you to do just to prove your worth.

Look at this card and ask yourself: *Where am I already feeling resentful about giving too much?* The first card names the exact leak you need to patch.

## Step 3: Read Card 2 – The Internal Resistance

The middle card exposes the invisible block keeping you from saying "no." Setting boundaries is rarely about other people; it is almost always about our own internal fears. 

If the Three of Wands shows up here, your resistance might be FOMO—the fear that if you step back or miss out, you will fall behind professionally or socially. If you draw the Two of Cups, you might be dealing with people-pleasing tendencies. You are terrified that setting a firm boundary will make someone dislike you or cause a fight.

This card is often the most uncomfortable one to look at because it tells the truth about your habits. Acknowledge the fear without judging yourself for having it. 

## Step 4: Read Card 3 – The Actionable Boundary

The final card on the right is your practical prescription. This is the energy you need to step into in order to protect your peace. It translates the abstract idea of "boundaries" into a real-world action you can take this week.

Here is what this looks like in practice based on different cards:
*   **The Emperor:** Implement a strict schedule. If you work from home, log off at 5:00 PM sharp and stop answering messages. Treat your downtime as non-negotiable law.
*   **The Hermit:** Schedule a solid block of alone time. Cancel unnecessary plans, turn off notifications, and refuse to apologize for needing quiet.
*   **The Ace of Swords:** Have a direct, honest conversation. Send that text declining an invite or tell your boss you cannot take on another project without dropping something else.

Keep this final card in mind as your mantra for the week. When you feel the urge to cave, think of the archetype on this card and channel its strength.

## Moving Forward With Your Week

Once you have laid out your cards and interpreted what they mean for your schedule, take a photo of the spread with your phone. Having a visual reminder on your camera roll helps ground you when unexpected requests pop up on Wednesday afternoon and your old habits try to take over.
