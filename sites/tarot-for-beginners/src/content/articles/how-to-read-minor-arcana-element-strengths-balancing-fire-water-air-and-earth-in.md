When you first start working with tarot, the Major Arcana tends to steal the spotlight. Cards like The Fool, The Tower, and Death carry heavy life themes that feel dramatic and urgent. But if you want to know what is actually happening in your day-to-day life—your job, your bank account, or your dating calendar—you have to look at the Minor Arcana. 

Within the 56 cards of the Minor Arcana, the four suits—Wands, Cups, Swords, and Pentacles—map directly to the four classical elements: Fire, Water, Air, and Earth. Counting up which elements show up in a tarot spread gives you a quick, objective X-ray of where your energy is bottlenecked and where it is flowing. Here is how I look at element strengths in a spread, and how you can use this technique to make sense of a confusing reading.

## Assigning Elements to the Suits

Before you can balance a spread, you need to know which suit belongs to which element. If you use a standard Rider-Waite-Smith-based deck, the mapping is straightforward:

*   **Wands = Fire:** Action, passion, drive, motivation, and burnout.
*   **Cups = Water:** Emotions, relationships, intuition, empathy, and overwhelm.
*   **Swords = Air:** Thoughts, communication, conflict, logic, and anxiety.
*   **Pentacles = Earth:** Money, career, physical health, home, and tangible results.

Court cards (Pages, Knights, Queens, Kings) also carry these elemental associations. A Queen of Cups brings a double dose of Water energy, while a Knight of Wands brings pure Fire. 

## How to Do a Quick Element Count

The next time you lay out a spread—whether it is a simple three-card past/present/future or a ten-card Celtic Cross—ignore the individual meanings of the cards for a moment. Instead, flip them over mentally and count the suits. 

Let us say you pull a five-card spread about a career transition, and you end up with the Ace of Swords, the Three of Swords, the Page of Swords, the Eight of Pentacles, and the Two of Wands. 

That is three Swords (Air), one Pentacle (Earth), and one Wand (Fire). There is not a single Cup (Water) in sight. 

What does this tell you instantly? You are living entirely in your head. You are overthinking the decision (Air), maybe dealing with some sharp words or communication stress, and you have just enough grounded drive to keep working (Earth and Fire). But you are completely detached from your intuition and how this move actually makes you *feel* (no Water). 

## Reading the Extremes: Too Much or Too Little

Once you count the elements, look for extremes. A balanced spread usually features a mix of two or three elements. When one element dominates, or vanishes completely, the spread is shouting a specific warning.

If a spread is **heavy on Fire (Wands)**, you are moving fast, but you might be burning through your energy reserves without a solid plan. You are starting three projects at once and finishing none of them. 

If a spread is **missing Earth (Pentacles)** during a question about finances, it means you have plenty of ideas (Air) or passion (Fire), but you are not taking practical, physical steps. You are talking about launching a business, but you haven't opened a bank account or built a prototype.

When **Water (Cups) is totally absent** in a relationship reading, the connection might be functional or intellectual, but the emotional core is cold. You can analyze the problem all day long with logic (Swords), but without addressing the emotional reality (Water), nothing will shift.

## A Step-by-Step Practice Scenario

Let us walk through a practical example of how to adjust your approach based on element counts. 

Imagine you pull four cards to answer the question: *"How should I handle this conflict with my business partner?"*

1. **Seven of Wands** (Fire)
2. **King of Wands** (Fire)
3. **Five of Wands** (Fire)
4. **Knight of Wands** (Fire)

You have a pure Fire spread. Every single card is a Wand. 

If you read these individually, you might give standard advice about standing your ground, showing leadership, and managing competition. But looking at the element strength changes everything. Four Wands mean the room is scorching hot. Ego is flaring, tempers are short, and everyone is spoiling for a fight. 

If you respond with more Fire—arguing louder, pushing harder, or aggressively defending your position—you will burn the partnership to the ground. 

To fix an elemental imbalance in real life, you have to consciously introduce the missing elements. Since this spread lacks Water (calm emotion, empathy) and Earth (grounded practicality, slow pacing), your actual strategy becomes clear: step back, cool down the rhetoric, and focus on concrete, written agreements rather than heated verbal debates.

## Bringing Balance to Your Next Reading

Counting elements takes practice, but it prevents you from getting lost in the weeds of individual card meanings. When you look at a spread as an ecosystem of elements, you stop guessing and start seeing the structural mechanics of the situation. 

As you start experimenting with this technique in your own spreads, take note of which elements show up most often in your daily life and which ones you tend to ignore.
