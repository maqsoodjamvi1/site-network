When you suspect someone in your life is holding back how they really feel, the guesswork can drive you crazy. Whether it is a distant partner, a guarded friend, or a colleague who acts cold after a misunderstanding, trying to decode unspoken emotions usually just leads to overthinking. 

Over the years, I have found that standard relationship advice often boils down to "just talk to them." But what if you do not have the opening, or what if they simply shut down when put on the spot? That is when I turn to the cards. 

A simple three-card tarot spread can help you step back from your own anxiety, read between the lines, and figure out what is really going on beneath the surface. Here is how I set up and read a three-card spread specifically for uncovering unexpressed feelings.

## Preparing Your Deck and Setting the Intention

Before you pull a single card, you need to get clear on your energy. When we are stressed about someone else's hidden emotions, our own projections get mixed up in the reading. You might pull cards that reflect your fear of rejection rather than the other person's actual state of mind.

Find a quiet spot where you won't be interrupted. Take a few deep breaths and hold the deck in your hands for a moment. Instead of asking a vague question like "What do they think of me?", get specific. Name the person in your head and ask a targeted question, such as:
* "What is [Name] holding back from me regarding our recent argument?"
* "What is the underlying emotional barrier between [Name] and me right now?"

Shuffle the cards using whatever method feels natural to you—whether that is overhand shuffling, washing them across a table, or cutting the deck into three piles. When it feels right, deal three cards face down in a straight horizontal row from left to right.

## Position 1: What They Are Showing on the Surface

Flip the first card on the left. This represents the outer mask, the current dynamic, or the persona this person is presenting to the world—and to you. 

For example, if you pull the Three of Wands in this position, it might look like someone who is entirely focused on their future, busy, and moving forward. On the surface, they appear completely unbothered. 

When looking at this first card, ask yourself:
* How does this person act around me lately?
* Does this surface behavior match what my gut is telling me?

Often, this card explains the confusion. The reason you feel so unsettled is because the surface presentation does not match the energy you know is underneath. Acknowledge this card as the starting point, not the whole truth.

## Position 2: The Hidden Emotion They Are Suppressing

Now, flip the middle card. This is the core of the spread. This position reveals the unexpressed feeling, the unspoken fear, or the emotion they are actively keeping under wraps. 

Let us say your person appeared totally detached in the first card (Three of Wands), but you flip the Five of Cups in the middle. The Five of Cups is all about grief, regret, and dwelling on past disappointments. Suddenly, the picture clears up. They aren't cold because they don't care; they are withdrawn because they are nursing an old wound or regretting how things ended between you.

Other common signs of hidden feelings in this spot include:
* **The Two of Swords:** They are actively avoiding a choice and masking indecision with stubborn silence.
* **The Page of Cups (reversed):** They are feeling overly sensitive or immature about the situation, holding back a sentimental message because they are afraid of looking foolish.
* **The Eight of Pentacles:** They are throwing themselves into work or a project specifically to avoid dealing with emotional vulnerability.

Take your time with this card. Do not rush to judge them. Look at the imagery and let it translate into plain language: what are they actually afraid of admitting?

## Position 3: The Underlying Block or Motivation

Flip the final card on the right. This position answers the critical question: why aren't they saying anything? It points to the practical block, fear, or external factor keeping their lips sealed.

If you pulled the Seven of Swords here, the block is likely self-protection, distrust, or a fear of being exposed. They might feel that if they open up, they lose their upper hand or get hurt. If you pull the Hierophant, the block might be about tradition, rules, or a stubborn adherence to "how things are supposed to be done," preventing them from breaking protocol to text you first.

This third card helps you stop taking their silence personally. It shows you the mechanics of their hesitation. 

## Putting It All Together in a Real Scenario

Imagine you are trying to understand why your close friend has suddenly gone quiet. You do a three-card pull:
1. **Surface:** The Knight of Swords (They act sharp, blunt, and rushed when you talk).
2. **Hidden Feeling:** The Ace of Cups (They secretly care deeply and value the bond, but it feels overwhelming).
3. **Block:** The Seven of Pentacles (They are waiting to see if the investment is mutual before risking vulnerability).

Instead of getting defensive about their sharp tone (Position 1), you now see that they are secretly harboring a deep appreciation for the connection (Position 2), but they are paralyzed by scorekeeping and fear of unequal effort (Position 3). You realize that reacting with anger will only make them dig their heels in further.

Working with tarot for hidden emotions is ultimately about gaining empathy and clarity so you can decide your next move with a clear head.
