When people first start learning tarot, they usually focus on predicting external events or figuring out what someone else is thinking. But after reading cards for years, I have found that tarot is most powerful when it acts like a mirror for your own blind spots. A blind spot is that area of your life where you are actively missing the truth, usually because wishful thinking, ego, or fear is getting in the way. 

Certain cards in the Major Arcana appear specifically to drag these hidden dynamics out into the open. If you want to move past a recurring obstacle or stop making the same relationship mistakes, you need to know how to read these tricky archetypes. Three cards stand out as the ultimate masters of the unseen: The High Priestess, The Hermit, and The Moon. Here is how I use them in my own practice to catch blind spots before they derail my plans.

## The High Priestess: When You Already Know the Answer

The High Priestess is the card of intuition, silence, and secret knowledge. When she shows up in a reading about a blind spot, she delivers a humbling message: you already know the truth, but you are pretending you do not. 

I see this happen most often with career moves or toxic friendships. A client will ask if they should stay at a job where the boss is erratic, listing all the logical reasons to stick it out. Then The High Priestess pops up. That is my cue to stop analyzing the pros and cons on paper and look at what the client’s gut has been screaming at them for three months. 

To read this blind spot in your own spreads, stop asking for external advice. Put away the guidebook, cover the other cards on the table, and look directly at The High Priestess. Ask yourself: "What uncomfortable fact am I refusing to put into words?" Your intuition is rarely quiet; you have just trained yourself to talk over it.

## The Hermit: When You Are Isolating to Avoid Accountability

The Hermit is traditionally about introspection, wisdom, and taking a purposeful pause. However, when he represents a blind spot, he reveals a subtle trap: you are not resting or soul-searching; you are hiding. 

Many people use "needing space" or "doing the inner work" as a polite excuse to avoid difficult conversations, job applications, or social rejection. I pulled The Hermit constantly during a period of my life when I refused to pitch my writing to editors. I told myself I was just "perfecting my craft in solitude." In reality, I was terrified of rejection and using isolation as a shield.

If The Hermit lands in a position highlighting your blind spots, ask yourself a hard question: Is your solitude productive, or is it avoidance? Look at how long you have been mulling over a problem without taking action. If you have been "planning" a project or relationship boundary for six months without telling anyone or taking a single tangible step, The Hermit is telling you that your fortress has become a prison.

## The Moon: When Fear and Imagination Are Creating False Realities

Out of all the Major Arcana cards, The Moon is the trickster. It governs illusions, anxiety, and the subconscious mind working overtime. When The Moon highlights a blind spot, it means you are reacting to a horror movie playing inside your head rather than the actual situation in front of you.

This card frequently appears when people fall into loops of overthinking. For example, if you text a romantic interest, they take four hours to reply, and you immediately spiral into thoughts that they hate you and are about to ghost you, you are living under The Moon. You are letting temporary shadows distort your vision.

To decode this blind spot during a reading, separate observable facts from emotional projections. Write down what you actually know to be true on a physical piece of paper, and write down your fears on a separate sheet. The Moon shows you where your anxiety is inventing obstacles that do not exist, draining the energy you need to handle real problems.

## Practical Steps to Spot Your Own Blind Spots

Reading the Major Arcana for blind spots requires a slightly different approach than a standard daily draw. Instead of asking what will happen, you have to frame your questions around self-deception. 

* **Pull a single clarity card:** If you suspect you are missing something in a specific situation, pull one Major Arcana card and ask, "What am I refusing to look at right now?"
* **Audit your emotional reactions:** Notice which card makes you instantly defensive or dismissive when it appears. That visceral reaction is almost always a neon sign pointing directly at your biggest blind spot.
* **Keep a blind spot journal:** Record the scenarios where High Priestess, Hermit, or Moon energies show up for you, noting the exact excuses you were making to yourself at the time.

Learning to decode these subtle archetypes takes practice, but once you start recognizing your own blind spots, you stop repeating unnecessary cycles.
