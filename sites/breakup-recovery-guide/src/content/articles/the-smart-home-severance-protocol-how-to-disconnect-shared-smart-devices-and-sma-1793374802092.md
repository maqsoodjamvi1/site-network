When a relationship ends, the physical moving out is exhausting enough. But there is a second, invisible move-out happening at the exact same time: untangling your digital life. If you shared an apartment or a house with an ex, chances are your home is laced with smart technology. 

I learned this the hard way when my phone buzzed at 2:00 AM on a Tuesday, three weeks after my ex had moved out, notifying me that someone had unlocked the front door. It wasn't an intruder; my ex had simply used their old app login to check if I was home. That kind of digital tethering keeps your nervous system on high alert, trapping you in a panic loop where your own living space feels like it is still watching you. 

Untangling a shared smart home ecosystem requires a methodical approach. You cannot just delete an app and hope for the best. You need a protocol to sever these connections cleanly, securely, and without starting an avoidable digital war.

## Take Inventory of the Shared Ecosystem

Before you touch a single setting, walk through your home and list every device connected to Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, or a smart hub. Most people forget about the peripheral items. It is rarely just the main thermostat or the living room TV.

Look for smart bulbs in bedside lamps, security cameras in the hallway, smart plugs controlling the coffee maker, streaming sticks, and voice assistants like Amazon Echo or Google Nest hubs. Check who holds the "master" account for the primary router and the smart home hubs (like Apple HomeKit, Google Home, or Amazon Alexa). 

Write down the brands and the email addresses attached to the admin accounts. If your ex is the primary account holder for the main Wi-Fi network, your strategy will look very different than if you own the hardware. 

## Secure the Wi-Fi Router First

The absolute most important step in breaking free of a smart home ecosystem is cutting off the network access. If your ex still has your Wi-Fi password, they can potentially reconnect smart devices, monitor network traffic, or access local file storage.

Log into your router's admin panel. If your ex set it up and you do not have the login, you may need to physically factory-reset the router using a paperclip in the back reset port. Once you have control, do two things: change the admin password immediately, and change the Wi-Fi network name (SSID) and password.

Changing the Wi-Fi name and password will temporarily disconnect every smart device in your house. This is actually a feature, not a bug. It forces a hard reset on the ecosystem. When your smart lights and plugs go offline, it ensures that no lingering automation routines or background apps can secretly phone home to your ex's new device.

## Purge Shared App Homes and Household Accounts

Most major smart home platforms allow for "homes," "families," or "households" where multiple users share access to the same devices. Simply removing a user from an app is rarely enough; you need to audit the backend permissions.

*   **Apple HomeKit:** Open the Home app, tap the home icon, go to "Home Settings," and remove people. If your ex was the original "Owner" of the Home, you may need to completely reset the Home and set up a new one under your own iCloud account.
*   **Google Home:** Go to "Settings," tap "Household," select your ex's profile, and choose "Remove from home." Make sure to check shared speaker groups and Nest camera access permissions.
*   **Amazon Alexa:** Open the Alexa app, go to "Devices," select "Echo & Alexa" or specific smart devices, and check "Shared Household" settings to drop linked accounts.

If you share a streaming service or smart TV login, log out of all devices remotely. Netflix, Hulu, and Roku all have a "sign out of all devices" button in the account security settings. Use it. This prevents your ex from using your living room TV to watch shows or, worse, retaining passive visibility into your active hours.

## Factory Reset and Reclaim Physical Hardware

Once the network is secured and shared app access is revoked, you must physically reset the devices you are keeping. A smart device retains its pairing memory even if you change the Wi-Fi password. If someone has physical proximity and the right credentials, they can sometimes re-pair a device.

Perform a factory reset on every smart speaker, camera, thermostat, and lock. This usually involves holding down a specific physical button on the device for 10 to 15 seconds until it flashes a reset pattern. 

Re-add these devices to your network one by one, using a fresh email address or a newly secured personal account that your ex never had access to. If there are smart devices your ex bought and wants to take with them, pack them up and hand them over completely factory-reset so neither of you retains remote access to the other's property.

Untangling your living space from a shared digital past takes deliberate effort, but reclaiming your home network is a vital milestone in regaining your peace of mind. Taking these steps systematically ensures your space truly belongs to you again, allowing you to move forward without looking over your shoulder—or at your phone.
