Deciding to separate is exhausting enough without your browser actively conspiring against you. You close the laptop, trying to find a moment of peace, only to open a new tab and see their most recent Instagram post staring back at you in the "Suggested" feed. Or you start typing a random URL into the address bar, and autocomplete proudly finishes it with a shared Google Doc or an old flight booking site you used together. 

When you are trying to make a hard decision about a relationship—or nursing the raw, early days of a breakup—these constant digital reminders disrupt your focus. You do not want to wipe your entire browser history, because you need to find that recipe you looked at last Tuesday or the receipt for your utility bill. You just want the ambient presence of your ex to stop bleeding into your daily workflow. 

Here is how you can use browser extensions and built-in settings to build a digital shield around your attention span, without losing the rest of your internet life.

## Stop the Autocomplete Trap

The most jarring digital ambush usually happens in the address bar. You type the letter "r" to go to Reddit, and the browser helpfully suggests the Airbnb you rented together for your anniversary last year. 

Most people think the only fix is clearing their entire browsing history, which destroys months of useful autocomplete links and saved form data. Instead, you can surgically remove individual suggestions. 

On Google Chrome, Microsoft Edge, and Firefox, when an unwanted URL pops up in the drop-down address bar menu, do not click it. Use your keyboard's arrow keys to highlight the specific suggestion, then press `Shift + Delete` (on Windows) or `Shift + Fn + Delete` (on Mac). This permanently deletes that single line item from your history database without touching anything else. 

If your ex's name or shared email domain keeps appearing in form fields, click into the field, highlight the offending entry using your down arrow, and press `Shift + Delete` there as well. Doing this a dozen times over the course of a day will train your browser to stop volunteering memories you are trying to process.

## Deploy Targeted Redirect and Block Extensions

If you lack the willpower to stop typing your ex's favorite sites into the URL bar, let software do the heavy lifting. Standard ad blockers will not help here, but specialized productivity extensions can. 

Tools like "Block Site" or "LeechBlock" allow you to input specific domains and set strict rules for them. You can configure these extensions to completely block access to specific URLs, or set them to redirect you to a neutral page—like a blank screen or a news site—whenever you try to visit a restricted domain. 

For a more nuanced approach during a breakup, look for extensions that support time-delayed blocking. If you know you are most vulnerable to checking their social media profiles late at night, you can set the extension to block those specific domains between 9:00 PM and 7:00 AM, while leaving them accessible during work hours if professional curiosity demands it. 

The goal is not to punish yourself, but to put physical friction between a momentary impulse and the behavior itself. 

## Clean Up Your Feed Algorithms

Social media platforms study your habits to keep you engaged, and shared viewing history is a massive data point for them. If you and your ex shared a laptop, your browser cookies and device fingerprints are likely tangled together, telling the algorithm that you want to see content related to their hobbies, hometown, or mutual friends.

To fix this without logging out of every account you own, install a cookie-isolation or container extension, such as Firefox's Multi-Account Containers or similar profile-management extensions on Chromium-based browsers. These tools allow you to run different tabs in isolated sandboxes where they cannot share tracking data. 

Next, install an extension like "Social Focus" or "Hide Feed for Social Media." These tools let you strip away the algorithmic clutter from platforms like YouTube, LinkedIn, and Facebook while leaving the core messaging features intact. You can hide the right-hand recommendation sidebar on YouTube so you do not accidentally stumble on videos you watched together. You can also hide the main news feed entirely, transforming social media from an endless trap into a direct tool for sending a message and logging off.

## Reclaim Your Digital Workspace

Taking control of your digital environment is a quiet, practical form of self-preservation. You do not need to throw your computer out the window or live like a digital hermit to get some breathing room. By selectively pruning your autocomplete suggestions, automating your weak spots with smart blockers, and starving the algorithms of shared data, you create a cleaner space to think clearly. 

As you figure out your next steps and navigate the logistics of moving forward, remember that managing your digital space is just one part of protecting your peace of mind.
