When a relationship ends, the emotional fallout is heavy enough without the digital logistics. Long before you divide furniture or figure out who gets the toaster, you have to deal with the modern breakup tax: shared subscriptions. 

If you are trying to maintain strict no-contact to heal, the last thing you want is a text message saying, "Hey, your login isn't working for the streaming app," or worse, getting a notification that your ex is still ordering groceries using your saved credit card. 

Untangling your digital life without breaking no-contact is entirely possible if you use the right settings, change passwords strategically, and handle shared household accounts with minimal friction. Here is how to handle the most common shared accounts without reopening old wounds.

## The Amazon Household Dilemma

Amazon makes sharing perks very appealing. Between Prime shipping, Prime Video, and Amazon Household benefits, merging accounts feels like a milestone. Separating them, however, requires a specific sequence to avoid alerting your ex prematurely or leaving your payment info exposed.

First, secure your payment methods. Before you touch anything else, go to your Amazon wallet and remove any credit or debit cards that your ex might have saved. If you share an Amazon Household account—which links two adult accounts to share Prime benefits—removing your partner will trigger a notification period. 

To dissolve an Amazon Household without a direct confrontation, log into your account, navigate to the Amazon Household management page, and choose to leave the household or remove the other adult. Amazon has a mandatory 180-day restriction rule if you leave a household, meaning neither account can join a new household for six months. Keep this in mind, but prioritize your financial security first. Once you initiate the split, immediately change your Amazon account password and enable two-factor authentication.

## Uber and Uber Eats: The Saved Card Trap

Ride-sharing and food delivery apps are notorious for hiding active payment methods long after a relationship ends. If your ex has your credit card tied to their Uber or Uber Eats app, they can accidentally—or intentionally—charge rides and meals to your account. 

To fix this, you do not necessarily need to change your main email password if you want to keep your own login secure. Instead, go directly to your bank or credit card issuer's app or website. Lock or report lost the specific card that was saved on your ex's phone. This instantly cuts off the charges without requiring you to message them asking, "Can you please delete my Visa from your phone?" 

If you want to handle it inside the app, log into your Uber profile, go to "Wallet," and delete any shared or default payment methods. If your ex logged into your personal Uber account on their phone, go to your security settings and select "Log out of all devices." This forces a re-authentication across the board, locking them out permanently without giving them a chance to argue about it.

## Costco and Wholesale Memberships

Costco memberships usually come in pairs: a primary cardholder and a household cardholder. If you share a membership, untangling it depends on whose name is on the primary account.

If it is your membership, you have the upper hand. You can walk into any physical Costco customer service desk, present your membership card and photo ID, and request to remove the secondary household member immediately. You do not need your ex present, and you do not need to notify them. Costco will simply deactivate their specific barcode. 

If it is their membership and you have been using their household card, the cleanest break is to simply stop using it and apply for your own membership. Do not text them to ask if they have canceled your card yet. Assume your access is gone, protect your peace of mind, and set up your own account. If you need to return items or finish up joint purchases, handle those logistics through a trusted third party or accept the financial loss as the price of moving on cleanly.

## Netflix, Streaming Services, and Digital Footprints

Streaming services have cracked down on password sharing, which actually works in your favor during a breakup. However, you still need to protect your personal data, watch history, and recommendations.

Go into the account settings for Netflix, Hulu, Disney+, and Spotify, and look for the option to sign out of all devices. Once you have cleared all active sessions, change your password immediately. If your ex uses your profile, they will be hit with a login screen. They will figure out what happened without you needing to send an explanatory text. 

For shared utility apps or smart home devices—like Ring doorbells, Nest thermostats, or shared Apple iCloud storage—remove their admin access through the app settings before changing your master login credentials. 

Navigating the digital cleanup of a breakup is annoying, but it is a concrete, actionable step that helps you reclaim your independence. By utilizing security features like remote logouts, card locks, and account management settings, you can protect your boundaries and stay firmly on the path to recovery.
