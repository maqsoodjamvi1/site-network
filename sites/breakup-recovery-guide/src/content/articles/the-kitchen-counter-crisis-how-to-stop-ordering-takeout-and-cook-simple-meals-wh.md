The kitchen counter is a brutal place after a breakup. One night, you are staring at an empty space where your ex’s favorite coffee grinder used to sit. The next, you are looking at a stack of three delivery apps on your phone, wondering how you managed to spend forty-five dollars on lukewarm pad thai that tastes like cardboard because your chest feels hollowed out. 

Breakup grief ruins your appetite, but paradoxically, it also ruins your ability to make decisions. When your brain is flooded with stress hormones, the thought of chopping an onion feels like climbing Mount Everest. You either starve until 9:00 PM and eat saltine crackers over the sink, or you bleed your savings account on UberEats. 

Getting through this phase doesn't require a cookbook or a culinary revival. It requires lowering the bar so far that cooking becomes almost impossible to fail at. 

## Ditch the Meal Plan and Embrace Assembly, Not Cooking

Right now, you are not capable of cooking. Forget recipes that require you to "dice," "sauté," or "simmer while stirring occasionally." Those are activities for a person whose brain isn't currently occupied by a loop of every embarrassing thing they said during the final argument.

Instead, shift your definition of food to *assembly*. You are assembling calories so your body stops shaking. 

Keep your pantry stocked with items that require zero knives and minimal heat. Canned black beans, microwaveable rice packets, pre-washed spinach, rotisserie chickens from the grocery store, and jars of good salsa are your new baseline. Dumping a can of rinsed beans, a handful of spinach, and half a jar of salsa into a bowl and microwaving it for ninety seconds isn't gourmet, but it is warm, filling, and takes less effort than scrolling through a food delivery app.

## Weaponize the Rotisserie Chicken

If there is a holy grail for breakup survival, it is the standard grocery store rotisserie chicken. It is fully cooked, relatively cheap, and eliminates the two hardest parts of cooking: standing over a hot stove and raw meat anxiety.

When you bring it home, do not put it in a fancy container. Pull the meat off the bone with your bare hands directly into a Tupperware container while standing at the counter. Congratulations—you now have a protein source for the next three days. 

Here is how you turn that chicken into a meal with zero cooking skills:
*   **The Lazy Quesadilla:** Throw a handful of shredded chicken and some shredded cheese into a tortilla, fold it in half, and microwave it for thirty seconds (or put it in a dry skillet for two minutes if you have the energy). Dip it in salsa.
*   **The Desperation Soup:** Heat a can of chicken broth or vegetable broth, toss in a handful of spinach and a cup of shredded chicken. Add a squeeze of lemon if you want to feel fancy. 
*   **The Cold Plate:** Eat it straight out of the container with a side of baby carrots, hummus, and some crackers. 

## Lower Your Standards for Kitchen Cleanup

One of the hidden traps of cooking while depressed is the mountain of dishes waiting for you afterward. Looking at a sink full of pots and pans when your heart is already heavy is enough to make you swear off cooking forever.

Buy a pack of cheap paper plates and plastic forks. Use them. 

This is not the time to worry about your carbon footprint or aesthetic kitchen design. If using a paper plate means the difference between eating a homemade meal and ordering delivery for the fourth night in a row, use the paper plate. Furthermore, clean as you go—not out of discipline, but out of self-preservation. Wash the single fork you used *while* your microwaveable rice is spinning. If you don't touch the sponge tonight, tomorrow-you will pay for it.

## Eat the Same Thing Every Single Day

Decision fatigue is real, and breakups amplify it to a brutal degree. When you have to make fifty micro-decisions at work while trying not to cry in the bathroom, deciding what to eat for dinner can genuinely break you.

Give yourself permission to eat the exact same meal for five days straight. 

It does not matter if it is boring. Oatmeal with peanut butter and frozen berries for breakfast and dinner for three days in a row is totally fine. Your body does not care about dietary variety right now; it cares about predictable nourishment. Once your nervous system settles down a bit, you can worry about salads and balanced macros. For now, consistency is your anchor.

Taking care of your physical body when your emotional world has collapsed is a quiet act of rebellion against the grief. You do not need to cook a masterpiece to prove you are going to be okay. You just need to feed yourself well enough to wake up and try again tomorrow.
