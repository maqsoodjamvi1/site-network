When a relationship ends, the emotional toll is heavy enough without staring at your TV screen and realizing your ex is still watching *The Great British Baking Show* on your profile. Modern breakups come with a digital and domestic infrastructure that our parents never had to untangle. We share streaming services, smart home thermostats, airline loyalty miles, grocery delivery logins, and sometimes even a Costco membership. 

Doing a "shared asset audit" might sound a little clinical for something so painful, but treating it like a logistical project actually helps. When your brain is overwhelmed by grief, having a strict, step-by-step checklist keeps you from having to make emotional decisions on the fly. Here is how I walked through my own digital and domestic uncoupling without accidentally starting a text-message war or re-opening old wounds.

## Step One: Triage the Critical Utilities and Living Spaces

Before you touch a single streaming password, you have to look at anything attached to your physical safety, your credit, or your day-to-day living situation. This is where ignoring things out of avoidance can cause real financial or logistical damage.

If you share a lease or a mortgage, that is priority zero. But right below that are the modern conveniences we take for granted. Think about the Ring doorbell camera, the smart lock codes on the front door, the shared utility accounts, and the Wi-Fi router. 

Do not try to handle these over a tense phone call while you are both emotional. Send a simple, neutral text message to map out the logistics. For example: "Hey, I’m going to update the Wi-Fi password and change the Ring camera access today so we can both have some privacy. Let me know if you need me to leave the current internet billing info until the end of the month." This keeps the tone administrative rather than adversarial. You are managing an account, not debating the relationship.

## Step Two: Categorize Your Subscriptions by "Pain Level"

Next, open a notes app on your phone and list every single subscription, loyalty program, and shared digital space you currently split. Then, sort them into three categories based on how much emotional friction they are going to cause:

*   **Low Friction:** Services where you are the primary payer, your ex rarely uses it, and removing them requires zero interaction (like a magazine subscription or a standalone app).
*   **Medium Friction:** Shared streaming services (Netflix, Spotify, HBO) where someone’s algorithm, watch history, or downloaded content is going to get disrupted.
*   **High Friction:** Joint credit cards, shared Amazon Prime accounts with saved payment methods, or loyalty accounts with massive balances of points or miles.

Tackle the low-friction items first. Get them out of the way to build a little momentum. Cancel the app you never use, or remove their device from your Spotify family plan on a Tuesday afternoon when you have some quiet time. 

## Step Three: Establish the "Clean Break" Rule for Streaming and Digital Media

When it comes to Netflix, Hulu, or Spotify, sharing accounts is often an unconscious anchor keeping you tied together. Seeing your ex's profile pop up every time you want to watch a movie is a micro-dose of grief you do not need in your day.

If you are the account holder, the cleanest move is to change your password and sign out of all devices at once. You don’t need to announce it beforehand if doing so feels like inviting drama. If they text you asking why it stopped working, keep your reply boring and brief: "I'm auditing my monthly expenses and streamlining my accounts." 

If your ex is the primary account holder for a service you use daily, do not wait for them to cut you off. Bite the bullet, sign up for your own account, and migrate your data (like your Spotify playlists) immediately. Taking control of your own digital footprint feels surprisingly empowering. It sends a quiet signal to your brain that you are building an independent life.

## Step Four: Handle Financial Entanglements with Zero Drama

High-friction items like Amazon accounts with saved credit cards or shared airline miles require absolute precision. Do not leave a debit card or credit card attached to your ex's Amazon account just because it is convenient for one-click ordering. 

Log in, go to your account settings, and remove all payment methods immediately. If you have shared gift card balances or accumulated rewards points, decide quickly whether it is worth fighting over. In most cases, walking away from a small stash of airline miles is a bargain price for your peace of mind. If there is a substantial amount of cash or credit involved, propose a straight split over text or email, transfer the funds, and close the loop permanently.

Untangling your digital life won't magically heal a broken heart, but it stops the bleeding. Every password you change and every shared account you close is one less trigger catching you off guard during your morning coffee. 

As you start taking these practical steps to reclaim your space and your digital independence, it helps to have a clear roadmap for the next phase of your recovery and decision-making process.
