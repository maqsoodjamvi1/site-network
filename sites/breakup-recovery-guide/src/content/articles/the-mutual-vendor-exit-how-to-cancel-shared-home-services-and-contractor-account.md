Deciding to end a relationship is heavy enough without staring at a shared utility dashboard or a pending landscaping invoice. When you are trying to maintain strict no-contact to protect your healing process, the logistical loose ends of a shared household can feel like a series of tripwires. You do not need to text your ex to cancel the internet, nor do you need to endure a painful phone call about who pays the final pest control bill. 

Protecting your peace requires a systematic, administrative approach to untangling your living situation. Here is how to handle shared home services and contractor accounts cleanly, efficiently, and entirely on your own terms.

## Audit the Active Accounts and Autopays

Before you touch a single button, log into your primary checking account and credit card statements. Look back over the last three months for recurring monthly charges related to your shared living space. 

Make a physical or digital list of every active vendor: internet service providers, power and gas utilities, security systems, trash collection, lawn care, streaming bundles billed through a utility, and ongoing contractor payments (like bi-weekly house cleaning). 

Next, check whose name is primary on each account and which card is funding the autopay. If your credit card is tied to a vendor your ex uses at their new address, or vice versa, you need to detach your financial liability immediately. Do not guess about account ownership; log into the vendor portals to verify.

## Handle Utilities and Internet Without a Joint Phone Call

For standard utilities like electric, gas, and water, you do not need your ex’s permission or presence to close or transfer an account. If you are moving out, log into the utility provider’s website and schedule a disconnect date, or a transfer of service into your ex's name if they are staying. Most major utility companies allow you to do this entirely online or through an automated phone tree without speaking to a human agent.

Internet providers can be trickier, especially if there is equipment to return. If the account is in your name, log in, schedule the cancellation effective on your move-out date, and print the prepaid return shipping label for the router and modem. Drop the box off at the designated carrier location (like UPS or FedEx) and keep the receipt. 

If the internet account is in your ex's name and you are the one leaving, simply stop using it. Let your ex manage their own account closure. If you are staying and the account is in their name, do not call them to ask for the login. Call the provider directly, explain that you are the current resident, and ask to set up a new account in your own name. Providers love gaining new subscriptions and will usually waive activation fees or fast-track the installation to get a fresh account on their books.

## Cancel Ongoing Contractor and Home Maintenance Agreements

Independent contractors—such as landscapers, pool maintenance techs, snow removal crews, or house cleaners—operate a bit differently than major corporations. They often rely on text messages or personal relationships with their clients. 

If you hired a contractor using your credit card, log into your banking app and lock the card or remove it from the contractor's billing profile if they use a platform like Stripe or Square. Then, send a brief, polite, business-like text or email to the contractor. You do not need to overshare about the breakup. A simple message works best: 

"Hi [Contractor Name], this is [Your Name]. I'm writing to let you know that I am stepping away from the service agreement for [Property Address] effective immediately. Please cancel any future scheduled visits and do not charge my card on file for upcoming services. Thank you for your hard work."

Send the message and immediately mute or archive the contractor’s thread so you do not have to look at any confused replies or questions about what happened. If your ex has a problem with the service being canceled, they can hire the contractor themselves.

## Secure Smart Home Devices and Security Subscriptions

Shared smart home technology is a frequent vector for accidental boundary breaches. If you have a Ring doorbell, an ADT security system, Nest thermostats, or smart locks, you need to revoke access before you hand over keys or finalize your move.

*   **Change the Master Passwords:** If the primary account for your security system is tied to your email address, log in and remove all shared users or secondary profiles. 
*   **Factory Reset Hardware:** If you are leaving a smart home hub, security camera, or smart lock behind, perform a factory reset on the physical device so it completely unlinks from your cloud account.
*   **Cancel Cloud Subscriptions:** Turn off auto-renewals for cloud storage tied to home cameras so you are not unwittingly paying for your ex to monitor their new lifestyle on your dime.

By handling these accounts unilaterally and digitally, you bypass the emotional quicksand of negotiation. You are simply closing the books on a shared chapter so you can focus entirely on your own next steps.
