When I first started comparing relationship charts using numerology, I ran into a frustrating wall. I wanted to see if my partner and I were a good match, but I quickly discovered that numerologists use two completely different methods to calculate Life Path compatibility. One camp swears by addition, while the other insists subtraction is the only way to reveal the truth. 

If you are standing at this exact crossroads trying to figure out how to score your relationship before making a big decision—like moving in together or getting married—the conflicting advice can drive you crazy. Let’s break down how both math methods work, what they actually measure, and how to figure out which one you should trust for your specific situation.

## How the Addition Method Works

The addition method is the traditional approach most beginners encounter first. To use this method, you calculate your Life Path number and your partner's Life Path number separately by adding up all the digits in your respective birth dates until you get a single digit (or a master number like 11 or 22). 

Once you have both individual numbers, you simply add them together and reduce that sum to a single digit. For example, if you are a Life Path 3 and your partner is a Life Path 7, you add 3 plus 7 to get 10, which reduces to a 1. That resulting number represents the combined "vibe" or ultimate destination of your partnership. 

In practice, addition treats a relationship as a brand-new entity. Think of it like baking a cake: you take flour (your number) and sugar (their number), mix them together, and you get a completely different product (the combined number). This method is fantastic if you want to know what kind of energy you and your partner project out into the world together, or what your shared long-term mission looks like.

## How the Subtraction Method Works

The subtraction method takes a vastly different psychological approach. Instead of blending your numbers together into a shared score, subtraction looks at the energetic distance between you and your partner. 

To use this method, you take your Life Path number and your partner's Life Path number and subtract the smaller number from the larger one. If you are an 8 and your partner is a 3, the difference is 5. 

Subtraction measures friction, contrast, and growth potential. A low difference (like 0 or 1) means you and your partner view the world through a very similar lens. You will have an easy, comfortable rhythm, but you might struggle to push each other out of your comfort zones. A high difference (like 6 or 7) means you operate on almost entirely different frequencies. This creates immediate friction, but it also highlights where you have the most opportunity to learn from one another. 

## Which Method Should You Actually Use?

Neither math method is objectively "wrong," but they answer two entirely different questions. Choosing the right one depends entirely on what you are trying to figure out about your relationship right now.

*   **Use addition if:** You are already committed, living together, or planning a shared future, and you want to understand your collective strengths, shared blind spots, and overall partnership theme.
*   **Use subtraction if:** You are in the early dating stages, trying to understand recurring arguments, or wondering why certain topics cause immediate friction between you two.

Let's look at a real-world scenario. Say you are a Life Path 1 (independent, driven, likes to take charge) and your partner is a Life Path 8 (ambitious, business-minded, authoritative). 

If you use the addition method (1 plus 8 equals 9), you get a humanitarian, big-picture vibration that suggests you can achieve great things together by helping others. But if you use the subtraction method (8 minus 1 equals 7), you see that there is a gap of 7 points in how you process the world—meaning power struggles and a clash of egos are going to be your primary day-to-day challenges. 

In my experience, looking at only one of these numbers gives you a blind spot. The addition score tells you where you are headed as a team, while the subtraction score tells you what kind of terrain you have to cross to get there.

Before you make any major relationship decisions based solely on a single math formula, take a moment to look at both the sum and the difference of your numbers to get the full picture of your dynamic.
