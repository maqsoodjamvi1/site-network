If your weekend starts dissolving into a knot of anxiety around four o'clock on Sunday afternoon, you already know the Sunday Scaries. For me, that dread used to hijack the entire back half of my weekend. I would spend Sunday evening mentally drafting emails I had to send on Monday, pacing around the apartment, and completely psyching myself out about the early-morning alarm. 

The worst casualty of this cycle was almost always my Monday morning workout. I’d set my alarm for 6:00 AM with the best of intentions, but when it went off, the combination of fatigue and anticipatory work stress made staying under the covers feel like a matter of survival. I’d hit snooze until I ran out of time, feel guilty all day, and start the workweek already in a deficit.

Breaking that loop didn't require waking up earlier or finding some magical source of willpower. It required treating Sunday night not as the end of the weekend, but as the operational launchpad for Monday morning. Here is the exact framework I used to stop procrastinating and make Monday morning exercise non-negotiable.

## Weaponize Sunday Afternoon Decision Fatigue

When Monday morning rolls around, your brain is already working overtime to process a week's worth of tasks. If you leave even minor decisions—like which socks to wear, what podcast to queue up, or where your keys are—until 6:00 AM, you are giving your prefrontal cortex an excuse to quit. 

I call this the friction audit. On Sunday afternoon, I eliminate every micro-decision between my bed and the gym floor. 

*   **The Outfit Layout:** My gym clothes aren't just folded in a drawer; they are laid out on the floor next to my bed as a physical barricade. My socks are tucked inside my sneakers, and my gym bag is packed with a change of work clothes, deodorant, and headphones. 
*   **The Route and Playlist:** I pick the exact workout, the specific playlist, or the podcast episode before I go to sleep on Sunday. On Monday morning, I plug my headphones in before my feet even touch the cold floor.
*   **The Fuel Prep:** If I’m having coffee and a banana before I leave, the mug is sitting on the counter with the scoop of coffee ready in the basket. 

By removing the mental friction, you drop the barrier to entry so low that getting dressed takes less effort than debating whether or not to stay in bed.

## Redefine Monday's Workout Goal

One of the biggest reasons we procrastinate on Monday exercise is perfectionism. We think that because it is the start of a fresh week, we need to log a grueling, high-intensity, PR-chasing session to set the tone. 

That mindset is a trap. If you are already dealing with Sunday night anxiety, the last thing your central nervous system needs is a stressful, punishing workout. 

My rule for Monday morning is simple: the goal is participation, not performance. If I show up and lift weights for 30 minutes at 60% of my usual effort, or just walk briskly on the treadmill while listening to something I enjoy, that counts as a massive win. Lowering the bar removes the emotional weight of the workout. You aren't dragging yourself to a punishment chamber; you are treating your body to a transition ritual that burns off the weekend's cortisol.

## Use the "Ten-Minute Contract"

On mornings when the Scaries are hanging on particularly hard, bargaining with yourself doesn't work. Telling yourself "you'll feel great afterward" is a lie your brain won't buy at 6:15 AM. 

Instead, I use a strict contract: I only have to do ten minutes. 

I lace up my shoes and tell myself that if, after ten minutes of movement, I still want to crawl back into bed, I have full permission to quit. No guilt, no penalty. I can pack my bag, go home, and try again tomorrow. 

In the three years I've used this rule, I have actually walked out of the gym and gone home exactly twice. Once my body starts moving, the blood starts flowing, and the heavy feeling of the Sunday Scaries begins to lift. The hardest part of Monday exercise is never the workout itself; it's the twenty-second window between your alarm going off and your shoes getting tied. The ten-minute contract gets you through that window.

## Anchor the Habit to a Post-Workout Reward

If you want your brain to stop resisting Monday morning workouts, you have to give it a tangible reason to look forward to them. Relying purely on the abstract concept of health benefits isn't enough to combat a strong wave of work dread.

I built a rigid post-workout reward structure that only happens on Monday mornings. For me, it’s a specific pastry from a local coffee shop near the gym that I only visit on Mondays, paired with twenty minutes of reading fiction while I drink my second cup of coffee at my desk before my first meeting. 

When your brain starts associating Monday morning not just with the impending workweek, but with a specific, highly anticipated comfort waiting for you on the other side of the gym doors, the anticipation shifts. You aren't just suffering through an early alarm to check a box; you are executing a sequence that unlocks the best part of your morning routine.

Getting a grip on Monday morning procrastination isn't about becoming a naturally bulletproof morning person. It’s about building a system that protects you from your own tired, stressed-out mind on Sunday night.
