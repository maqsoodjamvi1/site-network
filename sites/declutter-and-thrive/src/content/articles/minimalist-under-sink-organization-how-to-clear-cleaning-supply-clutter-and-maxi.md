Opening the cabinet beneath the kitchen sink often feels like opening a trapdoor. Between the curved plumbing pipe cutting right through the middle, the random assortment of half-empty spray bottles, and the mystery sponges pushed all the way to the back, it is easily one of the most frustrating zones in a home. 

When I finally decided to overhaul mine last month, I pulled out four different bottles of window cleaner, two expired tubes of grout sealer from a project in 2019, and a bucket of rusty steel wool. None of it was doing me any favors. 

Applying a minimalist approach to this specific space changed everything. Minimalism under the sink isn’t about making your home look like a high-end showroom; it is about ruthless practicality. It means keeping only what you actually use, clearing out the visual noise, and making the remaining items dead simple to grab. Here is how I tackled the chaotic space under my sink, and how you can do the exact same thing this weekend.

## Step 1: The Total Empty-Out and the "Keep vs. Toss" Reality Check

You cannot organize your way out of having too much stuff. The very first thing you need to do is pull every single item out of the cabinet and set it on the kitchen counter. Be prepared for how messy it is back there—there is usually a layer of dust, stray crumbs, and leaked dish soap waiting for you anyway.

Once everything is out, wipe down the base of the cabinet with a damp cloth. Then, look at your pile of supplies and apply a strict filter:
*   **The Duplicate Rule:** Do you really need three open bottles of dish soap? Keep one active bottle and store one refill pouch in a utility closet. Toss or give away duplicate cleaning tools that you never reach for.
*   **The Expiration and Efficacy Check:** Check old spray bottles. If you bought a specialized cleaner five years ago for a stain that is long gone, and you haven't touched it since, let it go. 
*   **The Daily Driver Test:** Separate your absolute essentials—the spray you use daily for counters, your dish soap, and your primary scrub brush—from the occasional deep-cleaning supplies.

## Step 2: Working Around the Plumbing Trap

The biggest hurdle with under-sink storage is the vertical drain pipe and garbage disposal unit. Standard rectangular storage bins often fail here because they block the plumbing or waste the awkward gaps on either side.

To maximize this space, look for narrow, modular storage solutions that fit tightly against the side walls of the cabinet. I installed two small, sliding two-tier wire drawers—one on the left side of the plumbing and one on the right. Because they slide out like miniature filing cabinets, I can easily reach items stored in the very back without having to get down on my hands and knees and unload the front row. 

If you want to keep costs down, small plastic bins or even shallow shoeboxes work well, provided you place the items you use least frequently at the very back and your daily essentials right at the front.

## Step 3: Vertical Space and Door Hacks

Most people only use the floor of their cabinet, leaving a foot or two of empty vertical space up near the plumbing pipes. This is wasted real estate. 

Look up. The inside of your cabinet doors are prime territory for lightweight storage. I added a small, over-the-door wire basket to the inside of the cabinet door to hold my dish sponges, scrub brushes, and microfiber cloths. Keeping these items off the cabinet floor keeps them dry and prevents them from getting lost beneath larger bottles. 

Just make sure to measure the depth of your sponges and bottles before closing the cabinet door to ensure they don't smash directly into the plumbing pipes when the door shuts. 

## Step 4: Sticking to a Minimalist Maintenance Routine

The secret to keeping an under-sink area minimalist is not the containers you buy, but the habits you build afterward. When I bring home a new bottle of floor cleaner or laundry detergent, I make it a rule to finish the old one first, rather than letting them crowd the cabinet floor. 

Every few months, do a quick five-minute reset. Wipe down any drips, toss worn-out sponges, and make sure everything is back in its designated sliding drawer. By keeping the inventory low, you will never have to dread opening that cabinet door again.

As you look at your own kitchen or bathroom setup, think about which areas cause you the most daily friction and consider which storage tools might bridge the gap between your current clutter and a streamlined routine.
