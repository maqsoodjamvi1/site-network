If you are standing in the middle of a room, staring at piles of clothes, books, and mail, and your brain simply shuts down, you are not lazy. You are suffering from decision fatigue. When every single item requires a choice—keep, donate, trash, or relocate—the mental load becomes so heavy that doing nothing feels like the only option. 

When my own house reaches that breaking point, standard organizing advice falls flat. Telling me to sort things into four separate bins, categorize by frequency of use, or ask if an item sparks joy just makes me want to crawl under the covers. 

That is why I created a foolproof emergency protocol for myself called the Single-Bag Sweep. It completely bypasses the need to make micro-decisions and gets physical clutter off your floor in less than fifteen minutes. 

## The One Rule That Changes Everything

The secret to the Single-Bag Sweep is removing the burden of destination. Normally, when you pick up an item, you have to decide *where* it goes. Does it belong in the basement? The recycling bin? The Goodwill box? The junk drawer? That constant guessing game is what freezes your brain.

With this method, you grab a standard, opaque plastic trash bag. You are not allowed to use clear bags, because seeing the contents later will trigger a secondary wave of anxiety. 

Your only mission is to walk through the problem area and scoop up items that clearly do not belong there, or things you know instinctively you haven't used, worn, or cared about in months. You are not organizing. You are just sweeping surface clutter into a single container. 

## How to Do a 15-Minute Sweep

Set a timer on your phone for fifteen minutes. This creates a hard stop, which tricks your nervous system into realizing this isn't a life-long commitment—it is just a quick sprint. 

Walk into the room and start at the door, moving clockwise. As you go, sweep items into the bag using these three guidelines:

*   **The "Out of Place" Trap:** Grab items that belong in entirely different rooms—like the coffee mug from the bedroom, the mail sitting on the kitchen counter, or the toddler's sock in the home office. 
*   **The Obvious Excess:** Grab the empty water bottles, the expired coupons, the broken plastic toy parts, and the receipts fading at the bottom of a basket. 
*   **The Low-Hanging Fruit:** Grab the clothing items draped over the exercise bike that you know for a fact you haven't worn since before last winter. 

Do not stop to fold. Do not test whether a pen still works. If your hand touches it and it represents visual noise, sweep it into the bag. 

## What to Do with the Bag When the Timer Goes Off

When the timer rings, tie the bag shut. Do not look inside. Do not try to rescue anything from it. 

Now, take that bag and put it directly into the trunk of your car, or push it all the way to the back of your garage or coat closet. 

This is where the magic happens. The items are out of your line of sight, which immediately lowers your cortisol levels and gives you back visual breathing room in your home. You can now walk into your living room or bedroom and actually think straight, because the overwhelming pile has vanished.

## The 30-Day Quarantine Rule

Leave the bag alone for thirty days. Do not open it. 

Here is what usually happens during that month: If you desperately need something that ended up in that bag—say, a specific charger or a favorite shirt—you will remember it, go out to the garage, and retrieve it. That is a total win. You successfully identified a high-value item without getting bogged down in the sorting process.

However, for ninety percent of the items in that bag, you will completely forget they exist. When the thirty days are up, you can take the tied-up bag straight to a donation center or the dumpster without ever opening it. You bypass the emotional attachment trap because you already broke the physical tie to the item weeks ago.

When you are too overwhelmed to choose, stop trying to make smart, organized decisions. Just sweep the chaos into a bag, hide it from your sight, and let time do the heavy lifting for you as you figure out your next steps.
