If you are scrolling through travel blogs trying to decide between a crowded resort island in Thailand or a pricey eco-lodge in Bali, stop right there. When I first started planning a budget trip to Southeast Asia, I wanted that perfect mix of rich culture, incredible food, and extreme affordability without feeling like I needed a master’s degree in finance to pull it off. Cambodia completely delivered. 

While other regional hotspots have modernized and priced out backpackers, Cambodia remains wonderfully accessible. By focusing your first trip on the capital of Phnom Penh and the temple town of Siem Reap, you get a masterclass in history, culture, and street food—all while spending a fraction of what you would elsewhere.

## Why Cambodia Wins the Budget War

Let’s talk numbers, because that is why we are here. In places like Phuket or Singapore, the US dollar gets chipped away by high tourist taxes, mandatory resort fees, and inflated transportation costs. Cambodia operates differently. The US dollar is accepted everywhere alongside the local Riel, which makes budgeting dead simple since you do not have to constantly calculate weird exchange rates in your head.

Accommodation is where your wallet really breathes a sigh of relief. In Siem Reap and Phnom Penh, I stayed in clean, air-conditioned private hotel rooms with swimming pools for about $25 to $35 a night. If you are traveling solo or prefer hostels, you can easily find well-rated dorm beds for under $10. Compare that to a basic motel in the US or a tourist-heavy beach town in neighboring countries, and the savings are immediate. 

## Siem Reap: More Than Just Angkor Wat

Everyone goes to Siem Reap for Angkor Wat, and yes, it is worth the hype. But the biggest rookie mistake you can make is rushing through the temple complex in a single exhausted day. 

Buy the three-day temple pass. It gives you pacing flexibility so you do not suffer from temple fatigue. To make the most of it, hire a local tuk-tuk driver for the duration of your stay instead of booking an expensive tour bus. You can negotiate a flat daily rate—usually around $20 to $25—and your driver will wait for you outside every temple, handing you a cold bottled water the second you stagger back into the shade.

To round out your Siem Reap budget days without spending a fortune, skip the overpriced tourist restaurants on Pub Street and do this instead:
*   Grab breakfast at a local market where a massive bowl of *kuy teav* (pork noodle soup) costs about $1.50.
*   Book an afternoon tour with Phare, the Cambodian Circus, which supports a vocational school for disadvantaged youth and costs a fraction of a Broadway ticket.
*   Rent a bicycle for $3 to explore the quieter rural villages just outside the main tourist center.
*   Take a free evening walking tour of the Old Market area led by university students looking to practice their English.

## Phnom Penh: Raw History and River Vibes

After the serene (and sometimes tourist-heavy) atmosphere of Siem Reap, Phnom Penh brings a raw, energetic urban pulse. It is a city of grit, resilience, and incredible riverside sunsets. 

Start your historical education at the Tuol Sleng Genocide Museum (S-21) and the Killing Fields of Choeung Ek. These sites are heavy, emotional, and essential for understanding modern Cambodia. Admission to both combined with an audio guide is around $15. It is a sobering experience, but it grounds your travels in deep respect for the people you are meeting. 

To balance the heavier historical sites, spend your evenings walking the Riverside promenade. Grab a $0.75 draft beer at an open-air cafe, watch local families fly kites in the public parks, and sample freshwater fish skewers from the night market vendors. 

## Getting Around Without Getting Overcharged

Transportation in Cambodia is straightforward once you master local apps. Ditching street-hailing taxis in favor of the PassApp or Grab apps on your smartphone will save you from constant price haggling. These apps function just like Uber or Lyft; you input your destination, see the exact price in US dollars, and pay the driver directly in cash at the end of the ride. 

For longer journeys between Phnom Penh and Siem Reap, skip the expensive private taxis. Modern, air-conditioned express buses cost between $10 and $15 for the six-hour journey, and companies like Giant Ibis even offer onboard Wi-Fi and snack service.
