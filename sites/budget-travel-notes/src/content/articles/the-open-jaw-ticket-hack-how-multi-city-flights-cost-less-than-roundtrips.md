For years, I booked international travel the exact same way. I would search for a roundtrip ticket from New York to Rome, assume it was the cheapest option, and build my entire vacation backward from that assumption. That meant taking a stressful, expensive high-speed train all the way back down the Italian boot just to catch my return flight out of Fiumicino Airport. 

Then a flight attendant friend asked me a simple question: "Why are you paying to backtrack?"

She introduced me to the open-jaw ticket, and it completely changed how I plan budget trips. If you want to see more of the world without spending extra money on backtracking transportation, understanding how to book multi-city flights is the single most useful airline hack in your toolkit.

## What is an Open-Jaw Ticket?

An open-jaw flight is simply a multi-city itinerary where you fly into one destination, but fly home from a different one. The "open" part of the name refers to the gap in your itinerary—the space between your arrival airport and your departure airport. 

For example, you fly from Chicago to London, spend two weeks exploring England and France via train, and then fly home to Chicago from Paris. 

Many travelers assume that booking two one-way tickets will cost a fortune, or that flying into and out of different cities requires a complicated, expensive multi-city booking engine. In reality, major airlines price these itineraries using the same underlying mechanics as standard roundtrips. Often, the combined cost of flying "A to B" and "C to A" is roughly the same—or sometimes even cheaper—than a standard roundtrip.

## The Hidden Costs of Backtracking

Backtracking is the silent budget-killer of international travel. When you lock yourself into a traditional roundtrip ticket, you force yourself to return to your point of entry. 

Consider a standard two-week trip to Spain. If you fly roundtrip into Madrid, but you spend your second week eating tapas in Barcelona, you have to get back to Madrid to catch your flight home. That means paying for another train ticket, booking a final hotel night near the Madrid airport, and wasting an entire vacation day in transit. 

When you factor in the price of that domestic return train, the extra night of lodging, and the value of your lost vacation time, that "cheap" roundtrip ticket suddenly gets a lot more expensive. 

## How to Search and Book Multi-City Flights

Booking an open-jaw ticket does not require any special software. You can do it right now using standard flight search engines like Google Flights or Skyscanner. 

Here is my exact step-by-step process for finding these deals:

* Change your search settings from "Round trip" to "Multi-city" at the top of the search box.
* Enter your first leg: Your home airport to your first destination (e.g., New York to Lisbon).
* Enter your second leg: Your final destination back to your home airport (e.g., Madrid to New York).
* Leave the middle gap blank. You will handle that leg via budget train, regional bus, or a cheap low-cost carrier like Ryanair or EasyJet.
* Compare the total price against a standard roundtrip into your entry city.

In my experience, European open-jaw routes are particularly affordable because of the dense network of high-speed rail and budget airlines. You can easily stitch together an itinerary that starts in Amsterdam, ends in Berlin, and costs nearly the same as a standard roundtrip ticket to either city.

## Real-World Open-Jaw Scenarios That Save Money

To see how this works in practice, look at a classic Mediterranean vacation. If you fly roundtrip from Los Angeles to Athens, you are paying for the long-haul flight twice—once to get there, and once to get back across the Atlantic from the exact same tarmac. 

Instead, try this open-jaw combination:
* **Flight 1:** Los Angeles to Athens (explore the Greek islands).
* **Ground transport:** Ferry or budget flight from Greece to Italy.
* **Flight 2:** Rome to Los Angeles (explore Rome, then fly home).

By structuring your trip this way, you eliminate the need to buy a return flight from Rome back to Athens just to catch your ticket home. You only pay for the exact distance you actually travel.

## Things to Watch Out For

While open-jaw tickets are fantastic budget tools, they require a bit more strategic planning than a standard roundtrip. Keep these practical tips in mind before you book:

* **Check baggage rules:** If you use a budget airline to bridge the gap in the middle of your trip (like flying from Prague to Budapest), watch out for strict weight limits and baggage fees that can eat into your savings.
* **Confirm the math:** While open-jaw tickets are frequently cheaper or equal to roundtrips, airline pricing is dynamic. Always compare the multi-city total against a standard roundtrip just to make sure the airline isn't heavily penalizing the route change.
* **Know your visa and entry requirements:** Make sure multi-destination entries comply with local travel regulations, especially if you are traveling outside of major transit zones.

Mastering the open-jaw ticket takes away the logistical headache of looping your travels back to where you started. By treating your vacation as a straight line rather than a circle, you get to see more places, waste less time in transit, and keep your trip budget intact. 

Before you lock in your next big vacation plan and start mapping out train routes back to your arrival city, take a few minutes to price out a multi-city route and see how the numbers compare.
