If you have ever stared at a photo of a moody European fortress, checked the flights to Paris or Munich, and immediately closed your browser tab due to the price, I have a fix for you. Skip Western Europe and head straight to Romania. 

When I first planned a trip to Transylvania, I was looking for the fairytale architecture of Germany or France without the tourist crowds charging $30 just to walk into a courtyard. What I found was a region where you can explore actual medieval citadels, ride historic trains, and eat hearty local meals for a fraction of what you would spend in Italy or Spain. Here is how to plan your first budget-friendly trip to Romania without getting bogged down by travel myths.

## Why Romania Delivers More Value Than Western Europe

Western European hotspots have perfected the art of charging travelers for every little thing. In contrast, Romania remains wonderfully affordable because it operates on its own currency, the Romanian Leu (RON), rather than the Euro. 

When you compare daily expenses, the difference is staggering. A private room in a boutique guesthouse in the medieval town of Brașov often costs less than a bed in a crowded hostel dorm in Amsterdam or Dublin. Furthermore, entrance fees to castles, museums, and fortified churches usually range from $5 to $10. You do not have to budget-manage every single coffee or museum ticket, which takes a massive weight off your shoulders while traveling.

## Where to Base Yourself: Brașov vs. Cluj-Napoca

If you want to maximize your time and minimize your spending, base yourself in Brașov. It sits right in the heart of the country, surrounded by the Carpathian Mountains, and functions as an ideal hub for first-timers. 

Cluj-Napoca, located further northwest, is a fantastic, youthful university city with a great food scene, but Brașov puts you closer to the classic Transylvanian sights. From Brașov, you can easily access regional buses and trains to take day trips without needing to rent a car. Renting a car in Romania is entirely doable, but rural driving habits can be aggressive, and train travel is cheap enough that many budget travelers prefer the rails.

## The Castles and Culture You Need to See

You cannot visit Transylvania without seeing its architecture, but you should be strategic about which sites you visit to save money and avoid gridlock. 

*   **Peleș Castle (Sinaia):** Technically in Wallachia just south of Transylvania, this former royal residence looks like something straight out of a Disney movie. The exterior is free to admire from the gardens, and the interior ticket is well worth the cost for the woodwork alone.
*   **Bran Castle:** Famous for its association with the Dracula legend (though Vlad the Impaler barely spent time here), it gets very crowded. If you are short on time or cash, you can skip the interior line, take photos from the valley below, and spend your afternoon exploring the nearby Saxon villages instead.
*   **Râșnov Fortress:** Perched high on a hill, this peasant fortress gives you sweeping views of the valley and costs a fraction of the busier castles. 

Beyond the fortresses, the true cultural highlight of Transylvania is its UNESCO-listed fortified churches, such as those in Biertan or Viscri. These are remote villages where ethnic Saxon communities built massive defensive walls around their churches centuries ago. Visiting them gives you a genuine look at rural European history that mass tourism hasn't entirely sanitized.

## Practical Logistics for First-Timers

Getting around Romania on a budget requires a little bit of patience. The national railway network, CFR Călători, is exceptionally cheap, though trains can run behind schedule. Always check the official website or ticket windows rather than relying on third-party apps that tack on extra fees.

When it comes to food, skip the tourist traps right outside major monuments. Look for local spots serving *ciorbă* (sour soups served in bread bowls) and *sarmale* (cabbage rolls stuffed with minced meat and rice). A massive, filling meal paired with a local beer or mineral water will rarely top $12 to $15. 

As you map out your itinerary, look into shoulder-season travel during May, June, or September. The weather in the mountains is crisp and pleasant, the autumn foliage in Transylvania is world-class, and you will dodge the peak July crowds completely.
