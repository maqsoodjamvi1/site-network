If your passport is itching for a European vacation but your bank account is sweating at the thought of Western European prices, I have a secret for you. Skip the €18 Aperol Spritzes in Rome and the $300-a-night tiny hotels in Paris. Instead, book a flight to Romania. 

I spent three weeks road-tripping through Transylvania with a budget that would have barely covered four days in London or Amsterdam. What I found was a region that delivers cinematic landscapes, medieval history, and genuine hospitality for a fraction of what the rest of the continent charges. Here is how to plan your first budget trip to Romania and why Transylvania should be at the top of your list.

## Why Transylvania Wins on Value

When Americans think of budget travel in Europe, they usually look toward Southeast Asia or the well-trodden backpacker trails of Prague and Budapest. But Western tourists have pushed prices up in those cities. Transylvania, the historic region in central Romania defined by the Carpathian Mountains, remains remarkably underpriced for English-speaking travelers. 

The value comes down to supply and demand, plus a favorable currency exchange. Romania uses the Leu (RON), and your US dollars go exceptionally far here. A sit-down meal of hearty local food—think polenta with cheese and sour cream, or slow-cooked pork stews—rarely costs more than $8 to $12. A clean, central private room in a boutique guesthouse in medieval towns like Brașov or Sighișoara often runs around $45 a night. 

Crucially, you get world-class culture without the tourist crush. You can stand inside a 14th-century Saxon fortified church without dodging selfie sticks or waiting in a two-hour queue. 

## Getting Your Bearings: The Main Hubs

To make the most of a budget trip, you want to base yourself strategically. Transylvania is best explored by renting a car or utilizing the surprisingly efficient train network, but your anchor points should be well-chosen.

*   **Brașov:** This is the ultimate base for first-timers. It features a stunning medieval square, dramatic mountain backdrops, and a direct train line to Bucharest. It is also cheap and centrally located for day trips.
*   **Sighișoara:** A UNESCO World Heritage site that happens to be the birthplace of Vlad the Impaler (the real-life inspiration for Dracula). It is a colorful, walled citadel perched on a hill.
*   **Cluj-Napoca:** A vibrant university city with a massive student population, meaning cheap cafes, great nightlife, and a modern arts scene.

Flying into Bucharest (OTP) is usually your cheapest option from the US. From there, you can catch a direct train north to Brașov for about $15, which takes roughly two and a half hours and treats you to sweeping mountain views along the Prahova Valley.

## Real-World Costs: What to Expect Daily

Let’s talk numbers so you can build a realistic spreadsheet. As a solo traveler keeping a moderate budget—meaning private rooms, eating out for every meal, and paying for museum entries—I averaged about $65 a day. 

*   **Accommodation:** $40–$60 per night for a well-rated, central apartment or guesthouse. Hostels in Cluj or Brașov can drop that to $20 if you don't mind dorms.
*   **Food & Drink:** $20–$30 a day. A local draft beer costs about $2.50, a strong espresso is $2, and bakeries sell massive, cheese-filled pastries called *merdenele* for less than $1.50.
*   **Transport:** $5–$15 per intercity train ticket. Renting a compact car through a local aggregator runs about $25 a day, plus the cost of gas.
*   **Activities:** $3–$8 for castle entries, museums, and historic town towers.

## Practical Tips for Your First Trip

Navigating a new country can feel intimidating, but Romania is straightforward if you keep a few logistics in mind.

First, download the Bolt app (Europe's equivalent to Uber) for city transit. Taxis in Bucharest and larger towns can sometimes try to take advantage of obvious tourists, but ride-sharing apps lock in the price and route upfront. 

Second, do not skip rural Transylvania. While the cities are great, the magic happens in villages like Viscri, where unpaved dirt roads are flanked by Saxon houses and horse-drawn carts still share the lane with modern sedans. You can find local families offering traditional home-cooked dinners for a nominal fee if you ask your guesthouse host for a recommendation.

Finally, keep some physical cash (Lei) on hand. While card payments are accepted in most city restaurants and grocery stores, smaller rural guesthouses, local produce markets, and entry fees for minor historic sites often operate strictly on cash. 

If you are ready to trade the overwhelming crowds and inflated price tags of Western Europe for a destination that still feels like a secret discovery, mapping out a route through Transylvania is your best next step. Review your current travel calendar, look up flight routes into Bucharest, and start building an itinerary that prioritizes medieval charm over tourist traps.
