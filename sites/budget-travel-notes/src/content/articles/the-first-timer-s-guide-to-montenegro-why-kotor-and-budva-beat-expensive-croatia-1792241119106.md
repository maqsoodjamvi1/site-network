If you have been scrolling through travel feeds lately, you have probably noticed Croatia getting all the love. Dubrovnik and Split are gorgeous, but the secret is out—and your wallet is going to feel it. When I planned my first trip down the Adriatic coast, I realized I could easily blow my entire yearly travel fund in a single week just paying for coffee and old-town walking tours in Croatia. 

That is when I looked slightly further south and found Montenegro. 

Montenegro gives you the same dramatic fjord-like bays, crystal-clear water, and centuries-old Venetian stone architecture as its famous neighbor, but at a fraction of the cost. If you want a Mediterranean getaway without returning home to credit card debt, Kotor and Budva should be at the top of your radar. Here is how to plan your first budget-friendly trip there.

## Why Montenegro Wins the Cost Comparison

Let us look at actual numbers from the ground. In Croatia's peak season, a modest apartment rental inside a historic walled city can easily run upwards of $150 to $200 a night, and a basic restaurant dinner for two will push $70 before you even look at wine. 

In Montenegro, your money stretches much further. During a recent shoulder-season trip, I secured a clean, modern studio apartment with a balcony overlooking the bay for $45 a night. A hearty meal of grilled local trout, Swiss chard with potatoes, and a glass of Vranac wine routinely cost me around $15 to $20 total. 

Beyond accommodation and food, the hidden costs of travel—like museum entries, coffee, and local transit—are noticeably cheaper. You aren't paying a "tourism premium" on every single transaction.

## Kotor: The Medieval Gem in the Cliffs

Kotor sits at the very end of a dramatic, steep-walled bay that often gets compared to a Norwegian fjord, minus the freezing temperatures. The Old Town is a labyrinth of marble streets, Venetian palaces, and wandering cats (there is even a cat museum). 

Because the town is small and enclosed by medieval walls, you can walk everywhere. 

*   **Skip the overpriced harbor restaurants:** Walk just three blocks away from the main cruise ship gates into the residential alleys for local bakeries (*pekaras*) where you can grab a massive slice of *burek* (flaky pastry stuffed with meat or cheese) for about two dollars.
*   **Climb the San Giovanni Fortress early:** Instead of paying for expensive organized tours, hike the ancient city walls up to the fortress. It costs a few euros, but do it at 7:00 AM before the sun bakes the stone path and before the massive cruise ship crowds funnel in. 
*   **Take the local bus to Perast:** Skip expensive boat tours and walk to the local bus stop outside the north gate. A short, twenty-minute bus ride costs less than three dollars and takes you straight to Perast, a stunning baroque town where you can catch a cheap water taxi out to Our Lady of the Rocks.

## Budva: Beaches and Budget Nightlife

If Kotor is for history lovers and hikers, Budva is for beachgoers who still appreciate a bit of old-world charm. Budva also features a walled medieval old town, but it transitions quickly into a long stretch of Adriatic coastline dotted with gravel beaches and seaside promenades.

Budva has a reputation for being a party town, which can scare off budget travelers who want peace and quiet, but that reputation is mostly limited to July and August. If you visit in June or September, you get warm swimming weather, empty beaches, and cheap lodging.

*   **Walk the coastal path to Bečići:** Instead of paying for a sunbed at the crowded central beach, follow the cliffside pedestrian tunnel carved into the rock leading away from the Old Town. It brings you to Bečići beach, which is longer, quieter, and generally cheaper for amenities.
*   **Eat at local markets:** Budva has fantastic grocery stores and open-air markets just outside the tourist zone. Buying fresh local figs, soft cheese, crusty bread, and olives for a picnic by the water costs pennies compared to a tavern lunch.

## Practical Logistics for First-Timers

Getting to Montenegro from the US usually involves flying into Dubrovnik, Croatia, and taking a shuttle or bus across the border, or flying into Montenegro's capital, Podgorica. Tivat airport is right next to Kotor, but flights can be pricier. 

If you cross the land border from Croatia, be prepared for potential delays during peak summer months. A private transfer across the border can cost around $100, but local buses are significantly cheaper, though they require a bit of patience at customs checkpoints. Also, keep in mind that while Montenegro is not officially in the European Union, the country uses the Euro as its official currency, making price tracking simple.

As you finalize your travel dates and start mapping out your daily itineraries, take a moment to look into local transit schedules and lodging options a few months in advance to lock in the best rates.
