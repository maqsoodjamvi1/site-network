If you are tired of spending three grand on a five-day trip to Paris or Rome only to stand in two-hour lines behind tour buses, I have a better idea for your next vacation. Skip Western Europe entirely and head to the Baltics. Specifically, start with Lithuania. 

When I first planned a trip to Vilnius and Kaunas, I was looking for a budget-friendly alternative to the usual tourist traps. What I found was a country with an incredible cafe culture, world-class modern art, and medieval history that costs a fraction of what you would pay in Germany or France. Best of all, most people here speak fluent English, and the cities are compact enough that you do not need to rent a car or stress over complex train systems. 

Here is how to experience two of Lithuania’s best cities without draining your savings account.

## Vilnius: Baroque Architecture Meets Bohemian Edge

Vilnius is the capital, but it feels more like a collection of eccentric neighborhoods than a bustling metropolis. The Old Town is one of the largest surviving medieval quarters in Europe, filled with Gothic and Baroque churches, cobblestone alleys, and hidden courtyards. 

The real magic of Vilnius, however, is its independent spirit. Case in point: Užupis. This is a self-declared independent republic located just across a small river from the main old town. Artists and free spirits set up this micro-nation in the late nineties, complete with its own constitution (which includes whimsical rights like the right to have a dog and the right to be idle). Walking across the bridge costs nothing, and you can spend an afternoon browsing quirky metal sculptures, independent art galleries, and small artisan shops. 

When it comes to meals, skip the tourist-heavy Gediminas Avenue and head toward the Pilies Street side streets or the station district. You can easily find a hearty bowl of *cepelinai* (traditional potato dumplings stuffed with minced meat) or modern Baltic fusion food for under 10 euros. A craft beer in a local pub usually runs about 4 to 5 euros, which is about half what you will pay in Western capitals.

## Kaunas: The Capital of Brutalism and Interwar Modernism

After a few days in Vilnius, take the train to Kaunas. The ride takes just over an hour and costs around 6 to 8 euros each way. While Vilnius is romantic and winding, Kaunas is bold, intellectual, and heavily architectural. 

Kaunas served as Lithuania's temporary capital between the First and Second World Wars. During that brief period of independence, architects went wild building in the Art Deco and Lithuanian Modernism styles. Today, the city boasts a UNESCO designation for this unique collection of 1930s architecture. If you enjoy urban exploring, walking the streets of the New Town feels like stepping onto a vintage movie set.

Kaunas is also a stronghold for contemporary art and museums. The M.K. Čiurlionis National Museum of Art houses the mesmerizing, dream-like paintings of Lithuania's most famous symbolist artist and composer. Afterward, you can take the historic funicular railway—built in 1931 and still operating with its original wooden interior—up to the Aleksotas viewpoint for a panoramic look over the entire city for less than a euro.

## Practical Logistics for Your First Trip

Traveling through Lithuania independently is straightforward, but a few local details will save you time and hassle. 

*   **Public Transit:** Both Vilnius and Kaunas use the Trafi app for real-time bus and trolleybus schedules. You can buy digital single tickets directly on your phone or tap a contactless credit card as you board most modern buses.
*   **Walking is Best:** Both city centers are completely walkable. Wear comfortable shoes because the historic streets feature heavy cobblestones that will quickly punish thin-soled sneakers.
*   **Museum Discounts:** If you plan to visit multiple museums and historic sites, look into city passes, though individual entry fees are generally very low (usually 3 to 7 euros per museum).
*   **Language and Currency:** Lithuania uses the Euro. English is widely spoken by nearly everyone under the age of fifty, making navigation, ordering food, and asking for directions stress-free.

 Lithuania offers the rare combination of deep cultural immersion and genuinely low daily expenses, proving you do not need a massive travel budget to experience a rich, historic European getaway. As you map out your itinerary, the next step is to look at flight connections into Vilnius Airport and secure your accommodation well in advance to lock in the best rates.
