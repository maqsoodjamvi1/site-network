Planning a month-long trip when you have a dog or a cat usually brings one major budget shock: pet care. Traditional boarding facilities often charge a flat daily rate per animal, and once you factor in 30 days of consecutive care plus holiday surcharges or medication fees, the bill can easily outpace your actual flight and lodging costs. 

When I planned a five-week trip last year, quotes from local boarding kennels hovered around $45 a day for a single medium-sized dog. Multiply that by 30 days, and I was looking at $1,350 just to keep my dog in a concrete-and-chain-link run. That forced me to look closely at alternatives, specifically house-and-pet-sitting networks. 

Here is how I broke down the numbers, managed the hidden costs, and figured out whether traditional boarding or a long-term pet sitter made more financial sense for a month-long absence.

## Run the True Math on 30 Days of Commercial Boarding

Before you write off boarding entirely, you need to calculate the exact out-of-pocket cost. Kennels often quote a base rate, but long stays come with variable expenses that creep up on day 15 or 20. 

Make a list of every fee your kennel tacks on. Does your dog need individual playtime? That is usually an extra $10 to $15 daily. Is your cat on daily medication? Many facilities charge an administration fee per pill. Drop-off and pick-up times matter, too—if you miss the morning window, you are billed for an extra night. 

For my golden retriever, a true 30-day boarding estimate hit nearly $1,600 after adding tax, daily medication fees, and a mandatory exit bath. If you have two pets, boarding costs often double because facilities rarely offer multi-pet discounts in the same enclosure. 

## Calculate the Real Costs of Using a House-Sitter

Using a dedicated pet-sitting platform looks free on the surface—you pay an annual membership fee to access sitters, and in exchange, they stay in your home for free while caring for your animals. However, "free" house-sitting comes with its own budget line items that you need to anticipate before committing.

Utility bills are the most obvious hidden cost. With someone living in your home 24/7 for a month, your electricity, water, and gas usage will rise compared to leaving the house empty on "away" mode. Expect higher air conditioning bills in the summer or increased heating costs in the winter. 

You also need to factor in household consumables. It is customary—and practically necessary for a good review—to leave basic groceries like coffee, cooking oil, and basic pantry staples, plus clean bed linens and towels. Finally, factor in the annual platform membership fee, which usually runs around $100 to $150. Even with utilities and membership included, my 30-day sitter cost came out to roughly $250—saving me over $1,300 compared to a kennel.

## Compare Your Daily Logistics and Stress Factors

Money is only part of the equation. A 30-day stretch is a long time, and the logistical friction of your choice will impact your mental peace while you travel. 

Traditional boarding provides absolute reliability. Unless the facility has an emergency, your pet has a guaranteed spot, food, and basic supervision. However, many pets experience high anxiety in kennels, which can lead to refused meals, kennel cough, or behavioral regression that requires vet visits upon your return.

A house-sitter keeps your pet in their normal territory, maintaining their sleep schedule, feeding routine, and backyard access. The downside is human unpredictability. Sitters can have personal emergencies, flight delays, or misunderstandings about care instructions. Mitigate this risk by running a trial weekend stay a month before your big trip to test compatibility.

## Use This 30-Day Decision Checklist

To make the final call for your specific trip, walk through this quick assessment:

*   **Pet Temperament:** Does your animal panic in new environments, or are they easygoing social butterflies who love other dogs at daycare?
*   **Medical Needs:** Can a sitter manage complex insulin schedules or physical therapy, or is a professional kennel staff safer for high-needs medical oversight?
*   **Home Prep Effort:** Are you willing to deep-clean your guest room, clear out closet space, and prep detailed neighborhood guides for a stranger?
*   **Schedule Flexibility:** Can your travel dates flex by a day or two to accommodate a sitter's arrival and departure logistics?
*   **Total Financial Ceiling:** Once you add up boarding surcharges versus utilities and platform fees, what is the exact dollar difference?

If your pet is healthy, your home is comfortable, and you enjoy meeting new people, a house-sitter usually wins by a wide margin on a 30-day timeline. 

As you finalize your travel logistics, take a close look at your specific dates and run the numbers on membership platforms versus local kennel quotes to see which option protects your wallet best.
