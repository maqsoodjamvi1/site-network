When I started planning my first trip to Central America, everyone pointed me toward Costa Rica. It is the default answer for US travelers looking for rainforests, wildlife, and volcanoes. But when I actually sat down to price out flights, accommodation, and daily expenses, Costa Rica sticker shock hit hard. That is when a seasoned traveler pointed me just one country south. 

Panama gave me everything I wanted from a tropical vacation—biodiversity, outdoor adventure, distinct cultures, and modern infrastructure—at a significantly lower cost. If you are trying to stretch your vacation dollars without sacrificing comfort or safety, trading Costa Rica for Panama is the smartest budget move you can make. Here is how to tackle it, specifically by dividing your time between the high-energy capital and the misty mountain highlands of Boquete.

## Why Panama Costs Less Than Costa Rica

Costa Rica's massive tourism boom over the last two decades has driven up prices, especially in popular zones like La Fortuna and Manuel Antonio. Panama, on the other hand, flies slightly under the radar for mainstream US tourists, keeping prices grounded in local economic realities rather than inflated tourist bubbles.

Furthermore, Panama uses the US dollar (officially called the Balboa, but it is strictly pegged 1:1 to the US greenback, and US paper currency is used everywhere). This saves you a small fortune in currency exchange fees and mental math. You will not lose three percent every time you pull cash from an ATM or swipe a card. 

## Start in Panama City: Modern Comfort on a Backpacker Budget

Fly into Tocumen International Airport (PTY), which is a major regional hub. Because so many flights route through here, you can often find competitive airfares from major US hubs compared to smaller Central American airports. 

Once you are in the city, skip the expensive taxis and download the local ride-share apps or use the metro. The Panama City Metro is spotless, incredibly safe, and costs around 35 cents a ride. It connects the modern financial district directly to historic Casco Viejo and the fringes of the metro park.

For lodging, look for boutique hostels or budget hotels in El Cangrejo or Casco Viejo. In El Cangrejo, I stayed in a private room with air conditioning and fast Wi-Fi for under $45 a night. For food, bypass the tourist-trap restaurants lining the waterfront plazas. Instead, head to local cafeterias serving *comida corriente*—heaping plates of rice, beans, stewed chicken or beef, and plantains for about $4 to $6. 

To see the Panama Canal without paying for an overpriced guided tour, take a public bus or cheap ride-share to the Miraflores Locks. While the visitor center has an entrance fee, it is a fraction of the cost of booking an all-inclusive cruise transit, and you still get to watch massive container ships maneuver through the chambers.

## Head to Boquete: The Mountain Escape That Beats Monteverde

After a few days of skyline views and humid coastal air, catch an overnight long-distance bus or a cheap domestic flight to David, followed by a $3 shared shuttle up to Boquete. 

Boquete is Panama's answer to Costa Rica's Monteverde Cloud Forest, but it is much easier on the wallet. Nestled in a green valley beneath an extinct volcano, Boquete is famous for its coffee plantations, cool mountain air, and dramatic hiking trails. 

Here is how to experience Boquete cheaply:
* **Hike the Sendero de los Quetzales for free (or cheap):** While guided tours cost $50 to $80, you can take a local $2 public bus to the ranger station at the trailhead and hike the cloud forest independently. Keep your eyes open for the elusive, bright-green resplendent quetzal.
* **Do a DIY coffee tour:** Instead of booking a $60 multi-estate excursion, take a local bus to a nearby farm like Café Ruiz or Finca Lerida, order a cup of Geisha coffee straight from the source, and ask a barista to point out the drying patios. 
* **Eat at local *fondas*:** Look for small, family-run eateries where local workers grab lunch. You can easily get a filling breakfast of eggs, cheese, tortillas, and fresh fruit juice for $3.

## Making the Logistics Work

Getting between Panama City and Boquete is straightforward. You can book a 50-minute domestic flight on Air Panama for around $60 to $80 each way if you book in advance, saving you an eight-hour bus ride. If you prefer the bus, the long-distance coach terminal at Albrook in Panama City offers clean, air-conditioned night buses to David for less than $20. 

As you plan out the specifics of your itinerary, booking your inter-city transit and first-night accommodations ahead of time locks in the lowest rates and keeps your daily spending predictable.
