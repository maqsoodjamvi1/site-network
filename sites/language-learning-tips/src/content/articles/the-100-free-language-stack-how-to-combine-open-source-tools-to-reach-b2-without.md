Learning a language to a professional, independent level—what the European framework calls B2—usually comes with a hefty price tag. Subscription apps, private tutors, and physical textbooks add up fast. But you do not need to enter your credit card number to reach a point where you can watch foreign TV shows without subtitles, read novels, or hold a fluid conversation. 

Over the past few years, the open-source community and public digital archives have grown to rival commercial software. By assembling the right free tools into a daily routine, you can build a complete language stack that takes you from absolute beginner to upper-intermediate entirely for free. Here is how I structure a zero-dollar B2 study routine using open-source and public-domain resources.

## Step 1: The Spaced-Repetition Core with Anki

If you want to reach B2, you need to remember thousands of words. Flashcard apps often charge for cloud sync or extra features, but Anki is 100% free and open-source on desktop and Android (iOS users can use the free web version, AnkiWeb). 

Do not waste time downloading massive pre-made decks with 5,000 random words. Instead, build your deck from the media you consume. When you encounter a new word while reading a free online article, take three seconds to make a flashcard. Include the word in context, an audio clip if possible, and a picture. Review your cards for 15 minutes every morning over coffee. This habit forms the memory anchor for everything else you learn.

## Step 2: Immersion and Reading with Readlang and Standard Ebooks

Grammar books will only get you so far; B2 requires massive input. To bridge the gap between textbook examples and native material, I use a combination of free reading tools.

Standard Ebooks provides meticulously formatted, professionally proofread public-domain literature in English, which makes it easy to find matching foreign-language translations on sites like Project Gutenberg or Wikisource. For contemporary content, I use Readlang, a web app that lets you click on any foreign word in a text to instantly see its translation. 

Here is my exact reading workflow:
* Find a short news article or a chapter of an older novel in your target language.
* Read through it once without stopping, using Readlang to tap unfamiliar words.
* Export those clicked words directly into an Anki flashcard deck.
* Reread the same text the next day to ensure you understand it without looking up definitions.

## Step 3: Listening Comprehension with Language Reactor and Podcasts

Passive listening does not build comprehension nearly as well as active listening. If you watch a foreign film with English subtitles, your brain ignores the audio track. 

To fix this, install Language Reactor, a free browser extension for Chrome and Firefox. It transforms Netflix and YouTube into language-learning labs by displaying dual subtitles (showing both your native language and the target language simultaneously) and allowing you to instantly loop a line of dialogue or slow down the playback speed. 

For offline listening while commuting or doing chores, bypass commercial apps and use a standard, open-source podcast client like AntennaPod. Search for podcasts specifically produced for learners at the intermediate level, or native-level interview shows if you are already at an intermediate stage. Listen to a 10-minute episode three times: once while reading the transcript, once without the transcript, and a third time while trying to shadow (speak along with) the host.

## Step 4: Output and Correction via Lang-8 and Forvo

Getting to B2 means you must eventually produce the language yourself. Without spending money on a tutor, you have to rely on community-driven language exchange platforms.

For writing practice, post journal entries or short essays on Lang-8 or its active community alternatives. Native speakers will correct your grammar and phrasing, usually within a few hours. Always return the favor by correcting posts written in English. 

For pronunciation, use Forvo, a crowdsourced pronunciation dictionary. If you are unsure how a specific conjugated verb sounds in conversational speed, type it into Forvo to hear native speakers from different regions pronounce it naturally. Record your own voice reading the same sentence and compare the cadence.

Reaching B2 without spending money requires discipline because you are acting as your own curriculum designer. You have to actively hunt down the articles, manage your flashcard reviews, and seek out your own speaking partners.
