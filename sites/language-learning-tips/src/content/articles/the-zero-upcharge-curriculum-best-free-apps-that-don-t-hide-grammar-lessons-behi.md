There is a specific kind of frustration reserved for adult language learners. You download a popular app, spend twenty minutes setting up your profile, and feel like you are finally making progress. Then, you click on a lesson about verb conjugations or past-tense markers, and a bright yellow paywall slams down. Suddenly, understanding *why* a sentence is structured a certain way costs fourteen dollars a month. 

Most major platforms treat grammar like a luxury add-on. They feed you endless vocabulary flashcards for free, but make you pay if you want to know the mechanics behind the language. For adults who want to understand how a sentence actually works rather than just memorizing phrases by rote, this model falls flat. 

Fortunately, several completely free resources take a different approach. They build structural rules directly into their core lessons without hitting you with an upgrade prompt halfway through.

## Why Free Apps Usually Hide Grammar (And How to Spot the Exceptions)

Language apps are expensive to run, so monetization is a given. The standard industry trick is the "vocab trap." It is cheap to program picture-to-word matching and simple multiple-choice quizzes, so apps give those away. They hold back structural explanations, verb tables, and syntax notes because they know serious learners will eventually pay to get them.

When looking for a genuinely free curriculum, you need to look past the app store ratings and check how the lessons are sequenced. A true zero-upcharge app weaves sentence patterns into its daily exercises. You should not have to Google a grammar rule on a separate browser tab just to understand why a word changed its ending in lesson three.

## Duolingo’s Free Tier: What You Actually Get Now

Duolingo is the elephant in the room. Everyone tries it, and almost every adult learner eventually hits its limits. If you are using the free version today, your experience depends entirely on how you study. 

The good news is that the core tree—the actual path of lessons—is still free. You can complete entire language trees from start to finish without entering a credit card. The bad news is that Duolingo has steadily stripped away its traditional grammar tips sections on mobile. 

If you want to use Duolingo without paying, change your device habits. Do your lessons on a desktop computer through a web browser instead of the mobile app. The desktop version still features desktop-only "Guidebooks" at the start of most units that explicitly explain sentence structure, gender rules, and conjugation patterns before you start clicking. If you rely solely on the phone app, you will miss these explanations entirely and be forced to guess patterns through trial and error.

## Busuu and Memrise: The Fine Print on "Free"

It helps to know what other popular options hide behind their paywalls so you do not waste an evening downloading software that will immediately ask for money.

Busuu is famous for its clean interface and native-speaker feedback, but its free tier is essentially a teaser. While you can do basic vocabulary units for free, structural lessons and official unit tests are locked behind Busuu Premium. It is a fantastic platform if you have the budget, but it does not fit the zero-upcharge model.

Memrise relies heavily on user-generated flashcard decks. While the official Memrise-created courses have added more subscription prompts over the last couple of years, the platform remains one of the best places to find community-made grammar drills. If you search Memrise for a specific textbook's vocabulary or a dedicated conjugation deck, you can bypass the main app's paywall entirely by using their desktop web browser interface.

## Open-Source and Public Projects That Cost Nothing

Some of the best grammar resources are not flashy venture-capital-backed apps. They are public-interest projects or open-source tools funded by grants or donations. 

The Foreign Service Institute (FSI) and Defense Language Institute (DLI) courses, originally developed for US government diplomats and military personnel, are now entirely in the public domain. Websites like FSI Languages compile these complete audio and text curricula into free, mobile-friendly formats. There are no ads, no subscriptions, and no hidden fees because there is no company trying to sell you anything. 

The downside is that FSI materials look like scanned textbooks from the 1970s. They are dry, academic, and require discipline. But if you want to know the exact mechanics of a language's case system without a cartoon owl nagging you to buy more "hearts," these public archives offer the most thorough structural explanations available anywhere online.

## How to Build a Zero-Dollar Curriculum Today

Relying on free tools means you have to act as your own curriculum designer. You cannot just open an app and let an algorithm dictate your day. 

To make this work, pick one primary source for structural rules—such as a public-domain textbook or a desktop-accessed platform like Duolingo’s web version—and pair it with a free flashcard app like Anki for long-term vocabulary retention. Spend twenty minutes a day reading the structural explanation, write out three original sentences by hand in a physical notebook, and test yourself on the vocabulary the next morning.
