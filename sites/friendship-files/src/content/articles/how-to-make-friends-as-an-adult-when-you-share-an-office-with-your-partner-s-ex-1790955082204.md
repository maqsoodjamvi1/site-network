Sharing a workspace with your partner’s ex is a very specific kind of modern awkwardness. Maybe you both work in the same co-working loft, or perhaps your partner runs a small business and you ended up helping out in an office where the ex is still a key employee. Whatever the exact layout, you walk past someone every day who shares a deep history with the person you love. 

When you are in that environment, making new friends feels complicated. You might worry about office politics, oversharing, or how your workplace relationships will look to your partner. But feeling isolated in a shared office space is a fast track to burnout. You need allies, lunch buddies, and professional confidants. Here is how I navigated the strange waters of making friends when my desk was fifty feet away from my partner’s college sweetheart, and how you can do it too.

## Separate Your Professional Identity from Your Domestic Life

The biggest mistake I made in my first three months at the shared office was acting like an extension of my relationship rather than an individual employee. When you share a space with an ex, it is easy to feel like everyone is watching you for signs of insecurity, jealousy, or territorial behavior. 

To make genuine friends, you have to establish yourself as your own person first. Focus on building connections based on your actual job duties, your industry interests, or your daily workflow. Ask the graphic designer down the hall for feedback on a slide deck, or invite the operations manager to grab coffee to talk about the new inventory software. 

When people see you as a competent professional with your own goals, you stop being "the new partner" in their minds and start being a coworker they actually want to chat with. Keep conversation topics grounded in the present workday rather than the history of the building or who used to sit at your desk.

## Curate Your Circle Outside the Immediate Room

If your office is small and your partner's ex is in your direct line of sight, your immediate vicinity might not be the best breeding ground for deep friendships. You need to expand your radius. 

Look for connection points just outside your immediate department or floor. 
* Join the rotation for the Thursday afternoon food truck runs in the building lobby.
* Sign up for the company kickball team or cross-departmental trivia night where the roster mixes people from different floors.
* Find the "remote workers who came into the office today" and stake out a communal lunch table in the breakroom rather than eating at your desk.
* Introduce yourself to people who work in entirely different companies if you are in a co-working space.

By casting a wider net, you remove the heavy pressure of trying to befriend the exact people who share your immediate air space. You give yourself room to breathe and find people who have zero baggage regarding your relationship history.

## Navigate the "Ex" Elephant in the Room Gracefully

At some point, someone in the office is going to mention the history. They might make an awkward joke, or ask a probing question about how you all manage to work in the same building. How you handle these moments dictates whether you become approachable or marked as dramatic.

When the topic comes up, keep your response remarkably boring. A simple, "We’re all adults, and we make it work so we can focus on the projects," is usually enough to shut down gossip while remaining polite. 

If you do click with a coworker and want to build a real friendship, you will eventually need to talk about it without turning into a venting session. My rule of thumb: never vent about the ex to anyone who works in that same office. Save your frustration for your friends outside of work, your therapist, or your partner. Keeping your workplace friendships clean and free of office gossip protects your reputation and ensures your new friends feel safe hanging out with you.

## Transition to Out-of-Office Hangouts

Once you find a coworker you genuinely enjoy—someone you talk books with, or grab coffee with every Tuesday—the next step is moving the friendship beyond the office walls. This is where things can feel tricky if your partner's ex is still lingering in your mental background.

Start with low-stakes, daytime invitations. Ask your coworker if they want to grab a quick pastry at the bakery down the street before the morning shift starts, or suggest walking to a nearby park to eat lunch together on a nice day. Once you have established a rapport outside the physical office building, graduating to happy hours or weekend dinners feels completely natural. 

Taking these steps helps you reclaim your workspace. You stop walking into the building with a knot in your stomach and start looking forward to seeing the people you've chosen to connect with. As you figure out your next steps for building a stronger support system in and out of the workplace, consider how your daily boundaries can support your peace of mind.
