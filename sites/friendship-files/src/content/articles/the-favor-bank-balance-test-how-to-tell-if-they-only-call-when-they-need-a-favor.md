We have all had that sinking feeling. Your phone lights up across the kitchen counter, and instead of feeling a little spark of excitement to chat, a knot forms in your stomach. You look at the caller ID, and you already know what is coming. They aren't calling to see how your weekend went or to grab a Tuesday coffee. They are calling because they need something. 

Maybe they are moving this weekend and need a pair of strong backs to carry a sofa. Maybe they need a ride to the airport at 5:00 AM on a Monday, or someone to watch their cat while they go out of town. 

When a friendship starts feeling less like a mutual bond and more like an unpaid internship as an errand runner, it is time to check the ledger. Here is how to use the Favor-Bank Balance Test to figure out if you are dealing with a reciprocal friend or just an opportunistic acquaintance.

## The Three Accounts: Overdrawn, Even, and Ahead

Think of every friendship as having a shared bank account. Deposits look like emotional support, showing up when you are sick, celebrating your wins, or doing someone a solid just because. Withdrawals look like asking for rides, borrowing money, or needing a shoulder to cry on after a breakup.

In a healthy friendship, the balance bounces around. Sometimes you lean on them hard through a messy divorce or a job loss; other times, they need you to help them plan a surprise party. But over the course of a year, the ledger roughly evens out. 

The "Favor-Bank Only" dynamic looks completely different. In this account, you are the only one making deposits, while the other person treats your good nature like an endless ATM. They make regular, heavy withdrawals, but when you walk up to the machine to ask for a little support, your card gets declined. 

## Look at the Initiation Ratio

Grab your phone right now and open your text message history with this person. Scroll back through the last ten conversations. Don't look at the content yet—just look at who started the chat. 

Did you initiate eight out of those ten times? More importantly, look at *why* they started the other two. Did they text out of the blue just to send a funny meme or ask how your doctor's appointment went? Or did their opening message start with, "Hey! Quick question..." or "Are you doing anything on Saturday?"

People who value you as a person check in when they want nothing from you. People who value you as a utility only reach out when an obstacle arises in their own life that they are trying to avoid handling themselves. 

## Run the 'No' Experiment

The absolute fastest way to pass the Favor-Bank Balance Test is to say no to a request. You do not need to be mean, and you do not need to make up a dramatic lie. Just use a polite, direct decline. 

"Ah, I can't help you move this weekend, I have plans!" or "I'm slammed at work this week, so I can't drive you to the airport."

Pay extreme attention to what happens in the next forty-eight hours. A true friend might sound momentarily disappointed, but they will bounce back, respect your boundary, and keep treating you normally. 

An opportunistic taker, however, will show their hand immediately. They might try to guilt-trip you ("Oh, come on, I really thought I could count on you"), get huffy, or—most tellingly—suddenly ghost you entirely until the next time they need something fixed. If their warmth is entirely dependent on your compliance, they never liked you as a friend; they liked what you could do for them.

## Shift From 'Yes' to 'Let's See'

If you aren't ready to completely cut ties or have a heavy confrontation, you can reset the dynamic by changing your availability. Stop being instantly accessible. 

When they text asking for a favor, do not reply within two minutes. Let an hour go by. When you do respond, remove the knee-jerk urgency from your own life to accommodate theirs. If they realize they can no longer treat you like a same-day delivery service, they will often go find an easier target, which quietly solves your problem for you.

Deciding what to do next with a one-sided relationship requires a clear head and a look at your own bandwidth. Figuring out where to draw the line between being a supportive friend and being taken advantage of is the first step toward reclaiming your time and energy.
