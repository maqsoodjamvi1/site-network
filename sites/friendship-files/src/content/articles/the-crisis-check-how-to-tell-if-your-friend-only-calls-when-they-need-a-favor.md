We have all had that sinking feeling. Your phone lights up with a text from someone you used to be close with, and instead of a wave or a quick "how have you been?" it starts with: *Hey! Quick question, are you free this weekend? I need a massive favor.* 

At first, you brush it off. Friends help friends move apartments, housesit, or look over a resume. But after the fourth time this month—and zero check-ins in between—you start to wonder if you are actually a friend, or just an unpaid personal assistant with a high emotional tolerance. 

Figuring out where the line is between a needy friend and a user can be tricky. When you are deep into a relationship history, you tend to make excuses for people. Here is how to run a practical crisis check on the dynamic to see if your friend only calls when they need something.

## The Ratio Test: Look at the Last Ten Messages

Memory is notoriously unreliable when we are annoyed. To get an honest read on the situation, open your messaging app and scroll back through the last ten interactions. Do not rely on how you *feel* the relationship is going; look at the raw data. 

Who initiated the last five conversations? If every single recent thread starts with them asking for a ride to the airport, a recommendation for a client, or a shoulder to cry on after a breakup—followed by radio silence the moment their problem is solved—you have your answer. 

True friendship runs on a two-way street of sharing mundane updates, sharing memes, and asking about each other's lives just for the sake of it. If your chat history looks like a customer service log where you are always the one providing the solution, the balance is gone.

## The Emergency Brake: Test the Waters with a Boundary

The easiest way to diagnose a one-sided friendship is to gently put on the brakes. You do not need to start a massive confrontation or send a dramatic text breaking up with them. Instead, simply say no to the next minor request.

When they text asking if you can watch their dog or help them move a couch, try replying with: *Ah, I can’t make that weekend work, things are super hectic on my end!* 

Pay close attention to how they respond. A healthy friend will say: *No worries at all, hope you have a good weekend!* A user, on the other hand, will often react in one of two specific ways:
* They will push past your boundary, guilt-tripping you or acting shocked that you aren't immediately available.
* They will immediately stop replying, vanishing from your phone until the next time they need a favor.

If their interest in you evaporates the second you stop being useful, they were never really interested in you as a person.

## The Inverse Check: What Happens When You Need Them?

Think back over the last year. When you had a bad day at work, a medical scare, or just wanted someone to grab a casual Tuesday coffee with, did this person show up for you? 

Often, people who only call for favors are masters of the vanishing act when the roles are reversed. If you text them that you are going through a rough patch and they reply with a thumbs-up emoji before immediately pivoting the conversation back to their own schedule, take note. 

A transactional friendship only serves one party. If you are pouring emotional, physical, or mental labor into someone who disappears the moment you ask for five minutes of support, you are carrying the weight of a connection all by yourself.

## How to Decide Your Next Move

Realizing a friend is using you doesn't necessarily mean you have to issue an ultimatum or block their number tomorrow. Depending on how much energy you want to invest, you have a few practical paths forward. You can choose to downgrade the relationship to an acquaintance level—stop rushing to answer their texts within five minutes and stop offering heavy favors. Alternatively, you can have a direct, calm conversation about how the dynamic feels one-sided lately. 

Deciding how to handle a draining relationship takes careful thought, especially when you are weighing the history you share against the reality of how they treat you right now. Take a moment to reflect on what you need out of your social circle moving forward before you decide your next steps.
