Have you ever noticed that a certain friend is suddenly available for a coffee date, a happy hour, or a weekend hike, but only during very specific, predictable windows? Maybe they text you out of the blue on a Tuesday afternoon asking to grab dinner, but when you suggest Friday night, they go quiet or say they are busy with their significant other. 

Over the years, I have learned to pay attention to these patterns. Friendships naturally shift when people get into serious relationships, but there is a big difference between a friend who is busy building a life with a partner and a friend who is using you to fill the lonely hours when their partner is out of town, working late, or playing poker. 

If you have a nagging gut feeling that you are serving as a placeholder, you need a concrete way to check the data. That is why I started running what I call the Calendar Asymmetry Audit. 

## Run the Three-Month Lookback

Stop guessing and look at your actual calendar, text threads, and photo history from the past ninety days. Do not rely on your general impression of how often you hang out, because our brains tend to smooth over uncomfortable truths. 

Scroll back through your messaging history with this specific friend and look at two things: who initiated the hangouts, and *when* those hangouts actually happened. 

Map them out mentally or on a piece of paper. You are looking for a stark division. Did you hang out exclusively on Tuesday lunch breaks, Sunday mornings before their partner woke up, or during the exact weeks their significant other traveled for a work conference? If every single time you see them conveniently aligns with a gap in their primary relationship, you are looking at a scheduling convenience, not a mutual friendship. 

## Test the Prime-Time Boundaries

The easiest way to confirm your suspicions is to test the prime-time boundaries of their schedule. This does not mean playing games or being manipulative; it simply means extending invitations for times when a committed partner is typically around.

Invite this friend to a Saturday night birthday dinner, a holiday gathering, or a weekend trip a month in advance. A normal friend with a partner will either say yes, or they will say no because of a prior commitment, but they will actively offer an alternative date to make it up to you. 

When you are dealing with a part-time friend, the response looks very different. You will usually get a vague deflection ("Things are so crazy right now!") or a flat refusal with zero effort to reschedule. Yet, three days later, that same friend might text you asking if you want to grab a quick drink on Thursday because their partner is heading out of town. That contrast is your answer.

## Shift Your Availability Matrix

Once you spot the pattern, the most practical step you can take is to change how you show up. You do not need to deliver a dramatic speech or end the friendship with a text message. Instead, simply stop being on-call for their convenience.

Start treating your own time with the same respect you give to theirs. If they text you on a Friday at 4:00 PM asking if you want to hang out tonight because their plans fell through, look at your actual evening. If you were planning to stay home and watch a movie in sweatpants, that still counts as plans. 

- Politely decline last-minute convenience invites when you know they only arose because a partner is busy elsewhere.
- Redirect the invitation to a time that actually works for a real friendship, such as a proper weekend brunch two weeks out.
- Observe how they react when you stop acting as an on-demand emotional band-aid for their temporary solitude.

When you stop being available exclusively on the rebound from their primary relationship, one of two things will happen. Either they will step up and start making genuine, advance-planning effort to include you in their life, or they will fade away because you are no longer serving your functional purpose in their routine. Both outcomes are a win for you, freeing up your mental energy for people who actually value your presence seven days a week.
