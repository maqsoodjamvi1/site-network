If you are sitting on your couch scrolling through social media and suddenly realize that a close friend bought a house, got engaged, or switched careers two weeks ago without telling you, the sting hits immediately. You wonder how you missed it, and then the sinking realization sets in: you are always the last to know.

I call this pattern the Life Event Notification Test. It is one of the most reliable ways to figure out if you are stuck in a one-sided friendship. When someone routinely drops major milestones into a group chat or posts them publicly before texting you privately, it is rarely an accidental oversight. It is usually a symptom of an imbalanced dynamic where you are holding up your end of the relationship, but they have quietly checked out of theirs.

## Recognizing the Pattern of Delayed Disclosures

We all lead busy lives, and sometimes friends get wrapped up in their own bubbles. A missed text or a delayed phone call happens to everyone. But there is a distinct difference between a busy friend and a pattern of exclusion. 

Think back over the last year of your friendship. When your friend achieved something significant, how did you find out? Did they call you the evening it happened, or did you see it on Instagram days later? Now, look at the reverse. When you got a promotion or went through a breakup, did they actively check in on you, or did they barely acknowledge it? 

One-sided friendships often disguise themselves as easygoing connections. Your friend might be warm and fun when you hang out one-on-one, making you feel like things are fine. But the Life Event Notification Test cuts through the pleasantries. It measures intimacy by access to real-time information. If you only get news when it is public consumption for everyone else, you are an audience member in their life, not a confidante.

## Why We Make Excuses for Them

When we realize we are at the bottom of a friend's contact list, our first instinct is usually to make excuses for them. I have been guilty of this myself, telling myself that my friend was just stressed, overwhelmed by wedding planning, or naturally bad at texting. 

Making excuses keeps us stuck in limbo. It prevents us from addressing the real issue: mutual investment. If you are constantly initiating coffee dates, remembering their dog's birthday, and asking deep questions about their life, but they cannot manage a quick heads-up about a major life change, the math simply does not add up. 

To break this cycle, you need to look at concrete behaviors rather than potential intentions. Here are a few red flags that usually accompany the delayed notification pattern:
* They only reach out when they need a favor, a recommendation, or venting space for their own problems.
* Conversations immediately pivot back to them whenever you try to share your own news or updates.
* They seem defensive or distracted when you ask basic, supportive questions about their life because they are used to keeping you at arm's length.
* You realize that if you stopped texting first, weeks or months would pass without hearing from them.

## Shifting Your Energy and Protecting Your Peace

Passing the Life Event Notification Test in reverse—meaning you stop notifying them of your own milestones—is often the next logical step. This is not about playing games or being petty; it is about matching energy. When you stop offering deep personal access to someone who only offers you surface-level updates, your social bandwidth opens up for people who actually reciprocate.

Try scaling back your outreach for a month. Do not ghost them entirely, but match their level of investment. If they send a casual meme, send one back. But stop planning surprises, stop asking for deep check-ins, and stop treating them as an inner-circle friend. Pay attention to how long it takes them to notice the shift. Often, in a truly one-sided dynamic, they will not even realize you have stepped back because they were barely paying attention to begin with.

Evaluating where you stand with a friend can feel uncomfortable, but it is also deeply freeing. You only have so much emotional energy to give, and it deserves to be spent on people who are genuinely glad to have you in their corner. As you think about how to adjust your social circle moving forward, looking at the practical steps for setting boundaries and investing in reciprocal relationships is a great place to start.
