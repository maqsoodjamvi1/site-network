Adult friendships rarely end in a dramatic explosion. More often, they fade away because one person quietly stops doing the heavy lifting while the other keeps pulling the cart. You might start noticing a strange, hollow feeling after hanging out, or realize that you are always the one initiating text threads, proposing dinner dates, and remembering birthdays. 

When you have known someone for years, it is easy to excuse this shift. You tell yourself they are going through a stressful season at work, or that parenting has consumed their calendar. But when months stretch on and the imbalance becomes the permanent default, doubt creeps in. You need a way to figure out if this is a temporary slump or a fundamental shift in the dynamic. 

## What Is the Reciprocity Drop-Off?

In a healthy adult friendship, the balance of effort swings back and forth like a pendulum. Sometimes you carry the logistical weight because your friend is dealing with a crisis; other times, they step up for you. A "reciprocity drop-off" happens when the pendulum stops swinging and permanently parks on your side of the court. 

You find yourself doing 90 percent of the emotional and scheduling labor. You ask about their life, listen to their venting, and remember the details of their medical appointments or career updates. Meanwhile, when you share something important, they pivot the conversation back to themselves within two sentences. 

## The Two-Week Pullback Experiment

If you suspect your friendship has quietly turned one-sided, stop guessing and test the waters. The Reciprocity Drop-Off Test is simple: completely stop initiating contact for two full weeks. 

Do not text them "good morning," do not share funny memes, and do not propose weekend plans. Go cold on the outreach, but keep your responses warm and polite if they happen to reach out to you. 

When I tried this with a longtime college friend, the results were jarring. Two weeks turned into six weeks, and I heard absolute silence. No check-ins, no "how are you doing?", nothing. It hurt, but the silence gave me the exact data I needed. It proved that the friendship only existed because I was holding it together with duct tape and wishful thinking. 

## Reading the Results of the Test

What happens during those two weeks tells you everything you need to know about where you stand. 

*   **Scenario A: Total Silence.** If two weeks pass with zero communication from them, the relationship was entirely dependent on your labor. They were not busy; they simply did not feel the need to reach out because you were always there to fill the gap.
*   **Scenario B: A Late, Casual Check-in.** If they finally text on day twelve saying, "Hey stranger, been busy!", they noticed your absence but didn't prioritize fixing it. They were comfortable letting you drop out of sight for a while.
*   **Scenario C: Genuine Concern.** If they reach out within a few days, notice your quietness, and ask if everything is okay, the reciprocity is still alive. They value your presence in their daily life.

## How to Handle the Truth Once You Know It

Spotting a one-sided friendship hurts, but it also frees up your calendar and your emotional bandwidth. You no longer have to spend mental energy crafting texts that get left on "read" for three days. You can redirect that care toward people who match your energy and actually enjoy your company. 

Before you write them off completely, you have a choice to make. You can either let the friendship quietly downgrade into a casual acquaintance status—where you only see them at group events—or you can have a direct, non-accusatory conversation about the shift in dynamics. If you choose to talk, focus on "I" statements, such as, "I've been feeling like our connection has become a bit one-sided lately, and I miss how it used to feel."

Deciding what to do next requires looking honestly at the results of your pullback experiment and figuring out how much energy you actually want to invest moving forward.
