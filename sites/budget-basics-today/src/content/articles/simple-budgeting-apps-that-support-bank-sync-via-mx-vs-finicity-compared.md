When you are shopping for a budget app, the feature that usually makes or breaks the experience is bank syncing. Manually typing in every coffee run or grocery trip gets old fast. If you want your transactions to flow into your dashboard automatically, the app you choose relies on underlying data aggregators. Two of the biggest names powering these connections behind the scenes are MX and Finicity. 

Understanding how these aggregators work can help you pick an app that actually keeps your accounts connected instead of forcing you to log in with two-factor authentication every three days. Here is a practical look at how MX and Finicity compare, and what it means for your daily budgeting routine.

## What Are MX and Finicity?

Neither MX nor Finicity is a budgeting app you download directly from the app store. Instead, they are financial technology infrastructure companies. Think of them as the plumbing that connects your bank account to consumer-facing apps like Monarch Money, Copilot, YNAB, or Quicken Simplifi. 

When you click "Link Account" in a budgeting app and enter your bank credentials, a secure window powered by MX or Finicity handles the communication. They fetch your transaction history, account balances, and routing details so your budget stays up to date. 

The main difference lies in their origins and partnerships. Finicity, which is owned by Mastercard, has traditionally leaned heavily into deep credit decisioning and data verification networks, making it exceptionally good at tapping into traditional, risk-averse financial institutions. MX, on the other hand, was built heavily around user experience and data enhancement, focusing on cleaning up messy merchant names so your coffee shop purchase doesn't show up as a random string of numbers and store codes.

## Reliability and Bank Coverage in the Real World

If you bank with a major national institution like Chase, Bank of America, or Wells Fargo, both MX and Finicity will connect seamlessly. The real test happens if you use a regional credit union, a digital-only bank, or a specialized investment account.

In my experience testing various apps over the years, Finicity tends to have slightly better historical stability with traditional regional banks and mortgage lenders. Its backend routing through Mastercard gives it robust authorization pathways with older banking infrastructure. 

MX often shines with digital wallets, fintech checking accounts, and modern credit cards. Furthermore, MX places a strong emphasis on data cleansing. If you have ever linked a bank where transactions look like cryptographic puzzles, MX is usually better at translating that raw data into a readable merchant name, such as "Target" instead of "TGT STORE #1405."

However, neither aggregator is 100% immune to dropped connections. Multi-factor authentication (MFA) updates, bank security upgrades, and routine server maintenance will occasionally break your sync regardless of whether your app uses MX or Finicity. 

## Security and Your Privacy

Giving a third-party app permission to read your bank data feels unnerving at first. Both MX and Finicity operate under strict financial regulations and use bank-level encryption, typically 256-bit SSL, to protect your data in transit and at rest. 

Crucially, neither company sells your raw personal financial data to advertisers, and neither can move money out of your accounts—their access is read-only. 

The biggest security difference for you as a user comes down to tokenization. Both networks use modern token-based authorization protocols where possible. This means your actual online banking password is never stored by the budgeting app or the aggregator; instead, the bank issues a secure token that allows the app to fetch read-only data. If you have a choice during the linking process, look for institutions that support API-based connections rather than credential-scraping, as these are faster and far less likely to break.

## How to Choose Your Next Budgeting App Based on Syncing

Do not buy a budgeting app based solely on whether it uses MX or Finicity. Instead, evaluate the app based on how it handles the data once it arrives. However, you can use aggregator knowledge as a troubleshooting tool.

If you belong to a small, local credit union, check if the budgeting app you want offers a choice of aggregators. Some advanced apps allow you to switch between MX and Finicity if one fails to connect with your specific bank. 

To figure out which system works best for your specific setup before committing to a paid subscription, take advantage of free trials. When you sign up, immediately link your most stubborn account—the local credit union or specialized rewards credit card that always gives other apps trouble. If the connection holds steady during the trial period without requiring daily re-authentication, you have found a winner.
