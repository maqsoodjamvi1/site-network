Managing money when you live in the United States but earn in Euros, or when you travel constantly and pay bills in three different currencies, breaks most traditional budgeting tools. Standard US-centric apps assume your paycheck hits a Bank of America checking account in US Dollars, your rent is in USD, and your grocery store speaks English and charges USD. 

When you introduce foreign exchange rates, cross-border transfer fees, and accounts held in foreign banks into the mix, standard budgeting setups usually crash or require tedious manual math. 

Over the last few years of freelancing with international clients and managing accounts across borders, I have tested nearly every personal finance tool claiming to handle multi-currency tracking. Here is how the leading simple budgeting apps actually compare for US expats and freelancers, focusing on what works without requiring a degree in accounting.

## Why Standard US Budgeting Apps Fail at Multi-Currency

Most popular budgeting platforms rely on automated account aggregators like Plaid to pull transactions into your dashboard. Plaid works brilliantly for Chase, Capital One, and local credit unions. However, it largely fails when you try to connect an account with Revolut UK, a digital bank based in Europe, or an Australian dollar account. 

Even if an app manages to pull foreign transactions, it often converts them to USD using a static exchange rate or messes up the historical value on the day the transaction actually occurred. If you budget dynamically, seeing your grocery bill fluctuate wildly next month just because the exchange rate shifted—rather than because you spent more euros—makes it impossible to track your actual spending habits.

To manage multiple currencies without losing your mind, you need software that either handles multi-currency natively or allows manual tracking without penalizing you for it.

## YNAB (You Need A Budget): Best for Zero-Based Multi-Currency Planners

YNAB is famous for its strict zero-based budgeting method, where every dollar gets a specific job. What many users do not realize is that YNAB handles foreign currencies remarkably well, provided you set up your accounts correctly.

When you add an account in YNAB, you can designate its currency as USD, EUR, GBP, CAD, and dozens of others. If you live in Spain part of the year and spend Euros from a Wise account, you can create a dedicated Euro account inside your primary YNAB budget. 

The catch is how YNAB handles transfers between currencies. If you move $1,000 USD to a Euro account to pay European expenses, YNAB treats it as an outflow from one category and an inflow to another, requiring you to manually calculate and input the exchange rate and any transfer fees at that exact moment. 

**The Verdict:** If you are dedicated to zero-based budgeting and want strict control over every cent regardless of the currency, YNAB works. It takes setup work, but your data stays accurate.

## Monarch Money: Best for Clean Dashboards and Aggregated Views

Monarch Money has quickly become a favorite alternative for people migrating away from Mint. It offers a modern interface, great collaborative features for couples, and flexible dashboard customization.

For expats and freelancers, Monarch supports international connections better than many competitors through open banking integrations, though your mileage will still vary depending on the specific foreign institution. If your foreign bank does not sync automatically, Monarch lets you create manual accounts and upload CSV statements.

The limitation with Monarch is currency conversion. Monarch normalizes all your accounts into a single base currency (USD for most US expats). While this is fantastic for getting a high-level net worth snapshot, it can obscure the reality of your cash flow if exchange rates swing heavily during the month. 

**The Verdict:** Choose Monarch if your primary goal is tracking your overall net worth and high-level monthly cash flow in USD, and you do not mind if foreign transactions are converted at the current daily rate.

## PocketSmith: Best for Advanced Forecasting and Multi-Currency Power Users

PocketSmith approaches budgeting through a calendar and forecasting lens. It was built from the ground up to handle multiple currencies, making it arguably the most technically capable app for freelancers with global income streams.

In PocketSmith, you can assign a primary currency for your overall profile, but individual accounts can operate in their native currencies. The platform pulls daily exchange rates, meaning your historical data reflects the actual value on the transaction date rather than today's conversion rate. 

Furthermore, PocketSmith lets you look years into the future based on repeating invoices from international clients, recurring subscriptions in foreign currencies, and anticipated tax payments. 

*   **Pros:** Native multi-currency support, excellent calendar view, powerful earning and spending forecasts.
*   **Cons:** The learning curve is steep. The interface feels less polished and more utilitarian than Monarch or YNAB.

**The Verdict:** If you manage income across three or more currencies and need robust forecasting tools to predict your tax liabilities in different countries, PocketSmith justifies the extra time it takes to learn.

## Spendee: Best for Simple, Manual Multi-Currency Tracking

If you do not want to link your bank accounts due to security concerns, privacy, or the simple fact that your foreign bank does not support API connections, wallet-based apps are your best bet. Spendee lets you create separate "wallets" for different currencies.

You can create a USD wallet for your US expenses, a EUR wallet for your European travels, and a crypto wallet if you dabble there. You log transactions manually or upload statements. When you transfer money between your wallets, Spendee prompts you to input the exchange rate and fee. 

**The Verdict:** Spendee is ideal for digital nomads and freelancers who prefer manual entry or want a lightweight app just to track travel expenses without syncing major financial institutions.

## Choosing Your Next Steps

Finding the right tool depends entirely on how much automation you require versus how much manual control you are willing to accept. If you want strict budgeting discipline, go with YNAB. If you want a clean dashboard that aggregates everything into USD automatically, try Monarch. If you need true multi-currency accounting with historical exchange rates, PocketSmith is worth the investment. Take a look at your current mix of foreign accounts, decide whether automated syncing is a dealbreaker, and test out a free trial to see which interface fits your workflow before committing.
