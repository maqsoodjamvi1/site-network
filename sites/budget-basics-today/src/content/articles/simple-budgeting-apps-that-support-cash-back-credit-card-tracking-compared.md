Using cash-back credit cards for everyday expenses is a smart way to squeeze extra value out of purchases you were going to make anyway. But if you aren't careful, the reward-chasing habit can quietly sabotage your monthly budget. When you buy groceries, gas, and utility bills on plastic to earn 2% back, your checking account balance tells a very different story than your credit card balance. 

If you want to earn rewards without accidentally carrying a balance or overspending, you need a budgeting app that handles credit card tracking seamlessly. I have tested dozens of budgeting tools over the years while managing my own household cash-back strategy. Here is how the most popular options compare when you are trying to track spending and collect rewards without losing control.

## How Cash-Back Tracking Breaks Most Budget Apps

The main challenge with cash-back credit cards and budgeting is that credit cards create a floating gap between your spending and your bank account. When you spend $100 on groceries using a cash-back card, you haven't actually lost cash from your checking account yet—you have just created a $100 bill that will come due at the end of the month.

Many basic budgeting apps treat a credit card payment just like any other expense, which completely ruins your category tracking. If you budget $400 for groceries and charge $100 of it, a poorly designed app might log the initial $100 grocery purchase and then log the $100 credit card bill payment as *another* expense, making it look like you spent $200. 

To track cash-back cards effectively, you need an app that either uses zero-based envelope budgeting or cleanly categorizes credit card payments as transfers rather than fresh spending.

## YNAB (You Need A Budget) for Zero-Based Reward Chasers

YNAB is the gold standard for people who want absolute control over their money, and it handles credit cards differently than almost anything else on the market. When you make a $50 purchase on your 2% cash-back card, YNAB automatically moves $50 from your grocery category directly into your credit card payment category. 

The best part about YNAB for reward enthusiasts is how it handles the cash-back itself. When you redeem your cash back as a statement credit, YNAB treats it as "Inflow: Ready to Assign," which you can then put right back into your savings or next month's budget. 

* **Pros:** Bulletproof math, forces you to only spend money you actually have, and handles statement credits cleanly.
* **Cons:** Steep learning curve and a subscription price that requires active budgeting to justify.
* **Best for:** People who pay their card in full every single week and want zero margin for error.

## Copilot for Automated, Visual Trackers

If you prefer a sleek, modern app that links to your accounts and does most of the heavy lifting automatically, Copilot is a strong contender. It uses smart categorization to learn your habits and offers fantastic visual dashboards.

When using Copilot with cash-back cards, the app focuses heavily on your net worth and tracking monthly cash flow. It links seamlessly with major credit card issuers, meaning your rewards balances and pending transactions show up quickly. However, Copilot treats credit cards more like traditional revolving debt tracks rather than strict envelopes. You have to pay closer attention to make sure your monthly payments align with your actual spending categories.

* **Pros:** Gorgeous user interface, fast syncing with banks, and highly customizable categories.
* **Cons:** iOS-first ecosystem (though web and Android options are expanding) and a paid subscription model.
* **Best for:** Visual learners who want automated tracking without spending hours setting up manual rules.

## Empower (Formerly Personal Capital) for the Big Picture

Empower is primarily a wealth-tracking and investment dashboard, but it includes a free budgeting tool that pulls in all your checking, savings, and credit card accounts. 

If your primary goal with cash-back cards is making sure you never miss a payment and can see your net worth tick upward as you earn rewards, Empower works well. It lists all your credit card transactions in one scrolling feed and categorizes them automatically. 

* **Pros:** Completely free to use and provides a fantastic holistic view of your entire financial net worth alongside your monthly spending.
* **Cons:** The budgeting features are basic. It lacks the granular envelope-style controls needed if you struggle with overspending on credit cards.
* **Best for:** High-income earners or established savers who already pay their cards in full and just want a single dashboard to monitor everything.

## Choosing the Right Tool for Your Strategy

To decide which app fits your routine, look at how you currently manage your credit cards. If you tend to lose track of what you have actually spent versus what is sitting in your checking account, a strict envelope system like YNAB will save you from accidental overspending. If you already have strong self-control and just want a clean dashboard to watch your transactions and net worth grow, an automated tool like Copilot or Empower will get the job done with far less manual effort.

Before you commit to a paid subscription, take advantage of free trials to test how each app syncs with your specific bank and credit card rewards structure. Setting up your categories correctly on day one will save you hours of frustration later.
