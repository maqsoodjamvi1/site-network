If you have ever tried to set up a modern budgeting app, you have likely run into Plaid. It is the middleman service most fintech apps use to securely connect your bank account to your phone. For millions of people, Plaid works fine. But you might be reading this because you tried linking your bank and hit a brick wall: your credit union isn't listed, your multi-factor authentication keeps breaking the connection, or you simply prefer not to share your online banking credentials with a third-party aggregator. 

When you want to track your spending without routing your data through Plaid, your options narrow down significantly. Fortunately, you still have choices. Some apps bypass Plaid by building direct application programming interface (API) connections with major financial institutions, while others rely on manual entry or file imports. Let’s look at how these alternatives work and which ones might fit your financial routine.

## Why Some Apps Skip Plaid

Plaid acts as a universal bridge, but building custom bridges directly to banks is becoming more common. When an app uses a direct API connection, it is partnering specifically with financial institutions—often massive banks like Chase, Bank of America, or Wells Fargo—to pull transaction data securely. 

In practice, a direct connection means the bank itself manages the permission layer. You log into your bank’s website or app during setup, check a box allowing that specific budgeting software to view your balances, and generate a secure token. The budgeting app never sees your actual username or password. 

The downside? Direct API connections are expensive and technically complex for software developers to maintain. This is why you will rarely see smaller, independent budgeting apps offer them. They are usually restricted to heavily funded platforms or apps that take a completely different approach to data altogether.

## Apps That Use Direct Connections or Alternative Infrastructure

If you want automated tracking without Plaid, you need to look at specific players in the market. 

Copilot, a sleek iOS and Mac budgeting app, is known for its modern design and smart categorization. While Copilot does use alternative aggregators alongside Plaid to improve reliability for smaller institutions, its primary focus is on high-fidelity, direct-style integrations with major banks. If a specific institution has a robust direct feed, Copilot utilizes it to keep your transactions updating smoothly without the constant re-authentication loops that plague older aggregator setups.

Another route is Monarch Money. Monarch allows multiple data providers behind the scenes—including Plaid, Finicity, and MX. If Plaid fails to connect with your specific bank account, Monarch lets you manually switch the data provider inside your settings dashboard without deleting your history. While it still relies on aggregators, having the ability to toggle away from Plaid gives you a workaround when your bank acts up.

If you want to step away from automated scraping entirely, YNAB (You Need A Budget) offers a hybrid model. YNAB connects to banks through various aggregators, but it is famous for being just as powerful if you use manual entry. Many seasoned YNAB users prefer typing in transactions as they happen at the cash register because it builds immediate awareness of where their money is going. 

## The Manual and CSV Import Alternative

If you want absolute privacy and zero third-party data sharing, the most reliable "non-Plaid" method is manual tracking or CSV importing. This means logging into your bank website once a week, downloading a comma-separated values (CSV) file of your recent transactions, and uploading it into your budgeting software.

*   **Spreadsheets (Google Sheets or Microsoft Excel):** This is the ultimate DIY approach. You control every cell, formula, and category. Free templates are widely available, and your bank data never leaves your own secure spreadsheet environment.
*   **Bank-Provided Tools:** Almost every major bank now has a built-in spending tracker hidden inside its mobile app. Because you are already logged into your bank, these tools use direct internal data feeds with zero external aggregators involved.
*   **Desktop Software:** Traditional offline software like Banktivity or Quicken Classic allows you to download statements directly from your bank's website and import them manually, completely cutting out cloud aggregators.

## Choosing Your Next Step

Deciding how to budget without Plaid comes down to a trade-off between convenience and control. If you love automated transaction feeds but hate Plaid specifically, apps that offer multi-provider switching or robust direct institution feeds will save you hours of data entry. If you value privacy above all else, switching to a manual CSV workflow or a dedicated spreadsheet will give you total peace of mind. 

Before committing to a paid subscription, take a moment to look at your primary bank accounts and evaluate how much time you are willing to spend managing your data, then choose the setup that fits your habits.
