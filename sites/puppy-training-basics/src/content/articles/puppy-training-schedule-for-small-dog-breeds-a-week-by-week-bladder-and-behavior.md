Bringing home a tiny puppy like a Chihuahua, Yorkshire Terrier, or Pomeranian is wildly exciting, but the physical reality of raising them can feel overwhelming. When I brought home my three-pound toy poodle mix, I quickly realized that standard puppy advice often fails for miniature breeds. Their bladders are roughly the size of a thimble, their metabolisms run dangerously fast, and their tiny frames mean a fall off the couch can spell disaster. 

If you are preparing to bring home a small puppy or currently have an 8-week-old bundle of energy sleeping in a pen, you need a realistic, week-by-week blueprint. Here is the exact schedule I use to manage potty training, socialization, and bite inhibition for tiny dogs during their most crucial developmental window.

## Weeks 8 to 9: The Thimble-Bladder Reality Check

Your first two weeks home are all about survival, routine, and preventing bad habits before they start. At eight weeks old, a small breed puppy cannot physically hold their urine for more than 45 to 60 minutes while awake. 

Your daily schedule must revolve around constant potty trips. Take your puppy outside (or to their indoor pee pad, if that is your chosen system) immediately after they wake up from a nap, fifteen minutes after eating or drinking, and after every vigorous play session. Do not wait for them to sniff or pace; if they have been awake for an hour, pick them up and carry them to the designated spot.

Because small breeds are prone to hypoglycemia (low blood sugar), you cannot let them go long stretches without food. Feed them three to four measured meals a day of high-calorie, small-kibble puppy food. During these first two weeks, focus entirely on name recognition and getting them comfortable wearing a properly fitted, ultra-lightweight puppy harness instead of attaching a leash directly to their fragile neck.

## Weeks 10 to 11: Expanding the World Safely

By week ten, your puppy should have their second round of vaccinations, which means it is time to carefully expand their world without risking their health. Parvovirus is a major threat to small puppies, so avoid high-traffic dog parks or pet store floors. Instead, carry your puppy in your arms or a dedicated sling to busy strip malls, children's parks, and quiet neighborhood streets so they can soak up novel sights and sounds.

House training will still feel like a full-time job. You will likely experience setbacks where your puppy pees on the rug two minutes after coming inside. This is normal. Clean accidents with an enzymatic cleaner to remove the scent, and double down on supervision. If your eyes are not on your puppy, they should be securely contained in an exercise pen or a appropriately sized crate. 

During these weeks, start handling their paws, ears, and mouth daily for a few seconds at a time. Pair these touches with high-value treats like tiny bits of boiled chicken. Small dogs often grow up to hate nail trims because owners wait until the dog is full-grown and fighting back; desensitize them now.

## Weeks 12 to 13: Curing the Tiny Shark Phase

Around three months of age, your puppy's needle-sharp baby teeth will start feeling like a personal vendetta against your ankles. Small dog breeds are notorious for relentless ankle-biting because humans tend to laugh or run away, which turns the behavior into an incredible game.

Stop the game instantly. When your puppy bites your skin, fold your arms, stand completely still, and turn your back. Do not yell, as high-pitched noises can sound like squeaky prey and ramp up their excitement. Once the puppy calms down for three seconds, redirect them to a sturdy chew toy or a textured rubber teething ring. 

At this stage, you can begin stretching out their daytime potty intervals to every 90 minutes. You should also introduce basic impulse control. Ask your puppy to sit before you put their food bowl down or open the door to go outside. Miniature breeds need to learn early that calm behavior is the key to getting what they want.

## Weeks 14 to 16: Building Independence and Confidence

As your puppy approaches the four-month mark, their bladder control will improve slightly, allowing for two-hour intervals between daytime potty breaks. You can also begin teaching true alone time to prevent separation anxiety, a common struggle for velcro small breeds.

Leave your puppy in their exercise pen with a stuffed, frozen puzzle toy for ten minutes while you sit in another room. Gradually increase this time over the next two weeks. This teaches them that you always come back, preventing the frantic barking and scratching that often plague small dogs when left alone.

Continue socializing them to different people, walking surfaces (grass, gravel, metal grates), and gentle sounds. A confident small dog is far less likely to develop fear-based barking or aggression later in life. 

Raising a miniature breed requires patience, a high tolerance for repetition, and a vigilance that larger dog owners rarely have to match.
