If you brought home a six-month-old puppy, or if your own little furball just crossed the threshold into adolescence, you are probably noticing some sudden changes. The sleepy, compliant puppy you remember has likely developed a stubborn streak, selective hearing, and an alarming amount of physical energy. 

When my rescue mix hit six months old, I felt completely unequipped. The easy house-training phase was behind us, replaced by a dog who suddenly forgot how to sit if a squirrel crossed the street. 

The secret to surviving the six-to-twelve-month window isn't harsh discipline. It is consistency, realistic expectations, and treating this age like high school for dogs. Here is a practical, week-by-week blueprint to help you guide your older adolescent dog into adulthood with your sanity intact.

## Months 6 to 7: Solidifying the Basics Under Real-World Distractions

At six months, your dog's brain is flooded with hormones and curiosity. Skills your puppy mastered in the quiet living room will suddenly fall apart outside. This month is about taking known commands and testing them in high-distraction environments.

*   **Move outside:** Practice "sit," "down," and "stay" in the driveway, then move to a quiet park corner, and eventually near a busy sidewalk. 
*   **Proof your recall:** Long lines (15-to-30-foot training leashes) are your best friend here. Practice calling your dog back to you in open fields, rewarding them with high-value treats like real chicken or cheese every single time they check in.
*   **Manage the chewing:** Adolescent teeth are usually settled, but boredom chewing peaks now. Provide sturdy chew toys like filled rubber kongs and swap items immediately if your dog picks up a shoe.

## Months 8 to 9: Managing the Teenage Attitude and Impulse Control

This is often the peak frustration period for owners. Your dog might push boundaries, ignore cues they have known for months, or show new reactivity toward other dogs. 

During these months, focus heavily on impulse control exercises. I taught my dog the "wait" command at every single door—front door, car door, and even before I set his food bowl down. If he rushed forward, the door closed or the bowl went back up on the counter. 

*   **Cap the energy:** Physical walks are no longer enough to tire out an eight-month-old dog. Incorporate mental stimulation like snuffle mats, puzzle toys, and nose-work games where they have to find hidden treats around the house.
*   **Enforce boundaries:** If your dog starts jumping on guests again, go right back to management basics. Put them behind a baby gate when the doorbell rings until they calm down, rather than hoping they will magically make good choices.

## Months 10 to 11: Fine-Tuning Leash Manners and Public Etiquette

By ten months, your dog is approaching full physical size, which makes pulling on the leash a much bigger safety hazard. If your dog is dragging you down the pavement, now is the time to reset your loose-leash walking routine.

Stop moving the second tension appears in the leash. Stand completely still like a tree. Only when your dog looks back at you or steps back to create slack in the line do you move forward again. It makes walks painfully slow for the first two weeks, but your shoulders will thank you by month twelve.

Take your dog to dog-friendly hardware stores or outdoor cafes. Practice having them lie quietly under your chair while distractions swirl around them. This builds environmental stability, teaching them that excitement doesn't mean they get to react to everything they see.

## Month 12: Transitioning to Adulthood and Adult Routines

Hitting the one-year mark does not mean your dog instantly transforms into a calm adult—many breeds continue mental maturation until they are two years old. However, the foundational habits you built over the last six months will start paying off in a major way.

Evaluate your daily routine. Does your dog still need enforced nap times, or can they settle outside of a crate now? Can they handle being left alone for a few hours without destroying couch cushions? 

Celebrate your progress. Adolescent dogs test our patience more than any other age group, but consistency during this specific window shapes the reliable, well-mannered companion you will enjoy for the next decade. 

As you look ahead to maintaining these good habits long-term, establishing a sustainable daily routine for exercise and mental enrichment will keep your newly minted adult dog balanced and happy.
