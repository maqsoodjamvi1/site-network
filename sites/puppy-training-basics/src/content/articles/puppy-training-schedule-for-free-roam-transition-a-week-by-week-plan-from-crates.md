When I brought home my last puppy, my ultimate goal was simple: I wanted a dog who could hang out in the living room without eating the baseboards or chewing up my favorite shoes. But I knew that giving a puppy free roam too early is a recipe for house-training disasters and shredded furniture. 

Transitioning a puppy from total crate and pen confinement to having the run of the house requires a deliberate, step-by-step strategy. Over the years, I have refined a predictable timeline that sets both the dog and the human up for success. Here is the exact week-by-week plan I use to phase out the crate and open up the house.

## Week 1: Lockdown and Mapping the Territory

During the first week, your puppy should have zero free roam. That sounds harsh, but it is the foundation of house training. When my puppy isn't in my direct line of sight, they are in a crate, an exercise pen, or tethered to my belt loop with a lightweight leash. 

Use this week to map out your home's traffic patterns. Watch where your puppy naturally tends to wander, where they try to sneak off to chew, and where puddles might happen. Your job this week is strictly observation and management. Do not test your puppy's reliability yet; assume they will make a mistake if given the chance.

## Weeks 2 and 3: The Hallway Pass (Single-Room Access)

Once your puppy has gone ten consecutive days with zero accidents inside and shows a solid understanding of where to potty outside, you can grant access to your first room. For me, this is usually the kitchen or living room, blocked off by baby gates rather than doors.

During these weeks, practice supervised freedom. If I am folding laundry in the living room, the puppy can roam that specific room. However, if I walk into the kitchen to grab a snack, the puppy either comes with me or goes back behind the baby gate. We are expanding the boundary, but strictly under my supervision. 

## Weeks 4 and 5: Expanding Corridors and Hallways

If your puppy handles single-room access without chewing forbidden items or sneaking off to have an accident behind the sofa, it is time to open up adjacent spaces. If your living room is mastered, open the gate to the adjoining hallway. 

This is usually when boundary-testing begins. My puppies always test me around week four by trying to sneak behind the television stand or test a rug fringe. Keep high-value chews readily available in these new zones. When I see my puppy sniffing a baseboard, I immediately redirect them to a chew toy. Management is still doing ninety percent of the work here.

## Week 6: Micro-Absences and Short Freedoms

By week six, I start testing what happens when I am physically out of the room, but only for very short bursts. I will let the puppy hang out in the open living room while I step into the bathroom for sixty seconds, leaving the door cracked. 

Gradually increase this time to two minutes, then five, then ten. If you return and find a chewed pillow, you have moved too fast and need to shrink their boundary back down for a few days. True free roam requires trust, and trust is built in increments of minutes, not hours.

## Weeks 7 and 8: The Full Open-House Transition

By weeks seven and eight, assuming consistency, your puppy should be ready for the final transition. You can remove the baby gates during waking hours and allow full access to the common areas of your home. 

Even at this stage, I maintain a few non-negotiable rules to protect my progress:
* **The Floor is Clear:** I keep shoes, laundry, and children's toys off the floor so the puppy is never tempted by forbidden items.
* **The Door is Closed:** Bedrooms and bathrooms remain off-limits unless I am actively inside them with the dog.
* **Continuous Monitoring:** Free roam does not mean unsupervised. I still keep an ear open for suspicious silence, which usually means the puppy is getting into something they shouldn't.

Transitioning a puppy to open-house living is entirely about earning privileges through demonstrated reliability. If you move slowly and only expand their boundaries after they prove they are ready, you will skip the chaos and end up with a trustworthy house dog.
