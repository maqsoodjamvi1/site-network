If you are researching side hustles, you have probably noticed that a lot of online gigs feel a bit shadowy. Platforms pop up overnight, pay out through sketchy apps, or ask you to jump through hoops just to get your money. But if you want to keep things strictly above board—especially for tax purposes—you need gigs that use proper formal onboarding. When a company asks you to fill out IRS Form W-9 before you start working, it means they treat you as an independent contractor and will issue you a Form 1099-NEC at the end of the year. 

Dealing with a W-9 is actually a green flag. It tells you the company is a legitimate business that reports its expenses, which usually means consistent work and reliable payment schedules. Over the last few years, I have navigated the 1099 onboarding process for various home-based roles. Here are five legitimate side hustles that require a W-9, what the onboarding looks like, and how to figure out if they fit your schedule.

## 1. Independent Transcription and Captioning
If you type quickly and have a sharp ear, transcription and video captioning are reliable ways to earn money from your kitchen table. Companies that handle corporate audio, legal depositions, or academic interviews usually require contractors to sign a W-9 during the setup phase. 

Unlike gig apps where you click a button and start working instantly, legitimate transcription platforms have an application process. You will typically take a grammar and typing test, and if you pass, you complete your profile by submitting your social security number or EIN via a secure W-9 digital form. Once approved, you log into a dashboard, claim audio files, and type them out. Pay is usually calculated per audio minute rather than your actual typing time, so the faster and more accurate you get, the more profitable this becomes.

## 2. Remote User Testing and Digital Quality Assurance
Tech companies constantly launch new apps, websites, and software updates, and they need real humans to test them for bugs and usability issues. While some quick-survey sites pay via gift cards, higher-end usability testing firms operate on a strict contractor model. 

When you apply to moderate software tests or participate in live video interviews with product managers, the onboarding paperwork often mirrors a traditional freelance contract. Because these projects can pay anywhere from $30 to $100 per test, companies cross the $600 annual threshold quickly, meaning they legally require a W-9 from you to report your earnings. You will usually fill this out through vendor management software like Deel or directly in the testing platform's secure dashboard before you are cleared to claim your first test.

## 3. Specialized Freelance Customer Support
Many mid-sized to enterprise companies outsource their customer service and live chat operations to remote contractors rather than staffing traditional call centers. These roles are vastly different from the automated micro-task sites you see advertised on social media. 

When I onboarded with a specialized support network, the process involved a background check, an automated W-9 submission, and a mandatory two-week training period. You receive official login credentials for ticketing software like Zendesk, and you work scheduled shifts from home. Because you are performing ongoing services for a set hourly rate, the W-9 establishes your legal status as an independent business owner, making you responsible for tracking your own self-employment taxes.

## 4. Online Tutoring and Test Preparation Contracting
If you have expertise in a specific academic subject, standardized test prep, or English as a second language, tutoring agencies provide steady home-based income. The most stable platforms do not just throw students at you; they vet your credentials, conduct mock teaching sessions, and run compliance checks.

During the hiring phase, you will submit your resume, complete a tax interview section, and electronically sign your W-9. This paperwork ensures that the agency can legally report your earnings as professional service fees. Payment is typically deposited via direct deposit on a bi-weekly or monthly schedule, and you receive an official 1099 form every January if you earned over the IRS threshold.

## 5. Independent Search Engine Evaluation
Search engine evaluators look at algorithmic search results, maps queries, and AI-generated outputs to rate them for relevance and accuracy. Major evaluation agencies act as intermediaries for massive tech conglomerates and take compliance very seriously.

The onboarding process for these roles is notoriously thorough. You will sign a Non-Disclosure Agreement (NDA), pass a comprehensive multi-part qualification exam based on a lengthy set of guidelines, and complete your W-9 paperwork through an HR portal. Once onboarded, you log your hours into a timekeeping system up to a weekly cap set by the client. It is quiet, independent work that requires zero phone calls, making it ideal if you prefer focus-heavy tasks.

Navigating 1099 onboarding means you will be responsible for setting aside money for your own federal, state, and self-employment taxes, since no taxes are withheld automatically from your paychecks. Keeping a separate business bank account and tracking your home office deductions makes tax season much simpler.
