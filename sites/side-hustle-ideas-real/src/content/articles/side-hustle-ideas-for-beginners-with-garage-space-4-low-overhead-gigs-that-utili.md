When I first looked at my cluttered two-car garage, I didn't see an extra income stream. I just saw a graveyard for old holiday decorations, a broken lawnmower, and a car that hadn't seen the inside of a shelter in three years. But once I cleared out the middle third of the space, I realized I was sitting on the ultimate low-overhead business asset. 

Most side hustles require renting commercial space or buying expensive inventory right out of the gate. If you own a garage, a driveway, or even a secure carport, you already have the most expensive overhead cost—rent—completely eliminated. Here are four practical, hands-on side hustles you can run out of a home garage with minimal startup cash.

## 1. Local Gear and Bicycle Maintenance

People accumulate a staggering amount of outdoor equipment that they rarely know how to fix. Between mountain bikes, road bikes, lawn mowers, snowblowers, and gas-powered pressure washers, local neighborhoods are full of gear that needs basic tune-ups. 

You do not need to be a certified master mechanic to start. You can learn how to patch tires, adjust shifting cables, replace brake pads, and clean carburetor bowls by watching free video tutorials. 

*   **Startup Gear:** A basic mechanic's tool set, a bike repair stand, a set of hex wrenches, tire levers, and a reliable air compressor. 
*   **How to Launch:** Clear a workbench area near your garage door. Post flyers on community boards, neighborhood Facebook groups, and Nextdoor offering free tire pressure checks to get your first few customers through the door. 
*   **The Math:** Charging $45 for a standard bicycle tune-up that takes an hour can easily net you $90 to $135 on a Saturday morning, minus about $5 in consumables like grease and patch kits.

## 2. Peer-to-Peer Storage Rental

If your garage is mostly empty and you want a completely hands-off hustle, you can rent out square footage to neighbors who need a safe place to store their things. Traditional self-storage units are expensive and inconvenient, making residential garage space an attractive alternative for people storing extra boxes, furniture, or a classic car.

This is fundamentally different from opening a commercial warehouse because the liability and zoning rules for peer-to-peer storage are usually managed through specialized platforms. 

*   **Startup Gear:** Heavy-duty shelving units to keep stored items off the concrete floor, and a padlock-secured partition if you want to keep your personal items separate from your renter's space.
*   **How to Launch:** List your available square footage—whether it's an empty corner or an entire single-car bay—on peer-to-peer storage marketplaces like Neighbor or StashPeer. Take clear, well-lit photos of the clean space.
*   **The Math:** Depending on your local cost of living, renting a 10x10 section of a clean, dry garage can bring in $100 to $200 per month in passive income with zero ongoing labor hours required.

## 3. Micro-Scale Furniture Flipping

Thrift stores, yard sales, and online free marketplaces are flooded with solid wood dressers, nightstands, and dining tables that look dated because of a bad coat of paint or outdated hardware. A garage gives you the dedicated square footage required to sand, paint, and stage furniture without ruining your home's interior air quality or living space.

The secret to making this profitable is ignoring complex woodworking repairs and focusing strictly on surface transformations. Look for furniture made from actual hardwood (like oak, maple, or mahogany) rather than cheap particle board, which falls apart easily.

*   **Startup Gear:** An orbital sander, a collection of medium- and fine-grit sandpaper, a drop cloth, a good quality spray gun or angled brushes, and a few cans of primer and cabinet paint.
*   **How to Launch:** Pick up one free or very cheap wooden dresser on Marketplace. Sand it down, paint it a modern neutral color, swap out the knobs for sleek modern hardware, and list it for $150. Use your profits to fund the next two pieces.
*   **The Math:** If you spend $20 on materials and invest four hours of labor into a piece you bought for $15, selling it for $175 leaves you with a solid $140 profit for a few evenings of work.

## 4. Curbside Scrap Metal Processing

Every weekend, neighborhoods throw away old gas grills, metal bed frames, broken exercise equipment, copper piping, and discarded appliances. If you have a truck or a utility trailer, you can collect these items and break them down in your garage to separate the metals before hauling them to a local scrap yard.

Yards pay significantly more for "clean" sorted metals—like separating clean copper wire from insulated wire, or keeping aluminum separate from steel. 

*   **Startup Gear:** Heavy work gloves, safety glasses, a pair of tin snips, bolt cutters, and a standard magnet (to test whether metal is steel/iron or valuable non-ferrous metals like aluminum and brass).
*   **How to Launch:** Watch the free sections of online classifieds daily for people giving away broken appliances or metal debris if you haul it away yourself. Use a corner of your garage to break down large items using basic hand tools so they take up less space in your vehicle.
*   **The Math:** A single load of sorted scrap copper and aluminum collected over two weeks of casual weekend pickups can easily turn into a few hundred dollars of cash at the recycling center for materials that other people were paying to throw away.

Turning your garage into a workspace requires a bit of upfront cleaning and organization, but it removes the biggest financial hurdle new business owners face. Take a close look at your available square footage and your own weekend habits to decide which of these paths fits your schedule best for your next steps.
