When I brought my second puppy home while working a full-time remote job, I made the classic mistake of thinking, "I'm home all day, so house training will be a breeze." By day three, my laptop was covered in drool, my spreadsheet was a mess, and puddle patrol had interrupted four different client calls. 

Working from home with a new puppy is entirely possible, but only if you stop trying to work a traditional eight-hour day while simultaneously watching a tiny predator. You need a structural schedule that divides your day into predictable blocks of hyper-focus, potty management, and mandatory nap times. Here is the exact daily routine I use to survive puppyhood without losing my job.

## The 6:30 AM to 9:00 AM Wake-Up and First Shift

Puppies do not care about your morning stand-up meeting. The secret to a peaceful morning is getting your dog's biological needs met before you even look at Slack. 

At 6:30 AM, take your puppy straight outside to their designated potty spot. Use a specific phrase like "go potty" every single time, and throw a high-value treat (like a tiny piece of boiled chicken) the second they finish. Bring them back inside for breakfast, but do not give them free roam of the house. Put them in a playpen or a small, puppy-proofed room with you while you drink your first coffee.

Around 8:00 AM, when you need to check your morning emails, give your puppy a puzzle toy stuffed with frozen plain Greek yogurt or kibble soaked in bone broth. This buys you twenty to thirty minutes of uninterrupted focus while they work out their brain.

## The 9:00 AM to 12:00 PM Crate Nap and Deep Work Block

If your puppy is awake for three hours straight, they will become overtired, land-sharking monsters. Puppies actually need roughly 18 to 20 hours of sleep a day. 

At 9:00 AM, take your puppy out for a quick, strictly business potty trip. No long walks—just outside, business, treat, and right back inside. Then, put them in their crate for their morning nap. Cover the crate with a light blanket to block visual distractions. 

This is your prime productivity window. Schedule your deep-work tasks, coding sprints, or complex writing assignments here. Do not feel guilty about the crate; enforced naps are the single greatest tool for house training because a sleeping puppy does not have accidents on your rug.

## The 12:00 PM Lunch Break Reset

Use your lunch break to reset both yourself and your puppy. At noon, take them out for a longer walk or twenty minutes of active play in the backyard. This is also when you should eat your lunch—separately. Feed your puppy their midday meal, ideally through a slow feeder bowl to prevent gulping.

Between 1:00 PM and 3:00 PM, put the puppy back in their crate or a secure playpen for another scheduled nap. Use this afternoon block for meetings and collaborative calls where you have to talk, since your puppy will be sleeping off their midday energy.

## The 3:00 PM to 5:00 PM Shadow Shift

Late afternoon is usually when puppies get a sudden burst of energy, often called the "witching hour." By 3:00 PM, take your puppy out for another bathroom break, and then bring them into your office space for the "shadow shift."

Since my puppy can't be trusted to roam free yet, I tether a lightweight house leash to my belt loop or chair leg, with the puppy lying on a dog bed next to my desk. They get to be near me, but they cannot sneak off to chew baseboards or leave a surprise behind the couch. 

During this block, do low-stakes administrative work, clear out your inbox, or listen in on webinars where you don't need to be on video. If they stir or start sniffing the floor intensely, interrupt them immediately and carry them outside.

## Evening Wind-Down and Consistency

Once 5:00 PM hits, log off. The temptation to check emails one last time is high, but your puppy needs your undivided attention before bedtime routines settle in. Feed them dinner around 6:00 PM, restrict water intake about two hours before bedtime to help them sleep through the night without emergency bathroom trips, and keep indoor play relatively calm.

To make this schedule actually work for you, keep a few logistical rules in mind:

*   **Set phone alarms:** Do not rely on your memory to take the puppy out. Set repeating alarms on your phone for every 60 to 90 minutes during waking hours.
*   **Keep high-value treats at every workstation:** Keep a stash of small training treats right next to your keyboard so you can reward outdoor bathroom success instantly without leaving your desk setup a mess.
*   **Embrace the baby gate barrier:** Keep your office door shut or use tall baby gates to block off hallways. Containment is safety.
*   **Accept lost productivity:** Expect to be about 15 to 20 percent less efficient for the first month. Communicate with your manager or team early about your adjusted availability so you aren't stressing over delayed replies.

Balancing a remote job and a new puppy isn't about doing everything at once; it's about trading off your attention so both your career and your dog get what they need.
